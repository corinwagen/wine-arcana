# Linking and naming

Filenames and ordinary Markdown links form Wine Arcana's durable identity and
knowledge graph. Keep both simple enough to understand without special tools.

## Article types and paths

The parent directory determines the article type:

```text
content/grapes/nebbiolo.md
content/regions/barolo.md
content/styles/vin-jaune.md
content/concepts/whole-cluster-fermentation.md
```

Use lowercase, kebab-case filenames. Prefer stable, recognizable English
identifiers with ASCII characters in the path; retain correct accents and
diacritics in the displayed title and prose.

Do not encode a taxonomy or appellation hierarchy in nested directories. A
region page may represent a broad region or a specific appellation.

Renaming a file changes its stable identifier and future URL. Treat a rename
as a deliberate migration, not copyediting.

## Canonical subjects

One biological grape variety normally has one canonical article. Regional and
historical names belong in that article as aliases and in explanatory prose.

For example, Grenache, Garnacha, Cannonau, and Garnatxa normally resolve to one
canonical grape page rather than four parallel articles.

Choose a canonical title by considering established English-language usage,
international recognizability, and stability. Do not automatically treat the
most commercially prominent synonym as biologically or historically primary.

When identity or synonymy is disputed, explain the evidence in prose. Do not
force uncertainty into separate pages or settle it through metadata alone.

## Aliases

Aliases exist primarily for discovery. Add genuine alternative names that a
reader is likely to search for:

```yaml
---
title: Grenache
aliases:
  - Garnacha
  - Cannonau
  - Garnatxa
---
```

Do not use aliases for:

- translations that are not actually used as names;
- misspellings;
- descriptive phrases or SEO keywords;
- appellations that use the grape;
- uncertain synonyms presented as settled fact.

Before adding a title or alias, check it case-insensitively against existing
canonical titles and aliases. A collision should prompt editorial review, not
an automatic disambiguation suffix.

## Internal links

Use ordinary relative Markdown links ending in `.md`:

```md
[Nebbiolo](nebbiolo.md)
[Barolo](../regions/barolo.md)
[whole-cluster fermentation](../concepts/whole-cluster-fermentation.md)
```

Use locally appropriate display text while pointing to the canonical file:

```md
[Garnacha](grenache.md)
```

Links should help explain the current subject. Link a meaningful first mention
or the passage where the relationship matters; do not link every repeated
mention. Prefer links in prose to a large, indiscriminate `Related topics`
list.

Link only to an existing article. If the ideal destination does not exist,
write readable unlinked prose and report the missing topic in the work handoff.
Do not create placeholder pages or leave intentionally broken links.

For now, link to whole articles rather than heading fragments. Heading text is
editorial and may change more often than canonical filenames.

## External links

External sources normally belong under `## Sources` or in a footnote, not as
inline substitutes for internal encyclopedia links. Use direct, stable URLs
when available and avoid search-result, tracking, and session URLs.

## Derived relationships

Backlinks, search records, alias indexes, and graph views will be generated by
scanning frontmatter and Markdown links. Do not maintain reciprocal link lists
or graph metadata by hand merely to support a future interface.
