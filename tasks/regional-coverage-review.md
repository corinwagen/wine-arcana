# Contemporary regional coverage review

Quick scan: 17 September 2026. Scope: the 114 grape pages, using headings,
regional passages, and targeted closer reading. This is editorial triage,
not a full factual audit of every article. Regions named below are research
leads unless a consulted source is given. Confirm significance and current
practice before drafting; do not expand pages merely to add countries.

## First revision batch — completed 17 September 2026

The eight pages below now contain researched regional additions, with sources
recorded in each article. Coverage includes Cape and Itata Cinsault, Maule
Carignan and VIGNO, Swartland and Stellenbosch Chenin, Finger Lakes and Niagara
Riesling, Washington and coastal Chilean Syrah, coastal Chile and Constantia
Sauvignon Blanc, Virginia and Eden Valley Viognier, and Franschhoek Sémillon.
The original triage notes follow for context.

1. **Cinsault:** the regional discussion concentrates on southern France;
   South Africa appears chiefly as history and parentage. Research the Cape's
   contemporary varietal wines and Itata in Chile. Wines of Chile identifies
   Itata as Cinsault's Chilean home; recent reporting also documents Cinsault
   within South Africa's old-vine movement.
2. **Carignan:** the reappraisal section remains Mediterranean. Add a researched
   account of Maule, where Wines of Chile documents Carignan and old dry-farmed
   vineyards. Investigate the producers and collective initiatives behind its
   modern regional identity.
3. **Chenin Blanc:** South Africa gets one broad paragraph and an acreage
   reference based on roughly 2015 data. Explain selected Cape regions and the
   old-vine movement; existing 2026 reporting provides a starting point. Replace
   the vague closing sentence about other countries only if a specific regional
   comparison earns its place.
4. **Riesling:** North America is compressed into a generic sentence. Start
   with the already-existing Finger Lakes page and research which Canadian and
   US regional contrasts best explain contemporary styles.
5. **Syrah:** France and Australia receive useful detail; South Africa, the
   United States, and Chile end up in a country list. Research a small selection
   of named regions and explain their distinct styles and producer choices.
6. **Sauvignon Blanc:** the broad “Warmer regions” section is a weak substitute
   for regional coverage beyond France and Marlborough. Research specific
   coastal, elevated, and inland comparisons, including South Africa and Chile.
7. **Viognier:** international spread appears mainly in a historical sentence.
   Virginia's wine body foregrounds the variety; explain that contemporary role
   and select another regional comparison if it adds something distinct.
8. **Sémillon:** Bordeaux and Hunter Valley are developed, but contemporary
   Cape wines are absent. Research South African old-vine expressions, using
   current reporting as a lead, and assess whether other regions warrant space.

## Useful existing models

Assyrtiko compares Santorini, mainland Greece, and an Australian planting.
Brancellao explains conservation and revival; Aligoté connects renewed interest
with hillside sites. Counoise describes a limited California revival without
inflating its scale. Verdelho develops specific Australian comparisons.
Muscadelle and Durif explain contrasting regional uses. These demonstrate that
contemporary relevance can involve an old region or a small but instructive
planting, not just expansion into new countries.

Cabernet Franc, Chardonnay, and Meunier already have focused updates in the
working tree from the preceding task. They are not a completed corpus-wide pass.
The initial scan changed no article prose; the subsequent first revision batch
is recorded above.

Useful future region pages include Swartland, Stellenbosch, Franschhoek,
Maule, Itata, Niagara Peninsula, and Virginia. These additions currently remain
unlinked where no canonical region page exists.
