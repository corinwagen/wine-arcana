# Teinturier

A teinturier is a grape variety with red pigment in its flesh as well as
its skin. The French word means “dyer.” Most dark-skinned wine grapes have
pale flesh and obtain their wine colour chiefly through skin contact;
teinturier grapes can release coloured juice as soon as their flesh is
broken. [Alicante Bouschet](../grapes/alicante-bouschet.md) and
[Saperavi](../grapes/saperavi.md) are familiar examples.

The term describes a property of the berry, not a single cultivar or a
wine style. It helps explain where colour comes from, while leaving open
questions about the wine's flavour, weight, and texture.

## Pigment in two tissues

The pigments are anthocyanins, compounds responsible for much of the red
and purple colour in grapes. In teinturier varieties they accumulate in
the pulp as well as the skin. The two tissues can contain different
mixtures of these compounds. A comparative study of fifteen teinturier
varieties found distinct pigment profiles in skin and juice, with the
skin generally containing a more complex mixture.[^1]

This difference has a practical consequence. Separating juice promptly
from the skins limits one source of pigment, but leaves the colour
already supplied by the flesh. For pale [rosé](../styles/rose.md), grape
choice therefore matters alongside pressing technique and the length of
skin contact.

## Colour and cellar decisions

Teinturier grapes give a winemaker an additional source of colour, but
[maceration](maceration.md) still extracts compounds from skins and seeds.
Time, temperature, alcohol, and the handling of the floating cap affect
how much reaches the wine. Pigment and [tannin](tannin.md) follow different
courses during fermentation: colour extracts early, while increasing
alcohol makes seed tannins more readily extractable.

A strongly coloured juice consequently does not establish how much
tannic grip the finished wine will have. Winemakers can adjust extraction
to the texture they want, rather than using colour alone as the endpoint.
Nor is the colour at pressing permanent. Pigments react with other
compounds or attach to solids as fermentation proceeds, changing what
remains in the liquid.

## Blends and varietal wines

Alicante Bouschet illustrates the historical interest in breeding grapes
for colour. Plantgrape records its creation by Louis and Henri Bouschet
in 1855 through a cross of [Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md) and Petit Bouschet, and identifies
colour contribution as a principal use.[^2] A blending partner can bring
pigment to a pale wine, but it also contributes its own flavour, acidity,
and texture. The appropriate proportion depends on the whole blend.

Saperavi places the same berry characteristic in a different regional
tradition. It is Georgia's leading red grape, especially associated with
Kakheti, and makes wines with marked [acidity](acidity.md) as well as deep colour.
Its identity encompasses much more than a colouring role. Both grapes
are useful examples of why *teinturier* should be read as information
about the fruit, rather than a prediction of one uniform wine character.
