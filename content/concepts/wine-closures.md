# Wine closures

A wine closure seals the bottle and helps determine how the wine develops
after packaging. Natural and technical corks, synthetic and glass stoppers,
and screwcaps differ in construction and oxygen transfer. Their performance
also depends on the bottle, correct application, and the wine's condition
at bottling.

## Oxygen already present and oxygen entering later

Wine carries some dissolved oxygen into the bottle; more may remain in
the headspace or closure. This initial *total package oxygen* can consume
protective [sulfur dioxide](sulfur-dioxide.md). Subsequent oxygen entering
through the closure adds a separate influence during storage.

The *oxygen transmission rate*, or OTR, describes that later entry.
Closure types have different ranges, and individual closures of one
type can also vary. Peter Godden's summary of Australian Wine Research
Institute (AWRI) trials identifies both the transfer rate and consistency
between bottles as important
performance measures.[^trials]

## How screwcaps seal

A screwcap's metal casing holds a liner against the bottle rim. The liner
and the quality of that contact form the seal. The external appearance
therefore does not reveal all of its oxygen-transfer properties.

AWRI distinguishes commonly used Saran Tin and Saranex liners. The tin
layer provides a stronger oxygen barrier, while other constructions
allow greater transfer. Producers can choose a closure to suit a wine's
composition and intended development. Application checks also matter:
an incorrectly fitted cap cannot be assessed as though it formed the
intended seal.

## Taint, oxidation, and sulfur aromas

Cork taint is commonly associated with 2,4,6-trichloroanisole, or TCA.
It can give a musty smell and suppress fruit aroma. The compound can
also arise from sources such as contaminated oak, so a wine can show
TCA character without a cork-derived contamination.

[Oxidation](oxidation.md) is a different process. Oxygen exposure and the
wine's reactive constituents determine the resulting loss of fruit,
colour changes, and development. Some sulfur aromas associated with
[reduction](reduction.md) are more likely to become apparent under
very low oxygen entry when the wine already has a propensity to develop
them. Closure and cellar preparation therefore work together.

## Bottle development

Low oxygen transfer can preserve fruit and still allow
[bottle ageing](bottle-aging.md). Reactions among compounds already in
the wine continue after sealing. AWRI's trials and later comparative
tastings show how different closures alter preservation and bottle
consistency, with results tied to the wines and closures tested.

For the drinker, the closure supplies information about packaging and
the producer's choices. Assessing ageability also requires the wine's
composition, storage history, and the desired stage of maturity.
