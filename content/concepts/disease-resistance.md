# Disease resistance

Disease resistance describes a grapevine's ability to limit infection or the
development of a particular pathogen. It is a major goal of modern grape
breeding because protecting susceptible vines requires repeated work through
the growing season. Resistance is specific: a vine that withstands one disease
may remain vulnerable to another.

[Pierce's disease](pierces-disease.md), caused by a bacterium, provides a
different resistance target from the mildews commonly associated with PIWI.
UC Davis's 2019 release of five resistant wine grapes illustrates breeding
directed specifically at that disease.

Resistance and tolerance describe different advantages. Resistance limits the
pathogen's infection or development; tolerance allows a vine to suffer less
damage despite infection. [Blanc du Bois](../grapes/blanc-du-bois.md), for
example, can harbour the Pierce's disease bacterium while avoiding the decline
seen in susceptible varieties. A tolerant vine can consequently remain part
of the disease's host population.[^tolerance]

## What PIWI means

*PIWI* abbreviates the German *pilzwiderstandsfähig*, meaning resistant to
fungal diseases. In winegrowing it commonly refers to varieties bred for strong
resistance to [powdery mildew](powdery-mildew.md) and
[downy mildew](downy-mildew.md). The latter is caused by an oomycete, a
fungus-like organism, but is conventionally included in the category.

[Regent](../grapes/regent.md), [Solaris](../grapes/solaris.md), and
[Souvignier Gris](../grapes/souvignier-gris.md) are examples. Reduced need for
protective treatments can also mean fewer tractor passes, less fuel use, and
less soil compaction. The extent of those benefits depends on the vineyard
and season.

## Breeding and continuing protection

Breeders cross vines, then select offspring for agronomic performance and wine
quality as well as resistance. Agroscope's Swiss programme combines resistance
inherited through interspecific breeding with tests of the resulting grapes
and wines. Its Divona account describes selection from thousands of candidates,
including early biochemical tests of their defensive responses.

The same account makes the limits concrete: even for this strongly resistant
variety, Agroscope recommends some protective treatment around flowering and
fruit set according to disease pressure. Resistance reduces a management
burden while monitoring remains necessary.

Other characteristics must also fit the site. Divona ripens early, and
Agroscope cautions against the hottest situations and severely restricted
water supply. This illustrates why a resistant grape is not automatically
suited to every warming region. [Winter hardiness](winter-hardiness.md),
ripening time, water relations, and resistance to individual diseases are
separate considerations in choosing planting material.

## Making resistance last

Breeders can combine several resistance genes in one variety, an approach
called *pyramiding*. INRAE's ResDur programme uses such combinations to make
it harder for mildew populations to overcome protection. DNA markers help
identify promising seedlings, followed by vineyard and wine evaluation.

Durability depends on the combination and the pathogen population. INRAE's
2026 summary of experimental work reports that an already defeated resistance
locus can, in some combinations, compromise another locus's protection. The
number of resistance genes alone is therefore an incomplete guide to lasting
performance.[^durability] France's OSCAR vineyard network monitors resistant
varieties after planting, including changes in pathogen populations and the
appearance of other disease problems.
