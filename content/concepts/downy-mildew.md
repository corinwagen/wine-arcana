# Downy mildew

Downy mildew is a grapevine disease caused by *Plasmopara viticola*, an
oomycete: a fungus-like organism with a different biological ancestry from
true fungi. It can damage leaves and bunches, reduce the usable crop, and
weaken a vine by removing the healthy leaf area needed for photosynthesis.
Warm, wet conditions make it a recurring problem in many wine regions.

## Water drives the infection cycle

Resting spores carry the pathogen between seasons. When conditions permit,
initial infections establish lesions on green tissue. Further spore production
and infection can then amplify an outbreak. Leaf symptoms include yellowish
“oilspots,” with downy growth developing on the underside under favourable
conditions.

Wetness is central to this cycle. That distinguishes downy mildew from
[powdery mildew](powdery-mildew.md), whose secondary spread can occur without
free water. Monitoring rainfall, temperature, and leaf wetness helps growers
judge when infection is plausible, while inspecting vines reveals whether
the disease has become established.

## The current crop and the next season

A damaged bunch directly reduces harvestable fruit. Loss of leaves has wider
consequences: less photosynthesis can compromise ripening and the vine's
preparation for winter. A University of Georgia account emphasizes this
connection between severe defoliation and subsequent cold injury.

The same researchers documented widespread resistance to one fungicide group
in Georgia samples collected in 2017.[^1] This is resistance of the *pathogen*
to a treatment, distinct from [disease resistance](disease-resistance.md) bred
into a grapevine. Repeatedly using a treatment with the same biological target
can select pathogen populations that survive it.

Varietal choice, [canopy management](canopy-management.md), observation, and
appropriate protection therefore work together. A general description such as
“hybrid” does not by itself establish how well a vine withstands downy mildew.

[^1]: Sarah Campbell and colleagues, University of Georgia, 2018 report on
    fungicide sensitivity in 2017 vineyard samples and associated field trials.
