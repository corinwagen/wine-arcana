# Massal and clonal selection

Massal and clonal selection are ways of choosing grapevines for vegetative
propagation. Massal selection takes material from a group of selected vines;
clonal selection follows the descendants of an individual source vine.
Both preserve a variety through cuttings or grafting, while giving growers
different ways to retain and evaluate variation within it.

## A variety can contain different plant material

Grapevines grown from seed acquire a new combination of their parents' genes.
Cuttings instead preserve the source vine's identity. Over repeated growth
and propagation, mutations can create differences among plants of the same
variety. [Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) provides a familiar example of
such variation, including differences in bunch form and productivity.

A clone can be selected for useful traits such as crop, ripening, or bunch
structure. Performance still depends on rootstock, site, and season. Choosing
a named clone is one planting decision alongside
[grafting](grafting.md), spacing, and training.

## Selection and evaluation

Massal selection can preserve several lines from an established vineyard.
Growers identify suitable source vines and propagate their material together.
That makes [old vineyards](old-vines.md) potential resources, particularly
where they predate narrower planting programmes. Visible performance needs
careful interpretation: a vine may crop differently because of its soil,
health, or management rather than an inherited trait.

Clonal evaluation compares material under conditions intended to separate
genetic differences from environmental ones. The OIV's procedure includes
identity, health assessment, agronomic observations, and wine evaluation.
Repeated observation is more informative than choosing a vine from a single
season's fruit.

*Polyclonal selection* is a further approach. It assembles a group of evaluated
lines to retain diversity within a variety. The OIV's protocol describes broad
sampling followed by replicated field trials, helping distinguish genetic
effects from differences in growing position. It is more formal than simply
taking cuttings from several attractive vines.

## Health and naming

Propagation can also transfer viruses. [Leafroll](grapevine-leafroll.md)
illustrates why a productive-looking source vine needs health assessment.
Selecting for a desirable crop trait and testing for pathogens address
separate questions.

Foundation Plant Services at UC Davis makes another useful distinction. Its
numbered *selections* record source material and testing, while FPS itself
does not conduct a formal clonal performance evaluation for every accession.
A selection number therefore identifies material; it does not rank wine
quality or guarantee a particular vineyard result.

Identity testing has developed over time. The Australian Wine Research
Institute now offers clonal identification using high-resolution DNA analysis
and a verified reference database. Such a test can help establish what was
planted, while field observation establishes how it performs in that vineyard.

For the drinker, a clonal or massal description gives information about
planting material and the grower's priorities. Wine character follows the
interaction of that material with farming, ripeness, and cellar choices.
