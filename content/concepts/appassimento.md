# Appassimento

Appassimento is the partial drying of grapes used to make wine. In many
Italian traditions, growers harvest sound bunches and place them in ventilated
rooms before pressing or fermenting them. Water loss concentrates the must,
while the living berries continue to undergo chemical changes. The eventual
wine depends on both this drying phase and the fermentation that follows.

![Hands arranging green grape bunches on a mesh tray.](../../media/images/grapes/nosiola.jpg "Nosiola selected for Vino Santo, one regional use of drying after harvest.")

## Concentration and time

Temperature, humidity, airflow, and the structure of the berry influence how
quickly water leaves the fruit. The rate matters beyond achieving a target
weight loss. In a controlled study of [Corvina](../grapes/corvina.md), forced
airflow brought grapes to the same degree of dehydration much sooner than
natural drying, but the resulting patterns of gene expression and metabolite
accumulation differed.[^1]

The experiment helps explain why two lots with similar sugar concentration
need not have identical flavour potential. Drying preserves some compounds,
concentrates others, and allows metabolic changes to proceed over time.
[Acidity](acidity.md), for example, reflects both water loss and the berry's
continued use of malic acid in respiration.

## Regional interpretations

[Valpolicella](../regions/valpolicella.md) uses dried grapes for both the
relatively dry [Amarone](../styles/amarone-della-valpolicella.md) and sweet
Recioto. Drying raises potential alcohol; the amount of sugar left after
[fermentation](alcoholic-fermentation.md) determines much of the difference
in sweetness.

White grapes offer other expressions. For Torcolato in Breganze, Maculan
describes four months of drying selected
[Vespaiola](../grapes/vespaiola.md) in a *fruttaio*, followed by barrel
maturation of the wine. Cantina Toblino's Valle dei Laghi Vino Santo uses
[Nosiola](../grapes/nosiola.md) dried on racks until Holy Week before pressing
and a long cellar maturation.

These methods share a concentration step but differ in grape, drying duration,
and subsequent handling. Appassimento therefore describes a process that can
support several distinct regional wine traditions.

[^1]: Sara Zenoni and colleagues, 2020. Their Corvina experiment compared
    natural and forced-air drying at the same temperature; reaching 30%
    weight loss took approximately 120 and 60 days, respectively.
