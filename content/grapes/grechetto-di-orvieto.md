# Grechetto di Orvieto

Grechetto di Orvieto is a white grape of central Italy, particularly associated
with [Umbria](../regions/umbria.md) and the white wines of
[Orvieto](../regions/orvieto.md). It appears both in blends and in varietal
wines, where producers can bring its texture into clearer focus. A wine called
Orvieto and a wine called Grechetto therefore describe different things:
the first identifies an appellation, the second a grape name that needs some
care in interpretation.

## Untangling the name

The Italian Vitis Database records *Grechetto* as the registered variety and
lists Grechetto di Orvieto and Grechetto Bianco among its documented names.
This article uses the more specific regional name to distinguish it from
[Grechetto di Todi](pignoletto.md), or Grechetto Gentile, identified with
Pignoletto in the
Rauscedo varietal reference.[^identity]

The distinction matters in vineyards, blends, and nursery descriptions.
An unqualified mention of Grechetto does not always resolve which local
usage a writer means. Nor does a similar name establish identity with the
Campanian grape [Greco](greco.md). Names invoking Greece recur in Italian
viticulture and cannot substitute for varietal identification.

## Vineyard behaviour

Rauscedo describes the Orvieto type as having compact bunches, mid-season
ripening, and susceptibility to bunch rot. Compactness makes the health
of the fruit particularly relevant near harvest: tightly packed berries can
be difficult to keep dry and sound when conditions favour fungal growth.
The nursery recommends medium to long pruning and notes its suitability for
hillside cultivation, including relatively heavy, calcareous soils.

Those recommendations should be read alongside site conditions. Soil texture,
canopy density, and the weather during ripening all influence the condition of
the fruit that reaches the press.

## Blends and varietal whites

In Orvieto, Grechetto shares a blend with Trebbiano Toscano, locally called
Procanico, and other permitted white grapes. This gives it a role within an
appellation whose styles range from dry to sweet. Its proportion and the
handling of the blend help determine how much of its character the wine shows.

A varietal bottling makes another useful comparison. Palazzone's Grechetto,
labelled Umbria IGT, comes from clay-rich hillside soils and undergoes soft
pressing and temperature-controlled fermentation in steel. The producer
describes a little tannic grip, illustrating how a white wine can have texture
without prolonged fermentation on its skins. Pressing and handling determine
how much phenolic material passes into the juice.

Grechetto also contributes to [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md)-led wines around
Orvieto. In
Antinori's 2024 Cervaro della Sala, the Grechetto component was fermented
separately and received no oak, while Chardonnay underwent barrel fermentation
and partial malolactic fermentation. This is a useful example of blending
components whose treatment differs as well as their grape variety.
