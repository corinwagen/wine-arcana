# Traminette

Traminette is an aromatic white grape released by Cornell in 1996 and
subsequently adopted as a signature variety in Indiana. Its floral and
spicy character recalls [Gewürztraminer](gewurztraminer.md), one of its
parents, while its breeding offers growers a different combination of
cold tolerance and disease susceptibility.

## Aroma begins in the vineyard

Purdue's wine team identifies the other parent as Joannes Seyve 23-416.[^1]
Its winemaking guide emphasizes sunlight reaching the fruit, which helps
develop the monoterpenes responsible for much of Traminette's perfume.
A shaded crop can therefore differ from a well-exposed one even before
fermentation begins.

The practical challenge is to allow enough maturation while maintaining
sound fruit through the changing weather around harvest. The grower's
[canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) and picking decisions
help determine how much aroma the cellar has to work with.

## An Indiana style

Purdue's guide advocates gentle processing and restrained extraction,
warning that excessive skin contact can bring harshness. It describes
cool fermentation, possible [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md), and
balancing the wine's [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) with its remaining sugar. These are regional
stylistic recommendations, not rules defining every Traminette.

The contrast with Gewürztraminer is useful when the wines are made in
comparable styles. Both can be strongly perfumed, but grape identity,
site, crop exposure, and [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md)
all contribute to what reaches the glass.

[^1]: Christian Butzke, Bruce Bordelon, and Jeanette Merritt,
    *Traminette Winemaking*, Purdue Extension, 2010.
