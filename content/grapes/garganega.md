# Garganega

Garganega is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety associated with [Soave](../regions/soave.md) and
Gambellara in northeastern Italy. Its long ripening season, productive vines,
and suitability for drying help explain a range extending from light dry
whites to concentrated sweet wines. Sicily grows the same variety under the
name Grecanico Dorato.[^1]

Dry Garganega often combines restrained fruit and floral aromas with an
almond-like finish. The balance between freshness and breadth depends on the
site, the crop carried by the vine, the harvest date, and the time spent in
the cellar.

## Identity and distribution

The Italian Vitis Database confirms Garganega's identity with Grecanico Dorato
through molecular analysis. It also records parent–offspring relationships
with several Italian grapes, including [Trebbiano Toscano](ugni-blanc.md) and
[Catarratto](catarratto.md).
Those relationships establish its place in a large family of varieties; its
own parents and precise geographical origin remain uncertain.[^1]

Verona and Vicenza are its principal northern associations. Around Soave it
grows across hills and lower ground, while Gambellara continues the tradition
eastward into Vicenza. Smaller Veneto plantings extend to the Berici and
Euganean hills. The Sicilian name belongs to a separate regional wine culture,
with different climates and blending practices.

## A long season and a broad canopy

Garganega is vigorous and has a long growth cycle. In Soave, the traditional
pergola spreads its shoots overhead, allowing bunches to hang beneath a canopy
of leaves. This arrangement provides shade during hot weather and distributes
the vine's growth across a large surface. Pruning, crop adjustment, and
[canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) determine how effectively
that leaf area ripens the fruit.

Its relatively long, open bunches help air circulate around the berries.
Growers still need to manage fungal disease and autumn weather, particularly
when keeping fruit on the vine late or selecting bunches for drying. The
FAO agricultural-heritage nomination for Soave describes moderate susceptibility
to [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md) and grey rot, with lower susceptibility to [downy mildew](../concepts/downy-mildew.md).

## Dry wine and recioto

Garganega supplies the majority of Soave DOC. The specification available from
the consortium, consolidated through 2019, requires at least 70%, with
[Trebbiano di Soave](verdicchio.md) and [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md) among the permitted
partners.[^2] This leaves
room for both varietal wines and blends. Trebbiano di Soave can contribute
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), while the site's ripening conditions and the producer's harvest
choices affect how broad the Garganega component becomes.

Cellar choices develop that range further. A wine kept in tank can retain a
direct fruit profile; [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can add texture,
and wood introduces another influence according to vessel size and age.

For [Recioto di Soave](../styles/recioto-di-soave.md), growers select sound
bunches for *[appassimento](../concepts/appassimento.md)*, drying
after harvest. Water loss concentrates the juice before fermentation. Leaving
some grape sugar unfermented produces a sweet wine with greater concentration
and a different aromatic development from fresh-grape Soave.
