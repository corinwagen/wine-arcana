# Maréchal Foch

Maréchal Foch is an early-ripening red
[hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) bred in Alsace and subsequently
adopted in North American vineyards. Often shortened to Foch, it remains
part of Canadian winegrowing, from Nova Scotia blends to British Columbia
vineyards. Its usefulness begins with the chance to ripen red grapes in a
limited season; the resulting wine can take several forms.

## Origins and its relationship to Millot

Eugène Kuhlmann bred Foch at the Oberlin Institute. Foundation Plant Services
records its parents as an open-pollinated seedling of the rootstock
101-14 and Goldriesling. Older accounts contain competing pedigrees, so the
precise wording of the catalogue matters. It should not be simplified into
a direct Pinot Noir cross.[^identity]

[Léon Millot](leon-millot.md) comes from the same breeding family. Comparing
the two is more useful than treating their shared ancestry as a promise of
identical behaviour. In Nova Scotia guidance, Millot ripens earlier, while
Foch has somewhat greater winter hardiness.

## What early ripening provides

Foch's small berries and early maturity make it a candidate for places
where a later red might struggle to finish. Iowa State's cultivar review
also records continuing disease concerns, including [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md)
and [black rot](../concepts/black-rot.md). Early harvest and [winter hardiness](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md)
do not remove the need for vineyard protection.

That distinction matters when comparing it with newer cold-climate grapes
such as [Marquette](marquette.md). A planting decision requires evidence
about the particular site, winter exposure and intended wine, not simply
the word *hybrid*.

## Wine and regional uses

Foch can produce lighter, fruit-led reds as well as more substantial wines.
Iowa State's review discusses [carbonic maceration](../concepts/carbonic-maceration.md)
among the techniques used with it. Fermenting intact berries changes the
route to aroma and texture; a dark grape need not be made into a wine
centred on strong extraction.

In Nova Scotia, Jost includes Foch alongside Millot, Castel and Lucie
Kuhlmann in its 4 Skins red blend. This is a useful reminder that a grape's
regional contribution can persist through blended wines as well as
varietal labels. In British Columbia, Quails' Gate lists Foch at its Upper
Boucherie Bench property and at its Old Vines vineyard in Osoyoos. Those
documented plantings also place it within the contemporary Okanagan wine
scene, rather than solely in an account of early Canadian breeding and
planting history.
