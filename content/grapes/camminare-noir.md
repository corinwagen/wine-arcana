# Camminare Noir

Camminare Noir is a red [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) released
by the University of California, Davis, in 2019. Its breeding addressed
[Pierce's disease](../concepts/pierces-disease.md), a bacterial disease that
can kill susceptible vines. It belongs to a generation of wine grapes
intended to combine resistance with fruit suitable for contemporary
winemaking, and commercial bottles now extend the story beyond trials.

## Resistance and ancestry

Andrew Walker's breeding programme used resistance from *Vitis arizonica*
and repeated crosses with *Vitis vinifera*. Foundation Plant Services
records Camminare Noir as approximately 94% vinifera, with [Petite Sirah](durif.md)
and [Cabernet Sauvignon](cabernet-sauvignon.md) contributing substantial parts of its ancestry.
That ancestry reflects a multigeneration pedigree; they do not mean
the variety is a simple Petite Sirah × Cabernet Sauvignon cross.[^breeding]

Its selection number was Walker 07355-075. It should also be distinguished
from Caminante Blanc, the similarly named white variety released in the
same group. Camminare Noir's identity is separate from the other red
releases, Paseante Noir and Errante Noir.

## What the trials establish

The Davis profile reports strong Pierce's disease resistance in repeated
greenhouse screening. It also describes early budbreak, flowering and
ripening, together with large berries. Napa River trial plantings supplied
fruit for commercial-scale winemaking tests.

These observations answer different questions. Screening tests resistance
to a particular pathogen; vineyard and cellar trials examine growing
behaviour and wine potential. Neither the hybrid label nor that resistance
result establishes immunity to mildew or tolerance of severe winters.
Early budbreak also needs to be considered against local
[spring-frost risk](../concepts/spring-frost.md).

## From California breeding to Texas wine

Red Rock Vineyards provides a documented commercial example in Texas.
Its 2023 Camminare Noir is identified as an estate wine from Bastrop,
with a producer description emphasizing dark fruit, sour cherry and
relatively gentle tannin. This is evidence of a specific wine, not a
settled flavour template for the variety across regions.[^texas]

The geographic distinction matters. California supplied the breeding
programme and early trials; Texas supplies one example of adoption in a
different setting. As more vineyards accumulate experience, site choice,
crop balance and [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) will help explain
variation between bottles.

[Blanc du Bois](blanc-du-bois.md) offers an instructive comparison: it
addresses the same bacterial constraint through a different breeding
history and makes white wine. Together they show why disease resistance
can expand a region's choices without determining a single wine style.

[^texas]: Red Rock Vineyards, “2023 Camminare Noir,” estate and location
