# Hárslevelű

Hárslevelű is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape associated with Hungary,
especially [Tokaj](../regions/tokaj.md). Its name means “linden leaf,”
referring to the shape of its foliage. The wines often have floral and
honeyed impressions, with a rounded texture that makes the grape useful
both on its own and alongside [Furmint](furmint.md) in dry and sweet blends.

The Vitis International Variety Catalogue records Furmint and Tsimlyanskii
Belyi as its marker-confirmed parents.[^1] Lipovina and Lindenblättriger
are established regional synonyms.

## Water, frost, and autumn fruit

Hárslevelű needs an adequate water supply and is sensitive to frost and
[powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md). Site selection therefore involves both summer conditions
and the risk of cold damage. A warm exposure can help the fruit ripen,
while soil depth and its capacity to retain water matter during dry spells.

Its bunches can be very large, with round berries arranged relatively
loosely. In Tokaj it is generally less prone to developing aszú berries
than Furmint, although suitable seasons allow it to contribute to sweet
wines. Dry-wine grapes are picked before the prolonged selection of
noble-rotted fruit needed for Aszú.

[Noble rot](../styles/botrytized-sweet-wine.md) involves both dehydration
and biological change. A Tokaj study following Hárslevelű and Furmint
found that berry texture and chemical composition changed through the
process, with differences between sampling dates and varieties. The
microorganisms recovered from the berries also varied.[^2] Autumn weather
and the fruit's condition consequently influence what growers can select.

## Aroma and styles

Linden blossom, honey, and ripe pear are familiar associations. Honeyed
aroma can appear in a dry Hárslevelű; sweetness depends on the sugar
remaining after fermentation. Young, lighter wines can emphasize floral
fruit, while riper examples have greater breadth.

Hárslevelű often feels softer than Furmint in Tokaj. In a blend it can add
perfume and round out the palate, with the result depending on each wine's
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), ripeness, and proportions. It also makes substantial varietal dry
wines, including vineyard selections that develop with bottle age.

Sweet examples gain another layer from concentrated fruit and botrytis.
[Residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) increases sweetness and
weight, while acidity helps sustain freshness. Their dried-fruit and
honeyed character reflects this combination of grape, harvest, and
fermentation.

## Hungarian settings

Tokaj is its principal reference point, but Hárslevelű also grows on
[Somló](../regions/somlo.md) and in the [Eger](../regions/eger.md) area, where the name Debrői Hárslevelű has a separate
regional tradition. Loess sites and vineyards on weathered volcanic
material provide different rooting and water conditions within its
Hungarian range.
