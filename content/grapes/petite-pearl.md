# Petite Pearl

Petite Pearl is a red grape bred by Tom Plocher in Minnesota. Its appeal
to northern growers combines later spring budbreak with deeply coloured
fruit and useful tannin. It belongs to the wider development of wines
adapted to continental winters, alongside [Marquette](marquette.md) and
[Frontenac](frontenac.md).

## Breeding and seasonal fit

Plocher records it as a seedling from a 1996 crossing of MN 1094 and
E.S. 4-7-26.[^1] His vineyard observations describe moderate, drooping
growth and an ability to retain dormancy during late-winter warm spells.
Later budbreak can reduce exposure to some spring freezes, though it
cannot guarantee that a crop will escape frost.

The breeder also records winter injury at colder sites in severe years.
A variety's suitability depends on the local sequence of warmth and cold
as well as the minimum temperature. Sufficient summer heat remains
necessary to ripen the crop.

## Tannin and disease pressure

The University of Minnesota's 2023 introduction to its fact sheet reports
widespread adoption by Minnesota growers and highlights higher tannin
than in several other cold-hardy reds. That makes
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) management a meaningful part of the cellar
work, alongside colour extraction and acid balance.

Plocher describes useful resistance to both mildews, but identifies [black
rot](../concepts/black-rot.md) as a concern in more humid regions. The
combination of winter and
disease traits must therefore be evaluated locally rather than treated
as a promise of unattended cultivation.

[^1]: Plocher Vines, “Petite Pearl Grape,” breeder's pedigree and field account.
