# Tazzelenghe

Tazzelenghe is a red grape of [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](../regions/friuli.md) whose wines can combine
assertive [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) with marked
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Its Friulian name is commonly explained as
“tongue cutter,” an allusion to the force of the young wine. The Colli
Orientali, including the hills around Buttrio and Cividale, remain its main
regional setting.

## Structure and the producer's response

Acidity and astringency produce different sensations: acidity supplies
sourness and freshness, while tannin contributes the drying, gripping feel.
Their combination helps explain the grape's reputation for severity when
young. How much structure reaches the glass also depends on ripeness and
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md).

Jacùss offers a concrete example of the patient approach to this grape.
The estate identifies a Tazzelenghe vineyard in Torreano planted in 1989 and
describes fermenting with the skins before extended [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) and a
further period in bottle. This is an example of sustained local cultivation
and a deliberate cellar response to the wine's structure.

Maturation can make the tannins feel less angular and bring the fruit and
wood into a more integrated expression. The interest of Tazzelenghe lies in
that evolution as well as its youthful force. It belongs alongside
[Pignolo](pignolo.md) as one of Friuli's distinctive structured reds, though
the two grapes should be assessed through individual wines and producers.
