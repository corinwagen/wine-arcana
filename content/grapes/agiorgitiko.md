# Agiorgitiko

Agiorgitiko is a dark-skinned Greek *Vitis vinifera* variety most closely
associated with Nemea in the northeastern Peloponnese. It can make supple,
fruit-led young reds, more firmly structured wines for maturation, [rosé](../styles/rose.md),
and sweet wine. Its usefulness lies in that range: vineyard conditions and
extraction can give the same grape quite different proportions of fruit,
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md).

Young examples often combine conspicuous red-fruit aromas with moderate
acidity and rounded tannins. More concentrated fruit and sustained skin
contact can produce a firmer wine. Compared with the frequently acid-driven,
tannic [Xinomavro](xinomavro.md) of northern Greece, Agiorgitiko often offers
a softer starting balance, although neither variety has one fixed texture.

## Nemea's changing elevations

Nemea includes a warm plain, surrounding hills, and higher valleys and
plateaus. The differences affect ripening as well as soil water. Deeper
alluvial soils on the plain can hold more moisture and support more vigorous
growth; shallower hillside soils offer different constraints. Growers must
match crop and canopy to the resources of the site.

The highest vineyards are cooler and ripen later, sometimes into October.
That longer season can help preserve freshness, but completing ripening
becomes more dependent on autumn weather. A hillside position alone does
not determine the wine: exposure, soil depth, harvest decisions, and the
season act together. Wines of Greece's regional account describes this
contrast between the plain, foothills, and mountain vineyards.[^site]

Nemea remains the main reference, while plantings elsewhere in the
Peloponnese, Attica, and Macedonia broaden the grape's contemporary use.
These wines should be understood through their own sites and production
choices rather than assumed to reproduce Nemea.

## Extraction and maturation

For a young red, measured [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) can retain
fruit while avoiding excessive drying grip. A wine intended for longer
maturation needs sufficient fruit and structure to support its treatment.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) adds oxygen exposure and,
depending on the barrel, wood-derived aroma and tannin.

Semeli's Nemea Reserve illustrates the barrel-matured approach: the estate
uses hillside Agiorgitiko and describes a year in French oak. This is a
producer's recipe, not a universal requirement for Agiorgitiko. Rosé uses
much less extraction, while sweet versions retain sugar and may begin with
concentrated, sun-dried grapes.
