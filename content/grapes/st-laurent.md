# St. Laurent

St. Laurent is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape associated with Austria
and the Czech Republic. It produces deeply coloured reds with sour-cherry
fruit and a firm yet potentially supple texture. Austrian vineyards are
concentrated in the [Thermenregion](../regions/thermenregion.md) and around Lake Neusiedl; in Czech wine,
the variety is known as Svatovavřinecké.

The Vitis International Variety Catalogue identifies
[Pinot Noir](pinot-noir.md) as one parent, with the other still unknown.[^1]
Its geographical origin is less secure. The catalogue lists France, while
Klosterneuburg's viticultural institute considers Lower Austria probable.
St. Laurent subsequently became a parent of [Zweigelt](zweigelt.md), crossed
with [Blaufränkisch](blaufrankisch.md) at Klosterneuburg.

## Fruit set and autumn rain

St. Laurent's vineyard difficulties begin at flowering. Unfavourable
conditions can disrupt fruit set, making yields irregular. Its sensitivity
to late frost adds another source of crop loss, and shoots damaged by a
spring freeze regenerate poorly.

The vine benefits from deep soils with an adequate water supply.
Klosterneuburg identifies deep rooting space or irrigation as important
to its cultivation. On shallow, dry sites, limited stored water can leave
the vine short during summer growth and ripening.

Its compact bunches carry oval, blue-black berries with uncoloured flesh.
The berries have a marked tendency to split. Rain near harvest can open
the skins and encourage sour rot, so fruit condition can become decisive
while growers wait for ripeness. Grey rot and [downy mildew](../concepts/downy-mildew.md) also require
attention.

## Colour, fruit, and maturation

Sour cherry is a useful point of recognition, with plum-like aromas common
in fuller examples. The Czech National Wine Centre describes wines ranging
from moderately full to full-bodied, with pronounced young character that
can become more velvety in bottle. The wines are often deeply red,
with [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) contributing to their firm youthful
structure.

Colour develops through contact between juice and skins during
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md). Winemakers vary the duration and
intensity of extraction, and fruit ripeness influences how the resulting
tannin feels. Bottle maturation can soften the impression
of a young, structured wine while its fresh fruit develops.

St. Laurent also has a place in Czech wines released young for the
Svatomartinské season around St. Martin's Day in November. These give an
early view of the harvest's fruit. More substantial bottlings allow time
for the grape's initially assertive texture to settle.
