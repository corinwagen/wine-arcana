# Grillo

Grillo is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape strongly associated with western
Sicily and the vineyards around Marsala. It supplies both dry table wines
and base wines for fortified Marsala. Its capacity for substantial body,
ripe fruit, and sustained flavour suits these different uses, while fresh
examples often show citrus and floral impressions.

DNA analysis identifies its parents as [Catarratto](catarratto.md) and
Zibibbo, the Sicilian name for Muscat of Alexandria.[^1] The historical
circumstances of the crossing remain less certain. Grillo became
increasingly important in western Sicily during the replanting that
followed phylloxera in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## Growth and fruit set

Grillo grows vigorously and tolerates high temperatures well. Its berries
have substantial skins, and bunches range from loose to more compact.
The combination of plant material, rootstock, and pruning affects how
much foliage and fruit a vineyard produces.

The Italian Vitis Database records differences in bunch form and
productivity between two biotypes. It also describes losses through poor
fruit set and berry shedding, which can make the crop irregular. Excessive
vigour can aggravate fruit-set problems. Rootstock choice and the number
of buds retained at pruning help growers manage that growth.

Traditional Marsala bush vines coexist with trellised plantings. The grape's
relatively low basal-bud fertility means short spur pruning can reduce
the crop, while retaining a fruiting cane provides buds farther along
the shoot. The choice depends on the vineyard's vigour and intended yield.

## Dry wines

Grillo can give grapefruit, blossom, and sometimes tropical-fruit aromas.
Ripe examples may feel broad and lightly gripping. Its skins supply
phenolic compounds as well as aroma precursors, making pressing and skin
contact relevant to the final texture.

Harvest timing sets the starting balance of sugar, acidity, and aroma.
Fermenting more grape sugar to dryness increases alcohol, while earlier
picking can favour a lighter result. [Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md)
and the choice of tank or wood then offer ways to shape texture and
maturation.

The nursery Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes several Marsala-derived
selections suited to dry wine, with differences in floral, citrus, and
riper-fruit character. Some also have the alcohol and substance sought
for longer maturation. These uses overlap within the variety.

## Marsala and wider Sicily

Grillo's strong association with Marsala reflects its usefulness as a
base for a wine that undergoes [fortification](../concepts/fortification.md)
and prolonged maturation.
Those cellar processes increase alcoholic strength and allow aromas to
evolve beyond the original fresh fruit. A dry, unfortified Grillo retains
a much more immediate connection with the harvest's fruit character.

The grape also grows in other parts of Sicily, including Agrigento,
Palermo, and areas farther east. Sites with different soils and ripening
conditions give producers scope for lighter whites, fuller varietal wines,
and blends.
