# Savvatiano

Savvatiano, also spelled Savatiano, is a white Greek *Vitis vinifera* grape
with a long association with Attica around Athens. Its tolerance of heat
and drought and its productive vines have made it a practical part of the
region's farming. It supplies both ordinary dry white wine and retsina,
the Greek wine flavoured with pine resin.

The grape's own aroma is usually more restrained than the resin character
of retsina. Unresinated examples can show gentle fruit, herbal notes, and
a rounded palate. Separating those two expressions makes the effects of
vineyard and winemaking easier to understand.

## Working with a warm, dry site

Savvatiano can carry a substantial crop. Crop control and harvest timing
help growers retain flavour and enough [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md)
for a dry white. Heat tolerance makes cultivation possible, but the intended
wine still depends on how much fruit the vine must ripen and when it is
picked.

Attica's surviving dry-farmed vineyards are central to the grape's modern
reappraisal. Producers have increasingly bottled wines that identify the
variety and vineyard, giving attention to fruit previously associated with
anonymous volume. Savvatiano is also grown more widely in central Greece,
where differences in water supply, exposure, and cellar practice broaden
its range.

## Dry white wine and retsina

Papagiannakos's Savatiano Old Vines gives a specific example from
southeastern Attica. The estate describes north-facing, unirrigated vines
on limestone soil and temperature-controlled fermentation in stainless
steel. The combination foregrounds the fruit and freshness of that site.
The age of the vines is part of their history; exposure, farming, and
vinification remain important to the wine.

Barrel treatment offers another expression, adding wood influence and
changing texture through maturation. Savvatiano also joins blends with
[Roditis](roditis.md) or [Assyrtiko](assyrtiko.md), which can change the
balance of aroma and acidity.

Retsina adds a further production choice. Its resin character comes from
winemaking, not a naturally pine-flavoured grape. Modern producers pursue
both resinated and unresinated Savvatiano, so the renewed interest in the
variety includes a reassessment of retsina as well as wines that present
the grape without resin.
