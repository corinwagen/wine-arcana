# Passerina

Passerina is a white grape of Italy's central Adriatic regions, especially
Marche and [Abruzzo](../regions/abruzzo.md). It makes dry whites whose freshness can survive a fairly
generous crop, and its acidity also gives growers options for sparkling wine.
Alongside [Pecorino](pecorino.md), it helps explain why these regions' white
wines deserve attention beyond their better-known reds.

## Vineyard and wine

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes a vigorous, productive vine with relatively
late ripening and thick-skinned berries. Its technical profile favours long
pruning. These traits matter together: a vine capable of carrying plenty of
fruit still needs enough functioning leaves and season to ripen it. Managing
[crop load](../concepts/crop-load.md) means matching production to that capacity,
with the intended wine in mind.

The nursery's selections also show why Passerina cannot be reduced to a single
style. Its VCR 6 clone emphasizes acidity and suitability for sparkling wine;
VCR 450 gives more fruit and structure with less pronounced acidity. Those are
observations about particular plant material, rather than fixed properties of
every bottle bearing the name.

## Contemporary Marche expressions

Offida gives Passerina a prominent local identity. Tenuta Cocci Grifoni's
Notturna is an Offida DOCG Passerina, made by temperature-controlled white
vinification. Its existence alongside varietal Marche IGT wines illustrates
two ways the grape reaches the drinker: through a more specific place name or
through the broader regional designation.

Dianetti's Lulù comes from Carassai in the Val Menocchia. The producer describes
late-September harvesting, fermentation in steel and maturation on the fine
lees until spring. Its floral and citrus expression therefore accompanies a
deliberate period of [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md). Steel preserves
a useful distinction here: a white wine can gain texture during maturation
without acquiring oak flavours.

Passerina's appeal lies in this balance between a fresh young-white role and
the possibility of a more substantial palate. Harvest decisions, clonal
material and time on the lees help explain differences that a simple list of
aromas would miss.
