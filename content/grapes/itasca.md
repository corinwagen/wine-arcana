# Itasca

Released in 2017 by the University of Minnesota, Itasca is a white wine
grape combining substantial [winter hardiness](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md) with lower acidity than
some earlier cold-climate varieties. That combination broadens the options
for dry white wine in northern vineyards.

## A different acid balance

In its early winemaking report, the university compares Itasca with its
parent [Frontenac gris](frontenac.md) and emphasizes the lower total acidity of the new
grape.[^1] The comparison concerns a breeding objective and observed fruit
chemistry; individual sites and vintages still produce different results.

Lower [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) can make a dry wine easier to balance
without leaving as much [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md).
Itasca can also accumulate substantial sugar, so the grower must consider
the potential alcohol as well as the acid when choosing a harvest date.
Pear and melon-like fruit are among the breeder's reference points.

## Northern vineyard practice

The university's current profile describes strong winter survival and
useful resistance to both mildews and leaf [phylloxera](../concepts/phylloxera.md). [Black rot](../concepts/black-rot.md) and
anthracnose can nevertheless cause trouble in warm, humid conditions.
Its upright but vigorous growth calls for a training system and canopy
work suited to the site.

Minnesota remains an important centre of production. The university's
regional grower directory includes vineyards across the state, while its
research winery has tested cellar treatments since the grape's release.
The emerging range of wines reflects continued practical work with a
relatively recent variety.

[^1]: Matthew Clark and Andrew Horton, “Itasca Wine Making: New report,”
    University of Minnesota, 11 September 2019.
