# Kisi

Kisi is a white Georgian wine grape associated with Kakheti. It makes
aromatic white wines and amber wines fermented with their skins in qvevri,
the clay vessels used in Georgian winemaking. Its renewed use gives a
smaller regional variety a place alongside the much more widely planted
[Rkatsiteli](rkatsiteli.md).

## Decline and recovery

Georgia's National Wine Agency links Kisi's severe decline around the end
of the twentieth century to the preference for Rkatsiteli under Soviet
production and the subsequent contraction of vineyards. It records surviving
old vines and renewed planting in the Telavi area.[^1]

## From fragrance to texture

Kisi's pressed white wines can suggest pear and flowers. With extended
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), the wine acquires additional
texture from grape solids, and the fruit may seem closer to dried stone
fruit. [Tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) adds a drying sensation distinct
from the freshness supplied by [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md).

Giuaani provides a documented contemporary example from Manavi in Kakheti.
Its Kisi Qvevri ferments and matures with skins for six months. The winery
describes a dry amber wine with floral and dried-fruit aromas and a supple
tannic texture.[^2] This shows Kisi used on its own, with prolonged skin
contact central to the intended style.

The wine belongs to the broader family of
[skin-contact whites](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md). The duration
and amount of contact help determine extraction; the vessel name alone
does not specify them. Georgia's National Wine Agency describes keeping
white qvevri wines with grape solids until spring as a Kakhetian practice.
