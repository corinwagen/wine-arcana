# Ugni Blanc

Ugni Blanc is the white grape known in Italy as Trebbiano Toscano. In France,
it is particularly associated with the wines distilled into Cognac and
Armagnac. In Italy, the same variety contributes to fresh white blends and
to dried-grape Vin Santo. Its productivity, late ripening, and ability to
provide acidity have supported these different uses; a relatively restrained
aroma is often useful rather than a defect.

## Identity

Plantgrape identifies Ugni Blanc with Tuscany's Trebbiano Toscano.[^1]
“Trebbiano” alone is less precise, because the name occurs in several Italian
variety names. A reference to an unspecified Trebbiano should not
automatically be read as Ugni Blanc.

Its importance in France is closely tied to the reconstruction of vineyards
after phylloxera. In Cognac, it largely replaced
[Folle Blanche](folle-blanche.md), whose grafted vines proved more vulnerable
to disease. That change reflects suitability for a demanding production
system rather than a simple judgement that one grape makes better table wine.

## Viticulture

Ugni Blanc is vigorous and bears large bunches. It can be managed with
different training and pruning systems, but its young shoots are sensitive
to wind damage and benefit from support. Plantgrape describes late budbreak
and late ripening, with susceptibility to downy mildew and eutypa dieback,
but relatively little susceptibility to grey rot.

Late ripening helps explain why site and intended use matter. Fruit harvested
for a low-alcohol distilling wine need not reach the sugar concentration
sought for a fuller table wine. In warmer conditions, growers must also
consider the loss of acidity as ripening advances. Vigor and yield are
therefore managed against a particular harvest target, not an abstract
ideal of maximum ripeness.

## Why it suits distillation

A Cognac base wine is an intermediate stage whose balance is judged in
relation to distillation. Patrick Raguenaud, a Cognac master distiller and
former president of the regional interprofessional body, explains that acidity
helps preserve the wine before distillation without sulfiting. He also
describes earlier picking in warmer seasons to retain that acidity.[^2]
Ugni Blanc's generally modest aroma leaves considerable scope for character
to develop through fermentation, distillation, maturation, and blending.

Armagnac gives the grape another important distilling context. Its regional
body likewise emphasizes the combination of acidity and relatively low
alcohol, and describes its use across the area's terroirs. The grape name
alone does not make Cognac and Armagnac equivalent: origin and production
choices continue to shape the resulting spirit.

## Italian wine expressions

In Tuscany, Trebbiano Toscano can contribute a fresh, relatively neutral
component to white blends. The Maremma consortium describes restrained fruit
and useful acidity, and records uses ranging from white table wine to
late-harvest and dried-grape styles.

Vin Santo del Chianti shows how profoundly processing can change its role.
Trebbiano Toscano and Malvasia Bianca Lunga are traditional components. Bunches
are dried after harvest, concentrating the juice, then the wine undergoes
fermentation and extended maturation in small wooden vessels called
*caratelli*. The finished balance can vary in sweetness. These are substantial
wines built through concentration and time, a very different expression from
either an early-drinking white blend or a wine awaiting the still.
