# Vespaiola

Vespaiola is a white grape closely associated with Breganze, in the Veneto
north of Vicenza. It appears in fresh still and sparkling wines, commonly
labeled Vespaiolo, and in the sweet dried-grape wine Torcolato. Its local
importance spans both everyday white wine and a demanding concentration
process.

## One grape, different uses

The regional coöperative association identifies Vespaiola as a defining grape
of Breganze and documents still Vespaiolo, sparkling Vespaiolo, and Torcolato
in the Beato Bartolomeo coöperative's range. These provide a practical way to
understand the grape through different production choices within the same
area.

For Torcolato, [drying](../concepts/appassimento.md) occurs before pressing and fermentation. Maculan's
technical description specifies four months in a drying room for selected
Vespaiola bunches, followed by a year in French oak barrels. The estate
describes a full, sweet wine whose [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) balances the concentrated sugars.
This is a documented producer example, not a universal maturation schedule.

The contrast with a fresh white lies partly in the starting material.
Water loss makes the dried fruit yield richer juice, and subsequent
fermentation leaves the [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) that
contributes sweetness. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) adds
another influence on aroma and texture.

A comparison with [Nosiola](nosiola.md) in Trentino or
[Erbaluce](erbaluce.md) in Piedmont shows a wider northern Italian pattern:
a locally important white grape can sustain a dry wine tradition alongside
a distinct wine made from dried grapes. Each area's methods and wines
deserve to be understood individually.
