# Olaszrizling

Olaszrizling is a white grape with several Central European identities:
Welschriesling in Austria, Graševina in Croatia, and Riesling Italico in Italy.
It is distinct from [Riesling](riesling.md). Its importance is particularly
clear around Hungary's Lake [Balaton](../regions/balaton.md), where it connects everyday drinking with
wines identified by village and vineyard.

## Balaton and changing expectations

Sue Tolson's 2025 account for Wines of Hungary describes a broad range of
contemporary styles, from light whites and sparkling wines to skin-contact
and sweet examples. The familiar *fröccs*, wine diluted with sparkling water,
remains part of its cultural setting. Alongside this everyday role, growers
have developed more explicitly place-based wines. The [Csopak](../regions/csopak.md) Codex initiative,
started in 2012, is one expression of that work.[^1]

These uses place different demands on the fruit. A refreshing base for a
spritzer benefits from [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and a direct flavour;
a fuller vineyard bottling gives more attention to concentration and texture.
Neither use requires the grape to imitate Riesling.

## A producer's range

Jásdi in Csopak describes Olaszrizling as its principal variety and discusses
both light, acidic wines and richer late-harvest or botrytized examples. The
estate also blends it with [Furmint](furmint.md) in Ranolder White. This gives
a concrete view of the grape's flexibility within one locality: harvest
condition, crop management, and blending change its role.

Its many regional names can obscure this connection across borders. Reading
the place and producer alongside the synonym is especially useful when
comparing a Hungarian dry white with an Austrian sweet wine or a Croatian
Graševina.

[^1]: Sue Tolson, “Olaszrizling sparkles with a variety of styles,”
