# Norton

Norton is an American red grape strongly associated with Virginia and
Missouri, where it is also known as Cynthiana. Its wines can combine dark
fruit, substantial body, and pronounced acidity. It gives those regions
a wine identity grounded in American grape ancestry.

![Historical colour plate labelled Norton, showing a dark grape bunch and leaves.](../../media/images/grapes/norton.jpg "Norton plate from The Grapes of New York (1908).")

## Ripeness and acidity

Virginia Cooperative Extension describes Norton as a late-ripening grape
with small berries and clusters. Its account emphasizes the potential
for high fruit acidity and the importance of exposing the crop to light
through an appropriate training system. This makes the length of the
season and the condition of the canopy consequential to the eventual wine.

[Acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) contribute
different sensations. A dark, full Norton can feel sharp without being
especially drying; extraction and maturation further modify the balance.
A description based only on colour or weight misses that distinction.

## Two regional homes

Missouri's wine organization presents Norton as a central dry-red variety
and records Cynthiana as an alternative name. Virginia's 2026 grape guide
likewise places it within the state's contemporary wines, emphasizing
dark fruit and bright acidity. Its continuing relevance extends beyond
an account of nineteenth-century American viticulture.

Comparing wines from the two states is most useful when harvest conditions
and cellar choices are known. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md)
can add spice or vanilla and change texture, while the grape's own fruit
and acid balance remain the foundation.
