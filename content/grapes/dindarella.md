# Dindarella

Dindarella is a rare red grape of the Verona area, retained among the local
varieties of [Valpolicella](../regions/valpolicella.md). Its significance lies
partly in the region's practice of blending and drying grapes: varieties
that occupy little vineyard area can still offer useful differences in fruit
condition and behavior after harvest.

## Identity and preservation

Pelara is a synonym, though older authors sometimes treated it as a separate
grape. The Veneto region's conservation account describes work on local
plant material beginning in the 1970s, followed by morphological comparisons
that brought these forms together under Dindarella. It records national
registration with Pelara as a synonym in 1987.[^1]

The same account describes a vigorous vine suited to long pruning, with
fruit that keeps well both on the vine and in the drying room. Bunch
looseness can differ between forms. Those differences matter when growers
are selecting material for a process in which the grapes must remain sound
while losing water.

## A place among local blending grapes

The Valpolicella consortium continues to include Dindarella in its overview
of the area's varieties. It occupies a much smaller role than
[Corvina](corvina.md), but helps show why the region's grape heritage extends
beyond the main components named in most introductions to its wines.

Its most useful context is therefore the local blend and the handling of
fruit before fermentation. The finished wine reflects the proportion used,
the other varieties, and whether the grapes were dried.

[^1]: Regione del Veneto, *Programma di sviluppo rurale 2007–2013*, Annex 5,
    Dindarella entry, p. 15.
