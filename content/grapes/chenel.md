# Chenel

Chenel is a white South African grape developed by crossing
[Chenin Blanc](chenin-blanc.md) with [Ugni Blanc](ugni-blanc.md).
Its history connects local breeding and productive vineyards with
contemporary varietal and skin-contact wines. Springfontein's current
range explores it through several different cellar methods.

## Breeding and identity

Ampelographers Jean-Michel Boursiquot and Virginie Grondain identify Chenel
and Weldra as distinct varieties created from Chenin Blanc and Ugni Blanc
in Professor Orffer's South African breeding work.[^parents] Ugni Blanc
is the same variety called Trebbiano Toscano in Italy. Chenel has its own
identity as a selected offspring of that parent pair.

A 2025 WineLand report, drawing on interviews with Elsenburg and Vinpro
specialists, places the initial cross in 1950 and commercial release in 1974.
It describes years of evaluation, including selection for improved
tolerance of berry cracking and rot compared with Chenin.[^history]
The chronology separates creating a seedling from releasing a cultivar.

## Vineyard use and changing priorities

The same report associates Chenel's earlier use with productive vineyards
and distilling-wine supply. It describes considerable yields under suitable
training and irrigation, followed by a decline as plantings were removed
and not replaced. That history concerns vineyard economics and the
intended product as well as grape characteristics.

Reported tolerance of wet conditions is useful context, but vineyard
health still depends on the site, plant material, and season. Canopy
arrangement and crop balance determine how a productive vine carries
fruit towards a particular harvest target.

## Contemporary Cape wines

Springfontein near Stanford in [Walker Bay](../regions/walker-bay.md)
provides a documented contemporary example. Its Terroir Selection range
includes a varietal Chenel, while blends and small experimental wines
give the grape other roles.

Its Skins Agleam Chenel uses extended
[skin contact](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md). That method gives
white-grape skins time to contribute phenolics and texture, producing a
different structural starting point from promptly pressed white wine.
Juices Untamed explores intracellular fermentation, showing another
approach to handling intact fruit.

Those examples make Chenel useful to consider through both breeding
history and current winemaking. Harvest ripeness, pressing, skin handling,
and maturation determine how its relatively restrained fruit becomes
a finished wine. They also provide a practical comparison with wines
from its Chenin parent and with South Africa's other locally bred
reference, [Pinotage](pinotage.md).

[^parents]: Boursiquot & Grondain (2016), section “Historique, origine

[^history]: WineLand, “Enchanted by Chenel,” 2 February 2025, interviews
