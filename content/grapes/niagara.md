# Niagara

Niagara is a white American grape used for fresh eating, juice, jelly,
and wine. Its pronounced grapey aroma places it close to a familiar juice
tradition, while regional wineries use that character in their own
bottlings. The variety name should be distinguished from the [Niagara Peninsula](../regions/niagara-peninsula.md)
winegrowing region: a wine from that region need not contain Niagara grapes.

![Historical colour plate labelled Niagara, showing a pale grape bunch and leaves.](../../media/images/grapes/niagara.jpg "Niagara plate from The Grapes of New York (1908).")

## A grape with several markets

The University of Missouri describes large fruit and the distinctive
aroma often called *foxy*. The same cultivation account records
susceptibility to common grape diseases.

Penn State's Lake Erie research centre maintains Niagara alongside
[Concord](concord.md). These grapes connect winegrowing with the wider
regional production of fruit and juice. Their agricultural and cultural
importance is larger than their visibility in discussions of European
wine varieties.

## A Finger Lakes example

Lakewood Vineyards documents its 2020 Niagara as entirely varietal,
estate-grown fruit from Lakewood Farm in the
[Finger Lakes](../regions/finger-lakes.md). That establishes a specific
local use of the grape rather than treating every bottle bearing a
geographic Niagara name as the same thing.

When comparing Niagara wines, the balance of
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and [remaining sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md)
is especially useful information. A powerful fruit aroma can suggest
sweetness, but aroma and the actual amount of sugar are different
properties.
