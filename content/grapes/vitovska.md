# Vitovska

Vitovska is a white grape associated with the Carso around Trieste and the
neighboring Kras across the Slovenian border. Its contemporary importance
rests on producers exploring several expressions of this small regional
tradition, from directly pressed whites to wines fermented with their skins.

## Landscape and cellar choices

The Carso is a limestone landscape with thin patches of red soil, exposed
to the Bora wind. The local growers' association describes Vitovska as adapted
to the area's cold winters and dry summer conditions. Such local adaptation
still leaves the individual vineyard's water supply and exposure important.

Winemaking strongly affects the result. Directly pressed examples can
emphasize restrained fruit, herbal notes, and a dry, almond-like finish.
[Maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) with the skins can deepen the color,
bring riper fruit and hay-like associations, and add
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md). The producer's approach therefore tells a
reader a great deal about a bottle's likely texture.

Zidarich's Kamen is a particularly clear example of a contemporary cellar
experiment within the grape's home region. The estate describes commissioning
local stoneworkers to make limestone vats for macerating Vitovska. The vessel
is part of that wine's production method; the broader stylistic distinction
is the extraction that occurs while juice remains in contact with skins.

Vitovska rewards comparison across those approaches. The same regional
identity can appear in a pale white with a relatively gentle texture or in a
more deeply colored, firmly structured wine.
