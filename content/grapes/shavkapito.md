# Shavkapito

Shavkapito is a red grape associated with Kartli in eastern Georgia. Its
wines can combine cherry-like fruit and herbal notes with a relatively
light ruby colour, although site and extraction produce considerable
variation. Contemporary bottlings include both qvevri wines and dry reds
matured in oak.

## Kartli and the vine

The National Wine Agency describes Shavkapito as a mid-season ripener
with moderately compact bunches and dark blue berries. It associates
fuller wines with valley-floor sites and lighter examples with slopes,
a regional tendency rather than a rule for every vineyard. The agency's
2004 census figure was only ten hectares; that historical figure indicates
a small recorded footprint, not the grape's current planted area.[^1]

Kartli also grows the white grapes [Chinuri](chinuri.md) and
[Goruli Mtsvane](goruli-mtsvane.md). Shavkapito adds a red-wine counterpart
to a regional picture often associated with fresh whites and sparkling
wine.

## Two contemporary cellar approaches

Tevza offers a qvevri example. Importer Georgian Wine House describes
winemaker Goga Tevzadze's work with Kartli varieties and notes a Shavkapito
made with longer maceration and a more oxidative approach. Its account
concerns a particular release, so those choices should not be read as a
recipe for every Tevza vintage.

Agidela's 2024 Shavkapito from Shida Kartli provides a second documented
approach: a dry red with five months in oak barrels. The producer describes
berry fruit alongside oak-derived impressions.[^2]

These examples make [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md),
[oxidation](../concepts/oxidation.md), and
[oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) useful parts of the grape's
story. Skin contact governs extraction of colour and tannin; exposure to
oxygen and time in wood then alter the developing wine. A lighter colour
alone gives only a partial account of its structure.

[^2]: Agidela, “Shavkapito,” 2024 wine description, accessed
