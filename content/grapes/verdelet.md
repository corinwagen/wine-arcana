# Verdelet

Verdelet is a white French-American [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md)
also known as Seibel 9110. Its history includes both fresh-fruit and wine
uses. Contemporary [Finger Lakes](../regions/finger-lakes.md) winemaking
gives it another context,
including blends shaped by skin contact and separate maturation of their
components.

## An older variety and a vineyard tradeoff

Cornell's 1979 New York guide identifies Verdelet as a yellow-gold dessert
and wine grape. It describes a tendency to carry excessive fruit and
susceptibility to winter injury, explaining why it had become less frequently
planted in the period covered by that bulletin.[^history]

Those observations describe two separate constraints. Managing
[crop load](../concepts/crop-load.md) helps match fruit demand to the vine's
capacity; choosing a site with suitable winter conditions addresses survival
of the permanent plant. Hybrid ancestry covers varieties with markedly
different combinations of those traits.

## Skin contact in the Finger Lakes

Usonia's 2024 ’Member When? pairs Verdelet with Geneva White #2, in roughly
equal proportions. The producer describes foot-crushing the Verdelet and
giving it three days of maceration before ageing it in a 500-litre cask.
Geneva White #2 received a different treatment: a week of carbonic maceration,
followed by maturation in a polymer tank. The wines were blended after
about eight months.[^usonia]

The example makes [skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md)
a useful companion to the grape's name. Contact with skins changes texture
and extraction, while separate vessels let the winemaker develop each
component before blending.

[^history]: Pool and colleagues, *Grape varieties for New York State*,
    1979, Verdelet entry; its assessment of planting frequency is historical.
[^usonia]: Usonia, “’Member When? 2024,” composition and cellar account.
