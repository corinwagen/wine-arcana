# Ribolla Gialla

Ribolla Gialla is a white grape shared by northeastern Italy and Slovenia,
where it is called Rebula. Its range runs from light, citrus-led still whites
and sparkling wine to amber wines fermented with their skins. Understanding
that range requires looking at both vineyard location and the point at which
juice is separated from the grape skins.

## The border hills

[Friuli](../regions/friuli.md) and Slovenia's Brda form the grape's central contemporary landscape.
In Brda, Rebula remains an important part of ordinary commercial winegrowing,
as well as a vehicle for experimental and long-matured wines. Klet Brda's range
includes fresh, barrel-aged and sparkling expressions; the coöperative records
making sparkling Rebula since 1962. Sparkling wine is therefore an established
part of this grape's local history, alongside today's attention to amber wines.

The names need distinguishing from [Schioppettino](schioppettino.md), the red
Friulian grape also called Ribolla Nera. A shared word on a label does not make
the wines interchangeable.

## Vineyard behaviour

Rauscedo describes late budbreak, vigorous growth and a preference for
well-exposed, ventilated hills. Later budbreak can reduce exposure to some
[spring frosts](../concepts/spring-frost.md), while humid conditions still
create problems: the nursery records poor fruit set and bunch rot in wet
seasons. Long pruning and attention to the canopy help growers manage the
vine's growth and fruiting.

Freshly pressed Ribolla commonly gives restrained floral impressions and
pronounced [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). That combination helps explain
its usefulness for sparkling wine, where a second fermentation and subsequent
maturation add another layer to a relatively delicate base.

## Skins change the wine

Fermenting with skins extracts phenolic material, changing colour, texture and
aroma. Ribolla's amber versions can consequently feel firm and savoury, with
more grip than a conventionally pressed white. The length of
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), the vessel and the period of maturation
all matter; “Ribolla Gialla” alone does not specify the method.

This Friulian practice has also travelled. Matthiasson documents grafting
Ribolla into its Napa Valley home vineyard in 2006 and producing a skin-contact
version from 2008, following work with George Vare and inspiration from Josko
Gravner. Its current Matthiasson Vineyard bottling continues that approach.
This is a sustained Californian interpretation of a border-region tradition,
not evidence that the grape has become a major Napa planting.
