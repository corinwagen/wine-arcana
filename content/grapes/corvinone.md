# Corvinone

Corvinone is a red grape of the Verona area, particularly important in
[Valpolicella](../regions/valpolicella.md) and its dried-grape wines. Despite
the suggestive name and large berries, it is a distinct variety rather than
a large-berried clone of [Corvina](corvina.md). The Italian Vitis Database
maintains it as a separately identified cultivar, supported by morphology
and genetic markers.[^identity]

Understanding that distinction matters when reading about a Valpolicella
blend. Corvina and Corvinone can occupy much of the same legal space while
contributing different vineyard behaviour and wine composition. A wine
described casually as “Corvina-based” may conceal substantial Corvinone.

## In the vineyard

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes a vigorous, productive vine that buds
late and ripens in the middle-to-late part of the season. Its large bunches
carry substantial, thick-skinned berries. Basal buds are fruitful, an
important practical detail when selecting a
[pruning system](../concepts/pruning-and-training.md).

Vigour and reliable production require attention to
[crop load](../concepts/crop-load.md), especially where a large crop and
shaded canopy would make uniform ripening difficult. The nursery also notes
some susceptibility to Botrytis in humid conditions. Soundness matters
particularly for fruit intended for drying: a thick skin is useful, but
does not make a damaged or infected bunch suitable for prolonged storage.

## Fresh grapes and appassimento

Corvinone can enter straightforward Valpolicella and Bardolino blends, or
undergo [appassimento](../concepts/appassimento.md) for
[Amarone](../styles/amarone-della-valpolicella.md) and Recioto. Removing
water before fermentation changes concentration and the skin-to-juice ratio;
it also changes the berry metabolically. The resulting wine is therefore
more than a stronger version of a fresh-grape red.

The nursery's clone descriptions include fruit-led wines and selections
suited to drying. They also differ in colour and structure. This is a useful
reminder that “Corvinone” identifies a variety, not one uniform vineyard
selection or cellar outcome.

## Wine character and the blend

A University of Verona study of small, standardized vinifications found
Corvinone among the local varieties with relatively modest phenolic levels,
alongside appreciable aroma potential. Results differed between its clones.
Those findings support a nuanced account of its role, rather than treating
its large berries or imposing name as proof of unusually heavy tannin.[^wine]

In the Valpolicella specification consolidated in 2023, Corvina and/or
Corvinone together account for 45–95% of the vineyard composition, with
[Rondinella](rondinella.md) at 5–30%. The allowance makes Corvinone a possible
principal component, not merely a minor correction to Corvina. Harvest
maturity, drying, extraction, and blending still determine the character
of the finished bottle.
