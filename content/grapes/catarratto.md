# Catarratto

Catarratto is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape grown throughout Sicily, with
a particularly strong presence in the west. Its wines can combine citrus
and floral aromas with substantial texture and a gently bitter finish.
It supplies varietal dry whites, regional blends, and grapes for Marsala.

The names Catarratto Bianco Comune and Catarratto Bianco Lucido describe
different forms of the same variety. Although separately listed in Italy's
national register, they share the same microsatellite DNA profile. The
Italian Vitis Database treats them as biotypes with visible and compositional
differences.[^1] *Lucido* refers to the berries' glossy appearance.

## Growth and variation

Catarratto's considerable variation matters to growers. The regional
collection includes material differing in bunch size, productivity, and
the balance of alcohol and [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) in its wines. Some selections produce
larger crops more consistently than others. Short pruning and systems
that retain a replacement fruiting cane are both used.

In a productive vineyard, the number of shoots and bunches affects the
vine's demand for water and the time needed to ripen its crop. Pruning
sets the initial crop potential; subsequent
[canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) controls foliage and
exposure. These decisions become particularly consequential when summer
water is scarce.

Trapani, Palermo, and Agrigento are important growing areas. Catarratto
also reaches eastern Sicily, where it contributes to local white blends.
Differences in elevation, exposure, and the intended wine give growers
reasons to harvest at different stages of maturity.

## Dry whites and texture

Catarratto can retain freshness alongside a fairly broad palate. Orange
blossom and citrus are useful points of recognition, while some wines
show herbal impressions or a bitter almond-like finish. These traits
vary among selections and with cellar handling.

Agricola Cortese's Nostru Catarratto Lucido provides one example of a dry
wine shaped by harvest and sediment contact. Its technical account describes
vineyards at about 400 metres, early-morning picking around mid-September,
gentle pressing, and cool settling before fermentation. The wine then
spends about five months on fine lees, with regular stirring.[^2]
[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can add breadth to the texture,
while harvesting cool fruit limits heating during transport and pressing.

## In Sicilian blends

Catarratto's western history includes the vineyards supplying Marsala.
On [Etna](../regions/etna.md), the Bianco specification permits up to 40%
Catarratto Comune or Lucido alongside a minimum of 60%
[Carricante](carricante.md).[^3] A producer can therefore use Catarratto
as a substantial component of the mountain's white wine, with the final
balance depending on the proportions and the fruit harvested.
