# Carricante

Carricante is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape associated above all with
[Mount Etna](../regions/etna.md) in eastern Sicily. Its wines can combine firm acidity with
substantial body and citrus or floral impressions. That combination makes
it valuable both as a varietal white and as a source of freshness in
local blends.

The name is traditionally explained through its capacity to carry a crop.
Carricante is productive, with relatively fertile buds even near the base
of the shoots. This helps explain its suitability for the short pruning
used on traditional bush vines. Growers also cultivate it on trellises.

## Crop and place

The Italian Vitis Database distinguishes Carricante material with smaller,
more compact bunches from material with larger, looser ones. Such variation
contributes to differences in yield and wine composition. The variety can
retain appreciable acidity alongside a substantial potential alcohol
level, although harvest decisions and the season change the balance.

Etna's altitude provides a cooler setting than much of lowland Sicily.
Carricante grows even above some of the mountain's red-grape vineyards.
The denomination's geographical account describes its harvest as generally
falling between late September and the middle of October. Later sites need
autumn weather that allows the grapes to finish ripening.

Carricante also grows away from Etna. Agricola Cortese near Vittoria, for
example, describes picking its Carricante around the beginning of September
from vineyards at about 400 metres. Its use of short pruning to restrain
production illustrates the practical importance of crop control outside
the mountain setting.[^1]

## Wine and maturation

Carricante's citrus and blossom impressions are often accompanied by a
fresh, savoury palate. Its acidity supports wines with more weight than
an austere first impression might suggest. The regional selection described
in the Italian Vitis Database is suitable for varietal wines that develop
in bottle and for blends needing acidity and structure.

[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) offers another way to shape the
texture. Cortese keeps its fermented Carricante on fine yeast sediment
for several months and stirs it regularly. Contact with this sediment can
add palate breadth while the wine retains its acid framework. This is a
documented producer practice; Carricante can be made with other maturation
choices.

## Etna Bianco and Milo

Etna Bianco requires at least 60% Carricante. Etna Bianco Superiore requires
at least 80%, and its grapes must come from the defined part of the commune
of Milo. These are separate composition and origin requirements.[^2]
Catarratto and other permitted white grapes can contribute to the blends.
A wine made entirely from Carricante therefore represents one choice within
a broader local tradition of white winemaking.
