# Nocera

Nocera is a dark-skinned grape associated especially with northeastern
Sicily, around Messina and Milazzo. It survives both in regional blends and
in varietal wines that make its contribution easier to explore. Its modern
story is one of recovery from severe contraction, rather than a newly
discovered grape with no local past.

## A surviving Messina variety

IRVO, Sicily's regional wine institute, treats Nocera's origin as uncertain.
Its March 2025 account draws on nineteenth-century descriptions of extensive
cultivation on the Milazzo plain and traces its later displacement, partly
by [Nerello Mascalese](nerello-mascalese.md). Earlier demand for deeply
coloured blending wine had helped sustain it; a change in commercial use
could therefore change the fortunes of the vineyard variety itself.

The same report identifies a surviving concentration around Milazzo,
Furnari, and the hills of Messina, including the Faro area. It records about
47 hectares in Sicily in 2021, while also noting cultivation in Calabria.
That dated figure conveys the small scale of the Sicilian planting without
assuming that it remains unchanged.[^area]

## Blending and varietal bottling

Nocera belongs to the local blending traditions of Faro and Mamertino.
For readers, the important distinction is between its supporting role in a
wine assembled from several grapes and the more direct view offered by a
varietal bottling. Neither makes the other obsolete.

Planeta's Nocera, first made in 2015, is a contemporary reference from La
Baronia at Capo Milazzo. The estate identifies it as entirely Nocera and
places the vineyard at only about 40 metres above sea level. This coastal
setting is a useful contrast with the high vineyards of nearby
[Etna](../regions/etna.md): northeastern Sicily contains very different
growing environments within a relatively small area.

The estate describes deep ruby colour, ripe fruit, and peppery or herbal
notes in its wine, with a supple palate. Those observations should remain
attached to this interpretation, rather than becoming a promise that all
Nocera is soft or tastes identical. Its Mamertino bottling, by comparison,
combines Nocera with [Nero d'Avola](nero-davola.md), showing how one property
can preserve both regional blending and varietal expression.

## Vineyard context

At La Baronia, Planeta reports spur-pruned cordons and organic cultivation
with cover crops. These details connect the grape to active vineyard
management: a rediscovered variety still needs choices about soil cover,
canopy, and [pruning](../concepts/pruning-and-training.md). They describe
one documented site, rather than a universal prescription for Nocera.
