# Vidal Blanc

Vidal Blanc is a white [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) particularly associated with Canadian
[icewine](../styles/icewine.md), while also making dry and sweeter wines in the United States.
Its usefulness for a winter harvest depends on the berries remaining
sound long after normal picking time.

## Staying on the vine

Purdue's cultivar guide describes thick-skinned berries on large, relatively
loose bunches and identifies that fruit durability as an advantage for
icewine. It also cautions that the vine needs a sufficiently long growing
season and a suitable winter climate. Fruit that survives late hanging
and wood that survives severe cold are separate agricultural questions.

The same guide notes later budbreak, which can reduce exposure to some
spring freezes. Crop size still needs attention: a productive vine may
require thinning to ripen its fruit adequately.

## Canada and the Midwest

Inniskillin's Ontario Vidal Icewine provides a documented Canadian example.
The winery describes concentrated tropical and stone-fruit aromas with
pronounced [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). In such a sweet wine, acidity
helps prevent the sugar from dominating the whole impression.

Missouri offers a broader contrast. The state's wine organization documents
Vidal as dry or semi-dry varietal wine, in blends, and in sweet sparkling
styles. Apple and pear-like impressions can be more prominent in these
less concentrated expressions.

The variety name consequently leaves a major question unanswered: whether
the fruit was picked for an ordinary table wine or held for a concentrated
sweet style. Harvest method and [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md)
are essential companions to the grape name.
