# Scheurebe

Scheurebe is an aromatic German white grape whose wines can suggest
blackcurrant and grapefruit. It supports both dry whites and sweet wines,
with ripeness and remaining sugar strongly affecting how its perfume is
perceived. Rheinhessen and the Pfalz provide useful contemporary reference
points.

## Breeding and ripening

Georg Scheu bred the variety at Alzey in 1916. The German Wine Institute reports
that DNA work corrected its once-assumed pedigree: the parents are
[Riesling](riesling.md) and Bukettrebe, rather than Riesling and [Silvaner](silvaner.md).[^1]
Sämling 88 is an alternative name.

The institute places its ripening shortly before Riesling and notes that
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) falls late in maturation. This creates a consequential picking
choice: waiting can develop fruit character and sugar while changing the
wine's eventual sharpness. Susceptibility to botrytis also matters. Suitable
conditions can support concentrated sweet wines, but growers making dry wine
need healthy fruit.

## Dry and sweet expressions

Sweet, botrytized bottlings helped establish Scheurebe's reputation in the
mid-twentieth century. [Residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) gives
its forceful aroma a different setting from a fully dry fermentation.

Pfeffingen's dry Ungstein Scheurebe in the Pfalz offers a specific comparison.
The estate describes hand-picking ripe fruit from a warm, south-facing part of
Herrenberg and leaving the crushed grapes briefly on their skins before
pressing. Its account emphasizes grapefruit, lime, and body. Short
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) here forms part of an aromatic white-wine
method; it does not automatically make the wine a prolonged skin-fermented
style.

[^1]: German Wine Institute, “Scheurebe,” breeding history and account of
    the 2012 parentage correction.
