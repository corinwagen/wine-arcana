# Furmint

Furmint is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape central to [Tokaj](../regions/tokaj.md)
in northeastern Hungary. It supplies both incisive dry whites and the
concentrated, botrytized fruit used for the region's sweet wines. Its
combination of firm acidity and capacity to accumulate sugar gives growers
considerable scope to pursue different styles through harvest decisions.

The Vitis International Variety Catalogue records Alba Imputotato and
Heunisch Weiss, also known as Gouais Blanc, as its marker-confirmed
parents.[^1] Furmint has a long history in Hungary and neighbouring
countries. Its regional names include Šipon in Slovenia and Moslavac in
Croatia.

## Choosing the harvest

For dry Furmint, growers seek ripe, healthy grapes with a useful balance
of sugar and acidity. Tokaj's regional wine council describes the dry-wine
harvest as generally falling in September and early October, substantially
earlier than the traditional late-October harvest shared with [Hárslevelű](harslevelu.md).
Fruit intended for sweet wine may remain longer, subject to the weather
and the development of suitable shrivelled berries.

These are different demands on a vineyard. Leaving fruit through autumn
increases exposure to rain and rot, while an earlier harvest gives up
some potential concentration. The intended wine helps determine which
trade-off a grower accepts.

## Dry wine and acidity

Young dry Furmint often suggests apple, pear, or citrus. Fuller examples
can show riper orchard fruit and a more substantial palate. Its relatively
restrained varietal perfume leaves fermentation and maturation choices
readily apparent.

[Acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) is an important part of its structure.
A small amount of [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) can soften the impression of sharpness,
while fully dry wines may feel particularly taut. Riper grapes can yield
more alcohol and weight. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md)
can add spice and alter texture, and some concentrated dry wines develop
well in bottle.

## Noble rot and sweet wine

Under suitable autumn conditions, *Botrytis cinerea* infects ripe berries
and promotes the development of [noble rot](../styles/botrytized-sweet-wine.md). Water loss concentrates the
juice, while infection changes the berry's chemistry. Growers select the
shriveled fruit used for Aszú, often making successive passes through
the vineyard as berries develop at different rates.

A study of Furmint and Hárslevelű in Tokaj followed changes in berry
texture, composition, and associated microorganisms through noble rot.
The early stages brought marked textural changes; larger chemical changes
followed later. The findings show why the stage of berry development
matters to the raw material for sweet wine.[^2]

## Beyond Tokaj

Furmint also makes dry wine on Hungary's Somló hill and around the northern
shore of Lake Balaton. Around Rust in Austria it contributes to both dry
and sweet wines. These settings extend its range beyond Tokaj's particular
combination of autumn humidity, berry selection, and sweet-wine traditions.
