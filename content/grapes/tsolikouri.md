# Tsolikouri

Tsolikouri is a major white wine grape of western Georgia, especially
Imereti and Guria, with further plantings in neighbouring regions. Its
uses range from dry whites and qvevri blends to the semi-sweet wines of
Tvishi in Lechkhumi. That range makes the intended style more informative
than a single description of the grape's weight or sweetness.

## Western Georgia and wine character

The National Wine Agency describes Tsolikouri as suited to western
Georgia's humid conditions, with relatively thick berry skins. It also
describes broad, sometimes slightly oily dry wines. In Imeretian blends,
[Tsitska](tsitska.md) can provide a lighter partner.

Descriptions of [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) vary. The agency
characterizes conventional dry examples as relatively soft, while
Georgian Wine House describes the fruit used for Baia's Tsitska-Tsolikouri
as high in acid. The accounts concern different contexts: site, harvest,
blending, and vinification can all change the balance in the glass.

## Partial skin contact in Imereti

Baia's Wine offers a documented example from Obcha. Its importer describes
estate-grown Tsitska and Tsolikouri fermented together in qvevri with only
part of the skins, then transferred to stainless steel after three
months.[^1] This is a useful alternative to assuming that all qvevri
wines undergo the same extraction.

Georgia's National Wine Agency contrasts traditional Imeretian use of a
portion of the grape solids with Kakhetian use of the full amount.
The proportion of solids and the length of
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) are separate decisions. Less solid
material changes the available supply of [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md),
even when the wine remains in contact for months.

Tsolikouri therefore connects western Georgian blending traditions with
the contemporary interest in [skin-contact white wines](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md).
A dry qvevri blend and a semi-sweet Tvishi also illustrate two different
contributors to texture: extraction from grape solids and
[residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md). Amber colour does not
identify the sweetness of the finished wine.

[^1]: Georgian Wine House, “Baia's Tsitska-Tsolikouri,” vineyard and
    vinification account, accessed 21 September 2026.
