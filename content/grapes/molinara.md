# Molinara

Molinara is a red grape of the Veneto, best understood through the blending
traditions of [Valpolicella](../regions/valpolicella.md). It offers a useful
counterpoint to the assumption that every red-wine ingredient must maximize
colour and tannin. A lighter contribution can be a deliberate part of the
style a producer wants to make.

The Italian Vitis Database records Rossano and Rossanella as official
synonyms. Its Italian profile connects the name Molinara with the conspicuous
waxy bloom on the berries, likened to flour from a mill. That surface bloom
should not be confused with the pigmentation extracted from grape skins
during winemaking.

## Its place in a blend

Molinara can accompany [Corvina](corvina.md) and
[Rondinella](rondinella.md). It is not compulsory in the Valpolicella
specification consolidated in 2023, which names the principal proportions
for Corvina/Corvinone and Rondinella and permits other suitable red grapes
within additional limits. A traditional association therefore does not
mean that every Valpolicella contains it.

Speri offers a clear contemporary example of continuing use. Its published
Valpolicella Classico composition includes 10% Molinara, alongside Corvina
and Rondinella, with short maturation in steel and glass-lined concrete.
This places Molinara within a deliberately youthful regional red. The
example establishes an active stylistic choice, without implying that the
finished wine is an isolated demonstration of Molinara's flavour.

## Colour, tannin, and interpretation

A University of Verona experiment compared wines from local grapes grown
in one vineyard and vinified by a common small-scale protocol. Molinara
had particularly low total phenolics in that comparison.[^study] That
provides a firmer basis for discussing a lighter structural contribution
than simply assuming all dark-skinned grapes behave alike.

It also helps explain why Molinara and [Oseleta](oseleta.md) should not be
treated as equivalent “minor grapes.” Their potential contributions differ.
The useful question is what a blend needs, including the balance between
aroma, body, and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md), rather than whether a
variety supplies the largest amount of any one component.

[^study]: D. Slaghenaufi et al., *OENO One* 55 (2021), pp. 279–294. The
    experiment describes particular vineyard material and vinifications,
    not a fixed composition for every Molinara wine.
