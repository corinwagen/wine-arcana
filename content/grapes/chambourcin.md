# Chambourcin

Chambourcin is a French-American [hybrid](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) red grape with an established
place in the eastern and midwestern United States. Its colour can be
striking even when the finished wine has relatively gentle tannins.
That combination makes it useful both as a varietal wine and in blends.

## Colour and texture

Missouri's wine organization identifies Chambourcin as a
[teinturier](../concepts/teinturier.md): its flesh contributes pigment,
in addition to the skins. Colour therefore cannot serve as a direct
measure of [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md). A winemaker can obtain a deeply
coloured wine without pursuing the maximum possible skin and seed
extraction.

The same regional account describes cherry-like fruit and a generally
supple texture. Barrel maturation can supply another layer of flavour;
vanilla or toast in a bottling should be considered alongside its
[oak treatment](../concepts/oak-maturation.md).

## Eastern vineyards

North Carolina State's survey of active vineyards, based on work in 2022,
records Chambourcin across several of the state's growing areas. It is
therefore part of a continuing regional industry, alongside European
varieties and other hybrids.[^1]

The University of Missouri lists [black rot](../concepts/black-rot.md), mildews, and gray mold among
its disease concerns. Hybrid ancestry does not confer equal protection
against every pathogen. Local rainfall and infection pressure remain
important to vineyard management and the condition of fruit at harvest.

[^1]: North Carolina State Extension, *A Preliminary Analysis of North
    Carolina's Winegrape Cultivars*, vineyard survey and cultivar appendices.
