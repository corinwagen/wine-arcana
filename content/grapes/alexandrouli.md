# Aleksandrouli

Aleksandrouli, also written Alexandrouli, is a red grape of Racha and
neighbouring western Georgian districts. It is closely associated in the
cellar with [Mujuretuli](mujuretuli.md), its partner in the semi-sweet red
wine [Khvanchkara](../regions/khvanchkara.md). The same grape also supplies
dry wines, making sweetness
an important distinction between bottlings.

## Racha and ripening

The nursery Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes Aleksandrouli as a
late-ripening variety cultivated around Ambrolauri, Oni, and Tsageri.
Its technical profile records susceptibility to downy mildew and the
potential to make both dry and semi-sweet wines. Reaching sufficient
ripeness and preserving healthy fruit are therefore central vineyard
concerns.

The berry has dark skin but colourless pulp. As with most red wine grapes,
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) brings pigments from the skins into
the juice. The finished wine's colour and
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) depend partly on extraction, while the
amount of [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) changes its
perceived fullness and balance.

## Beyond a single style

Gonadze Vineyards in Ambrolauri illustrates the range within Racha.
Its profile on Georgia's official tourism directory lists Khvanchkara
alongside dry red wines, including one sold as Aleksandrouli. The detailed
description identifies that dry wine as a blend with Mujuretuli, a useful
reminder to check composition even when a grape name leads the label.[^1]

Comparing a dry blend with a semi-sweet Khvanchkara keeps both the regional
partnership and the cellar decision in view. During
[alcoholic fermentation](../concepts/alcoholic-fermentation.md), yeast
converts grape sugar into alcohol; the sugar left in the finished wine
is a separate property from its ripe-fruit aromas. Dry examples give
Aleksandrouli a contemporary role alongside Racha's established sweeter
style.

[^1]: Georgia Travel, “Gonadze Vineyards,” wine descriptions, accessed
    21 September 2026.
