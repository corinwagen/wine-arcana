# Kerner

Kerner is a German-bred white grape that combines pronounced fruit aromas
with useful acidity. Germany remains an important home, while the Alpine
vineyards of [Alto Adige](../regions/alto-adige.md) give it a distinct contemporary regional identity.
Its ability to ripen a generous crop and withstand winter cold helped its
spread; those advantages still require careful site and canopy choices.

## Breeding and cultivation

August Herold developed Kerner at Weinsberg in 1929 by crossing the red grape
Trollinger with [Riesling](riesling.md), according to the German Wine
Institute. Its name commemorates the physician and poet Justinus Kerner.[^1]

The institute describes good wood maturation and [winter hardiness](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md), but also
susceptibility to [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md) and substantial canopy work. Winter survival
and freedom from growing-season disease are different traits. So are resistance
to winter cold and protection of young shoots after spring budbreak.

German examples span dry, sweeter, and sparkling wines. Fruit can suggest
pear, citrus, or a light muscat-like note. The grape's
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) gives that perfume structure, with remaining
sugar modifying the impression of sharpness.

## Kerner in the mountains

Valle Isarco in Alto Adige provides a useful comparison with Germany. The
regional consortium profiles Köfererhof near Novacella, where Günther
Kerschbaumer describes harvesting Kerner in three passes: an earlier pick for
freshness, the principal crop, and a later selection for greater richness.[^2]

This makes the harvest decision concrete. An [elevated](../concepts/altitude.md)
site can slow development, yet the producer still chooses among different
balances of sugar, acid, and aroma. A wine's place and grape establish the
possibilities; harvest timing selects among them.
