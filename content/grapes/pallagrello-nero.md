# Pallagrello Nero

Pallagrello Nero is a dark-skinned grape of northern Campania, associated
especially with the hills around Caiazzo and Caserta. Alongside
[Casavecchia](casavecchia.md) and [Pallagrello Bianco](pallagrello-bianco.md),
it illustrates a contemporary regional revival built around separately
bottled local varieties.

## Ripeness and the intended wine

Alois describes a vigorous vine with small berries, capable of accumulating
substantial sugar while retaining relatively moderate acidity. For a grower,
those observations make harvest timing important: waiting for greater
ripeness also changes the balance between potential alcohol and freshness.
The published profile concerns the producer's experience with the grape,
rather than a fixed chemical specification for every vineyard.

The variety's current relevance is visible in continued production. A 2026
account of Vestini Campagnano identifies Pallagrello as an early focus of
the estate and lists Pallagrello Nero among its wines. This is a local
viticultural identity with an ongoing commercial life.

## What two bottlings reveal

Alois's Cunto and Murella show how vineyard and maturation choices can vary
within one producer's range. Cunto comes from the Audelino site on volcanic
ignimbrite. Murella comes from a higher calcareous vineyard at Pontelatone.
Both technical sheets describe October harvesting, fermentation with skin
contact in large wooden vessels and completed
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md).

Their subsequent schedules diverge. Cunto spends a year in large wood before
bottle maturation, while Murella receives two years in wood. The vessels
listed are much larger than a standard barrique. That matters because
[oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) encompasses different ratios
of wood surface to wine volume, as well as different ages and origins of wood.
A long period in a large vessel is not equivalent to the same time in a new
small barrel.

These examples place Pallagrello Nero among reds for which extraction and
development before release are deliberate parts of the style. They also
offer a useful way to explore northern Campania beyond its better-known
[Aglianico](aglianico.md), without treating all three local red grapes as
variations on one wine.
