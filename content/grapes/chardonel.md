# Chardonel

Chardonel is a white [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) bred from
[Seyval Blanc](seyval-blanc.md) and [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md). It joins
Chardonnay-related wine character with greater cold tolerance than that
parent, while still requiring a sufficiently long season to ripen well.
Missouri offers contemporary examples, including dry whites shaped by
barrel maturation and time on the lees.

## Breeding and regional adaptation

Cornell's breeding programme made the cross in 1953 and tested the
selection under names including NY 45010 and Geneva White 9. The release
bulletin, dated 1990, describes trials in New York, Michigan and Arkansas.
Cornell's current variety list gives 1991 as the release year.[^origin]

The trial history is useful because it shows differences between sites.
The original bulletin describes moderate winter hardiness at Geneva,
including occasional trunk injury, and recommends longer-season New York
sites. Increased hardiness relative to Chardonnay does not mean either
unlimited cold tolerance or especially early ripening.

## Vineyard priorities

Chardonel's ability to crop well needs to be considered alongside season
length. A vine that survives winter must still have time to mature its
fruit. This separates two questions sometimes compressed into the phrase
*suited to a cool climate*: [winter survival](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md)
and reliable ripening.

The Cornell bulletin also records susceptibility to powdery mildew, downy
mildew and bunch rot. Its hybrid pedigree consequently does not make
fungal-disease management optional. [Canopy work](../concepts/canopy-management.md)
and local disease pressure remain part of judging a site's suitability.

## Missouri and cellar choices

The Missouri Wine and Grape Board describes both stainless-steel and
oak-handled styles, with fruit associations including apple and pear.
Les Bourgeois Vineyards supplies a specific example: its estate-bottled
Chardonel is a dry white matured in French oak on its lees for five months.

That combination helps explain why the wine may invite comparison with
Chardonnay. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) and
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) influence aroma and texture in
addition to the fruit's own character. The producer's creamy description
does not, by itself, establish that malolactic fermentation occurred.
Nor should a barrel-aged bottling define the whole variety: a wine made
without oak gives a different view of the same grape.

[^origin]: Reisch et al., “Chardonel Grape,” bulletin 132, dated 1990;
    Cornell's current variety table lists release in 1991.
