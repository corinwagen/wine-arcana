# Manseng Noir

Manseng Noir is a red grape of the western Pyrenees that has returned to
commercial use in southwestern France after almost disappearing from its
vineyards. Its deep colour, retained acidity, and relatively late ripening
have drawn interest in Gascony, where growers are reconsidering varieties
for warmer seasons.

## Decline and return

Plantgrape's French figures show the scale of its contraction: 790 hectares
in 1958 had fallen to just 3 in 2008, before rising to 34 in 2018.[^1]
This is a revival from a very small base, rather than a return to its former
extent. Plantgrape also records Ferron in Spain and Espadeiron Mole in
Portugal as names for the variety.[^1]

Plaimont documents its renewed use in Côtes de Gascogne and a dedicated
bottling. The coöperative presents the grape as a less alcoholic and less
tannic counterpart to [Tannat](tannat.md). That describes its production
interest; the broader varietal record also includes astringent wines suitable
for blending and maturation.

## Establishing the vineyard

Manseng Noir is vigorous and has low fertility at the base of its shoots,
which favours long pruning. It can nevertheless carry a large crop.
Plantgrape describes fairly loose bunches and adaptation to varied soils.
These characteristics make the balance between leaf growth and fruit an
important part of cultivation.

The Institut français de la vigne et du vin (IFV) examined plant material
and vineyards in Saint Mont and Côtes de Gascogne through its MANOIR project.
It found substantial variation in bunch size and
fertility. Young plantings also showed growth disorders, and the researchers
recommended prioritizing roots and wood over early cropping. Detection of
Grapevine Pinot Gris Virus did not establish that it caused those symptoms.[^2]

## Colour, acidity, and the limits of a trial

The project's wines developed intense colour quickly while showing ripe
fruit, spice, acidity, and relatively supple tannin. Plantgrape's more
astringent general description and these softer trial wines illustrate the
range that plant material, crop, ripeness, and winemaking can produce.

The IFV identifies late ripening and acid retention as promising traits for
warmer conditions. Its field observations covered four plots over 2019–2022,
including disruptive frost and hail. Water stress was generally moderate
or absent, so the observations provide limited evidence for performance
under severe drought.[^2]

The same report calls Tannat a probable descendant of Manseng Noir, leaving
the relationship qualified rather than presenting a settled pedigree.[^2]
