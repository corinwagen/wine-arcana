# Zierfandler

Zierfandler is a white grape closely associated with Austria's [Thermenregion](../regions/thermenregion.md),
especially the country around Gumpoldskirchen. Its alternative name Spätrot
refers to the reddish colour that sun-exposed berries develop late in the
season. It is distinct from [Zinfandel](zinfandel.md).

## Identity and the vineyard

Austria's agriculture ministry identifies Roter Veltliner as one parent and
leaves the other unidentified, while noting its resemblance to Traminer.
That resemblance is not a complete genetic pedigree.[^1]

The regional wine organization emphasizes warm, favourably exposed sites and
susceptibility to botrytis. Bunch condition and autumn weather consequently
matter alongside the amount of sugar in the grapes. A grower pursuing greater
richness must consider how long the fruit can remain healthy on the vine.

## Structure and blending

Zierfandler often combines a full palate with noticeable
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Young wines can suggest citrus and pineapple;
older examples may move toward dried-fruit and floral impressions. These are
reference points, not a compulsory sequence of aromas.

Its traditional partner is [Rotgipfler](rotgipfler.md), and the two appear
together in Spätrot-Rotgipfler blends. A blend can draw on different ripeness
and structural contributions from the two grapes. Varietal Zierfandler makes
its own balance easier to examine. In either case,
[residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md), extraction, and maturation
can change the impression of weight and sharpness.
