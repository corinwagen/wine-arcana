# Forastera

Forastera is a white grape associated with [Ischia](../regions/ischia.md), off the coast of Campania.
It appears both in island blends and in wines bottled under its own name.
Alongside [Biancolella](biancolella.md), it offers a way to explore the island's
white wines through their component grapes.

The Lazio agricultural agency ARSIAL also documents Forastera in the Pontine
Islands, tracing its introduction there to settlers from Ischia in the late
nineteenth century. Its profile describes relatively loose bunches with thin
skins, a preference for loose, less fertile soils, and susceptibility to
powdery mildew and botrytis. That island connection extends the grape's
regional story beyond Ischia itself.[^1]

## Ischia's hillside whites

Casa D'Ambra's varietal Forastera comes from vineyards in Forio, Lacco Ameno
and Serrara Fontana. The producer records elevations of 400–500 metres,
low-trained vines and hand harvesting in September. Its wine is entirely
Forastera, fermented under temperature control and matured in stainless steel.
The estate describes stone-fruit aromas and a dry, almond-like finish.

These vineyards complicate a simple picture of a hot seaside wine region.
An island can contain substantial differences in
[altitude](../concepts/altitude.md), exposure and access. The height of a site
is part of its growing environment, while the resulting wine also reflects
when the grapes were picked and how they were handled.

Forastera also contributes to D'Ambra's Biancolella blend. Comparing a blend
with a separately bottled Forastera gives the drinker different information:
the former shows how local grapes work together, while the latter offers a
clearer view of one variety under that producer's conditions.

## Continuing investment in the grape

Costa delle Parracine supplies a second contemporary example. Its 2024
Forastera technical sheet identifies a vineyard at Forio, at 300 metres,
with Guyot-trained vines and four months of vinification and maturation in
steel. The estate takes its name from the dry-stone walls that support the
island's vineyard terraces.

Both examples emphasize a dry white without wood maturation. That choice
allows fruit, fermentation and the wine's acid balance to shape the immediate
impression. It also places Forastera within a living island wine culture:
continued cultivation, maintained terraces and named bottlings give a small
regional grape a reason to remain in the vineyard.

[^1]: ARSIAL, *Agrobiodiversità: vitigni*, “Forastera b.,” p. 33.
