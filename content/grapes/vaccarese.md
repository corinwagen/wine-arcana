# Vaccarèse

Vaccarèse is the local southern Rhône name for the dark-skinned grape that the
French varietal catalogue calls Brun argenté N. It is a minor variety in the
Rhône. Its clearest legal home is
[Châteauneuf-du-Pape](../regions/chateauneuf-du-pape.md), whose specification
lists
“brun argenté N (localement dénommé ‘vaccarèse’)” among the permitted grapes for
both red and white wines.[^1]

## Identity and recent history

Brun argenté is listed in France's official catalogue and is described by
Plantgrape as originating in south-eastern France. “Vaccarèse” is the name that
may be used in France for plant propagation material; the Châteauneuf-du-Pape
specification preserves the local wine name alongside the catalogue name.[^2]

The French area series recorded by Plantgrape shows a severe long-term
contraction: 70 hectares in 1958, 55 in 1968, 44 in 1979, 34 in 1988, 11 in
2000, and 10 in 2008. The endpoint is 12 hectares in 2018.[^2] Growers have also
worked to preserve planting material: a conservatory of about ten clones was
planted in the Côtes du Rhône in 2001, and Plantgrape says selection work was
under way when its record was prepared.

## Viticulture

Brun argenté is fertile and late-season. At the French reference collection it
reaches maturity about four and a half weeks after Chasselas. Its late cycle
makes exposure and season length important, so Plantgrape recommends short
pruning and relatively poor, hot, well-exposed
sites. The variety can also be susceptible to millerandage, in which berries in
a cluster develop unevenly. Plantgrape lists powdery mildew and grey rot as
important disease susceptibilities.[^2]

Crop level, water supply, exposure, and the season still influence whether its
fruit reaches a sound balance before autumn conditions intervene.

## Wine character

Plantgrape describes Brun argenté as making slightly or moderately coloured,
relatively low-alcohol wines with a complex profile that may include fruity and
spicy notes.[^2] In a blend, the finished wine also reflects the proportions,
vintage, and winemaking.

## Where it remains relevant

Vaccarèse's strongest documented regional association is the southern Rhône,
especially Châteauneuf-du-Pape. The current INAO product page identifies the
appellation's current specification and describes its list of 18 permitted
grapes, while the specification's section V supplies the exact Brun
argenté/Vaccarèse wording.[^1]
