# Vaccarèse

Vaccarèse is the local southern Rhône name for the dark-skinned grape that the
French varietal catalogue calls Brun argenté N. It is a minor variety rather
than one of the Rhône's main commercial grapes. Its clearest legal home is
Châteauneuf-du-Pape, whose specification
lists “brun argenté N (localement dénommé ‘vaccarèse’)” among the permitted
grapes for both red and white wines.[^1] That wording matters: Brun argenté and
Vaccarèse are names for the same variety in this context, not separate grapes.

## Identity and recent history

Brun argenté is listed in France's official catalogue and is described by
Plantgrape as originating in south-eastern France. “Vaccarèse” is the name that
may be used in France for plant propagation material; the Châteauneuf-du-Pape
specification preserves the local wine name alongside the catalogue name.[^2]
Using both names in the article therefore reflects an administrative and
regional distinction, not a biological disagreement.

The French area series recorded by Plantgrape shows a severe long-term
contraction: 70 hectares in 1958, 55 in 1968, 44 in 1979, 34 in 1988, 11 in
2000, and 10 in 2008. The endpoint is 12 hectares in 2018.[^2] That two-hectare
increase is worth recording, but it is too small and too old to establish a
recent revival on its own. There is evidence of an effort to preserve and
rebuild planting material: a conservatory of about ten clones was planted in
the Côtes du Rhône in 2001, and Plantgrape says selection work was under way
when its record was prepared. Those are signs of renewed attention, not proof
that the variety has regained a substantial place in vineyards or blends.

## Viticulture

Brun argenté is fertile and late-season. At the French reference collection it
reaches maturity about four and a half weeks after Chasselas, a comparative
measure rather than a universal harvest date. Its late cycle makes exposure and
season length important, so Plantgrape recommends short pruning and relatively
poor, hot, well-exposed sites. The variety can also be susceptible to
millerandage, in which berries in a cluster develop unevenly. Plantgrape lists
powdery mildew and grey rot as important disease susceptibilities.[^2]

Fertility does not guarantee a useful crop when a late variety is grown in a
poorly exposed site, and a warm region does not remove the risks of uneven
fruit set or fungal disease. Crop level, water supply, exposure, and the season
still influence whether its fruit reaches a sound balance before autumn
conditions intervene.

## Wine character and the limits of blend claims

Plantgrape describes Brun argenté as making slightly or moderately coloured,
relatively low-alcohol wines with a complex profile that may include fruity and
spicy notes.[^2] In a blend, the finished wine also reflects the proportions,
vintage, and winemaking, so this catalogue description should not be treated as
a fixed contribution from Vaccarèse.

## Where it remains relevant

Vaccarèse's strongest documented regional association is the southern Rhône,
especially Châteauneuf-du-Pape. The current INAO product page identifies the
appellation's current specification and describes its list of 18 permitted
grapes, while the specification's section V supplies the exact Brun
argenté/Vaccarèse wording.[^1] Its inclusion gives the variety a place in the
appellation's historical blending vocabulary, but legal permission should not
be mistaken for widespread planting or a required proportion. The available
area figures instead point to a very small French presence.
