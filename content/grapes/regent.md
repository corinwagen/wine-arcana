# Regent

Regent is a German-bred red grape selected for wine quality and resistance
to important fungal diseases. Its deeply coloured wines can have substantial
body and tannin, while earlier ripening makes it useful in places where a
longer-season red variety may struggle.

## Resistance and its limits

The Julius Kühn Institute records Regent as a 1967 crossing of Diana and
[Chambourcin](chambourcin.md). Its breeder account describes relatively loose bunches and
potential for red, [rosé](../styles/rose.md), and sparkling wines.[^1] Germany's Rheinhessen,
Pfalz, Baden, and [Franken](../regions/franken.md) are among its established regional settings.

The German Wine Institute describes good [winter hardiness](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md) alongside a risk
of poor fruit set in cold, windy locations. A site therefore has to support
flowering and ripening as well as winter survival. The institute also notes
susceptibility to root [phylloxera](../concepts/phylloxera.md): fungal-[disease resistance](../concepts/disease-resistance.md) does not remove
the reason for [grafting](../concepts/grafting.md) onto suitable rootstocks.

Disease tolerance reduces some pressures without guaranteeing a clean crop.
The breeder's account distinguishes the degree of resistance to individual
diseases. Weather, infection pressure, and vineyard management still affect
the outcome.

## Wine character

Cherry and dark-fruit impressions are common reference points. Extraction
and maturation determine how much of the available
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) enters the wine and how it feels. A deeply
coloured Regent can consequently range from a relatively approachable red
to a more structured wine. Its breeding history explains agricultural
possibilities; it does not prescribe sweetness, barrel use, or a single
finished style.

[^1]: Julius Kühn Institute, *New Grapevine Varieties*, 2022, Regent profile.
