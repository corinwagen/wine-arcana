# Limnio

Limnio is a dark-skinned Greek *Vitis vinifera* variety associated with
Lemnos and northern mainland Greece. On Lemnos it is also called Kalampaki
or Kalambaki. Its reds often have moderate colour, herbal and red-fruit
aromas, and a relatively supple structure, although ripe fruit can produce
substantial alcohol and body.

The grape's contemporary importance extends beyond its island association.
Halkidiki in Macedonia and parts of Thrace, including Maronia and Avdira,
provide both varietal wines and blends. These uses show different ways of
working with a red grape whose contribution need not depend on very dark
colour or forceful tannin.

## Names and historical claims

Regional accounts connect Limnio with the *Limnia* grape named in ancient
literature. The Winemakers of North Greece account specifically cites the
*Onomasticon* of Polydeuces.[^history] This is a traditional historical
identification. A familiar regional name in an ancient text cannot by
itself establish that the vine had the same genotype as today's Limnio.

The secure starting point for understanding the wine is its established
cultivation on Lemnos and in northern Greece, together with the practices
of growers working with it today.

## Varietal wine and blending

A lightly handled Limnio can emphasize fruit and herbs with moderate
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md). More extended extraction and barrel
maturation produce a fuller expression. Ripeness, the amount of skin and
seed material extracted, and the maturation vessel all affect how much
grip accompanies the wine's weight.

In Halkidiki's Slopes of Meliton tradition, Limnio joins
[Cabernet Sauvignon](cabernet-sauvignon.md) and
[Cabernet Franc](cabernet-franc.md). The blend combines its aromatic and
textural contribution with the structure and fruit of the Cabernets.
Varietal bottlings make Limnio's own contribution easier to examine.

Vourvoukeli's Limnio from Avdira is one documented example of a more
extracted varietal approach. The producer describes fermentation and
extended extraction in steel, followed by six months in French and
American oak, with [malolactic
fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md). Its moderate ruby
colour accompanies a full-bodied wine. This illustrates why colour alone
is an unreliable guide to either weight or cellar treatment.
