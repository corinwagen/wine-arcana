# Torrontés Riojano

Torrontés Riojano is an aromatic white *Vitis vinifera* grape associated with
Argentina, especially the [Calchaquí Valleys](../regions/calchaqui-valleys.md).
Its floral and muscat character can be pronounced even when the wine is dry.
The useful distinction is between perfume, which comes from the grape and
fermentation, and [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md), which
depends on how far fermentation proceeds.

## Identity and parentage

Foundation Plant Services identifies Torrontés Riojano as the offspring of
Criolla Chica, the Argentine name for [País](pais.md), and
[Muscat of Alexandria](moscatel-de-alejandria.md).[^parents] That parentage
places it in the group of grapes that developed from varieties brought to
the Americas, while helping explain its aromatic relationship with Muscat.

Argentina also recognizes Torrontés Sanjuanino and Torrontés Mendocino as
separate varieties. The national wine institute's account of DNA work
distinguishes all three.[^names] Torrontés Riojano is likewise distinct from
the grape using the Torrontés name in Galicia. This page therefore covers a
particular biological variety, rather than every white wine labelled
Torrontés.

The INV's provincial table for 2022 also records Riojano in Mendoza,
La Rioja, Salta, San Juan, and other provinces.[^distribution] Cafayate's
cultural association with the grape forms one part of that wider planting.

## Cafayate and the vineyard

Cafayate supplies an especially useful regional context. Its arid mountain
setting combines strong sun with opportunities to retain acidity through
cooler conditions. Vineyard water, fruit exposure, and harvest date determine
how those influences combine. Aromatic richness can coexist with a fresh
palate, but the region's elevation does not guarantee that balance.

El Porvenir's Finca El Retiro provides a documented example of *parral*
training, in which the canopy is carried overhead. The producer uses this
system for its Torrontés. The arrangement makes leaf cover and fruit shading
part of the discussion of aromatic white grapes under strong sunlight.
Different sites and crop levels require different canopy decisions.

In an April 2025 regional report, Nicolás Orsini places Torrontés Riojano
alongside an expanding red-wine identity in the Calchaquí Valleys. Its
established white-wine role remains useful when comparing the region's
Malbec, Cabernet Sauvignon, and Tannat with a wine whose most immediate
feature is perfume.

## Building freshness and texture

El Porvenir's Laborum Single Vineyard account describes combining separate
harvest stages, cool fermentation, lees work, and avoidance of
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md). These are
ways to manage aroma, acidity, and palate weight independently. Lees can add
texture without requiring sweetness, while retaining malic acid supplies a
different balance from completing malolactic fermentation.

The example also shows why an aromatic grape needs more than a list of
floral descriptors. Picking date establishes the starting relationship
between perfume, sugar, and acidity. Fermentation and maturation then decide
whether the finished wine feels light and direct or broader and more
textural. A dry Torrontés can smell sweet while remaining structurally a dry
white wine.
