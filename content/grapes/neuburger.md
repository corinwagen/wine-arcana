# Neuburger

Neuburger is an Austrian white grape whose wines often emphasize breadth,
gentle acidity, and restrained fruit. With maturation, a nutty character can
become more apparent. Its main reference points include the
[Wachau](../regions/wachau.md), [Thermenregion](../regions/thermenregion.md), and Leithaberg.

## Identity and growing conditions

Austria's agriculture ministry, in an account revised by Klosterneuburg
researcher Ferdinand Regner, identifies Roter Veltliner and
[Silvaner](silvaner.md) as its genetically established parents. Older accounts
that name [Pinot Blanc](pinot-blanc.md) preserve an earlier hypothesis.[^1] The familiar story
of cuttings rescued from the Danube belongs to the grape's origin tradition;
it should be distinguished from the evidence for parentage.

The ministry records compact bunches, susceptibility to botrytis, and berries
that can split after autumn rain. These traits make fruit condition important
to the harvest decision. More time on the vine may improve ripeness, while
wet weather can reduce the amount of healthy fruit available.

## Texture and maturation

Winzer Krems describes a contrast between relatively quiet young wines and
the almond-like character of mature examples. Its account also stresses
unreliable crops caused by flowering problems, frost, and rot. The grape's
wine potential and its agricultural convenience are separate questions.

Relatively mild [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) allows texture to be prominent.
Riper fruit, [lees contact](../concepts/lees-aging.md), and time in a large
wooden vessel can each change that texture. A nutty aroma alone cannot identify
whether a wine has been oaked: fruit development and maturation also alter
aroma. Neuburger is useful to compare with more sharply acidic Austrian whites
when producer and harvest conditions are known.

[^1]: Austrian agriculture ministry, “Neuburger,” varietal account revised
    by Ferdinand Regner, 2021; accessed 22 September 2026.
