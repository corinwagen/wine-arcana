# Fokiano

Fokiano is a red Greek wine grape associated especially with Ikaria in the
Aegean. It produces dry and sweet reds as well as
[rosé](../styles/rose.md), with colour often lighter than its ripe fruit
character might suggest. Its regional identity encompasses several cellar
traditions, so the grape name alone gives only part of the picture.

Wines of Greece also records Fokiano on Lipsi, Naxos, and Syros, with
scattered plantings in mainland Macedonia. Ikaria provides particularly
clear contemporary examples of varietal wines and contrasting maturation
choices.

## History and regional identity

Fokiano is sometimes associated with the Pramnian wine of ancient accounts.
Wines of Greece presents that connection as probable. It is best understood
as a historical association: an ancient wine name does not by itself
establish the identity of a surviving cultivar.

The more concrete way into the grape is through documented island wines.
Afianes on Ikaria makes Fokiano as both dry red and rosé, providing examples
that can be compared without assuming that every local wine follows a
single inherited recipe.

## Wine character and cellar choices

Fokiano's relatively pale reds can still carry ripe dark-fruit flavours and
substantial texture. Colour, [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md), and alcoholic
warmth are different properties, and their balance depends on the fruit and
the way it is handled. Sweet versions add another variable:
[residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) changes both flavour and the
sense of weight.

For its Icarus Black 2015, Afianes documents Fokiano from the Prof. Ilias
area of Ikaria, fermented in stainless steel and matured for twelve months
in French oak. The producer describes a warm, structured dry red. That
example places [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) within
Fokiano's contemporary repertoire; the barrel contribution belongs to the
wine's production history, alongside the grape's own fruit character.

Afianes's Icarus Dry Rosé Oak 2019 used Fokiano from Patissa and received
five months in French oak after fermentation in steel. The comparison with
its red wine is useful because rosé production changes skin extraction,
while maturation can still build texture and contribute spice. These are
documented choices for particular wines, not fixed requirements for
Fokiano. Fresh rosé, dry red, and sweet red remain distinct possibilities
within the variety's Aegean setting.
