# Pallagrello Bianco

Pallagrello Bianco is a white grape associated with Caserta and the hills of
northern Campania. Its modern varietal wines belong to the same local revival
that has brought [Pallagrello Nero](pallagrello-nero.md) and
[Casavecchia](casavecchia.md) to greater attention. For readers familiar with
[Fiano](fiano.md) and [Greco](greco.md), it adds another distinct point of
entry into Campanian white wine.

## A local variety made visible

Alois describes small, compact bunches and rounded berries, and a fruity
white-wine expression when the grape is handled in steel. Contemporary
bottlings show that producers also use wood and extended lees contact to
develop a more substantial style.

The revival has an established commercial history. A 2026 account of Vestini
Campagnano records its early focus on Pallagrello and Casavecchia and lists
both its regular Pallagrello Bianco and Le Ortole. The significance is the
continued cultivation and separate presentation of local grapes, allowing
drinkers to encounter identities that a general Campanian blend can conceal.

## Two vineyards and cellar approaches

Alois's Caiatì and Morrone provide a particularly useful comparison. Caiatì
comes from the Audelino vineyard, whose technical sheet records volcanic
ignimbrite soils and an elevation of 148 metres. It is fermented and matured
in steel. That method gives a direct view of the producer's fruit-led style
without introducing wood-derived aromas.

Morrone comes from a higher, calcareous site near Pontelatone, recorded at
350 metres. The producer describes a divided cellar treatment: two-thirds
in steel and one-third in wood, with fine-lees contact, followed by further
bottle maturation.[^1] The resulting comparison involves both different
vineyard material and a different maturation route.

That distinction matters when discussing [altitude](../concepts/altitude.md)
or soil. A comparison between these finished wines cannot isolate the effect
of elevation, because the cellar treatment changes too.
[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can modify texture, while wood
provides another environment for development. The producer's choices explain
why Pallagrello Bianco need not have the same weight or expression in every
bottling.

[^1]: Alois, “Caiatì” and “Morrone,” vineyard elevations, soils and cellar
    methods as published in the producer's technical sheets.
