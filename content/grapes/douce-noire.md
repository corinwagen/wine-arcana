# Douce Noire

Douce Noire is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape from Savoie, known in
Argentina as Bonarda and in California as Charbono. Corbeau is the name used
in the French varietal catalogue. Its modern significance is especially
Argentine, where it supports both varietal red wines and blends.

The naming requires care. *Bonarda* is also used for other grapes in Italy,
so a shared name does not establish a shared cultivar. This page brings
Argentine Bonarda and Californian Charbono together through the documented
identity with Corbeau.

## Identity and historical home

Foundation Plant Services reports that DNA from Californian Charbono matches
French Corbeau, and that molecular work identifies Argentine Bonarda with
the same variety.[^identity] The record distinguishes California's grape from
Italian vines called Charbono, including [Dolcetto](dolcetto.md).

Plantgrape identifies Savoie as Corbeau's origin and recognizes Douce noire
as a name for French propagation material. That historical home is useful
for understanding the vine, even though the scale of its present Argentine
role is much greater.

## Viticulture and wine character

In French reference conditions, Plantgrape describes a fairly vigorous vine
with a semi-upright habit, suited to trellising and long pruning. It records
mid-season maturity relative to Chasselas. These observations describe the
vine under specified conditions, rather than fixing a harvest date for every
Argentine vineyard.

The same account describes well-coloured wines with relatively modest acidity
and tannin, and low-to-moderate sugar accumulation. Colour and astringency
come from different properties of the skins and seeds, so a dark wine need
not be especially tannic. Crop, ripeness, and extraction still alter the
result. Plantgrape also notes greater susceptibility to
[downy mildew](../concepts/downy-mildew.md) and anthracnose than to powdery
mildew or grey rot.

## Argentina's Bonarda

The INV's August 2026 account identifies Bonarda as Argentina's second most
planted red variety in 2025, concentrated especially in
[Mendoza](../regions/mendoza.md), San Juan, and La Rioja.[^plantings]
The grape's sustained place in these warm, dry vineyards makes it a useful
counterpart to [Malbec](malbec.md), with its own range of farming and cellar
approaches.

Altos Las Hormigas's Colonia Las Liebres Bonarda 2024 gives a specific modern
example. The producer combined fruit from overhead-trained vines in Luján de
Cuyo with fruit from Paraje Altamira in the [Uco Valley](../regions/uco-valley.md).
It fermented in steel with a small proportion of whole bunches and matured
the wine in concrete without wood. That sequence allows a red wine's fruit
and texture to develop without adding new-oak flavour.

The producer's account of 2024 also describes different ripening patterns
between its source areas during a season that included a prolonged heatwave.
Bonarda's Argentine context therefore includes regional and seasonal
variation, alongside its ability to serve as a familiar local red grape.

## California and the shared vine

Foundation Plant Services maintains Charbono material collected from Napa
and the former experimental vineyard near Jackson. These records document
another part of the variety's living history. They establish the vine's
identity and preservation; they do not imply that a California Charbono and
a Mendoza Bonarda will taste alike. Site, harvest, and
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) remain consequential under either name.
