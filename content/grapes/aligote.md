# Aligoté

Aligoté is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety most closely associated with
Burgundy. Its wines are defined by lively [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), which can make them direct
and refreshing. The grape is particularly important in Bouzeron, the only
Burgundian village appellation made exclusively from Aligoté.

## History

Aligoté's history is long in Burgundy but not securely ancient. Bourgogne Wines
places its emergence there in the seventeenth century. Published genetic
analyses indicate that it resulted from a cross between Pinot and Gouais blanc,
the same two lineages involved in [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md).[^1]

For much of its modern history, Aligoté was compared with Chardonnay, which
occupied more of Burgundy's most valued sites. Bourgogne Wines records that
Aligoté was sometimes planted alongside Chardonnay in prestigious vineyards
before phylloxera, and that the regional *Bourgogne Aligoté* appellation was
created in 1937. These facts explain why the grape can be both historically
Burgundian and less visible in discussions of Burgundy's classified white
wines.

Bouzeron provides a more specific history of esteem. INAO says the local wine
has had a reputation for several centuries and reports that, in the nineteenth
century, it sold for prices comparable to Pouilly-Fuissé and the third cuvées
of Meursault.[^2] The regional “Aligoté de Bouzeron” appellation became the
communal AOP Bouzeron in 1998.

## Viticulture

Plantgrape describes Aligoté as fertile and well suited to northern areas. It
buds one day after Chasselas and reaches first-period maturity about a week and
a half after it, making it an early-ripening variety by that reference. It has
small bunches and berries; its fertility makes crop level an important variable.
The variety is relatively little affected by powdery mildew but is
fairly susceptible to downy mildew and grey rot; it also often shows
millerandage, in which berries within a bunch develop unevenly.[^1]

Plantgrape's French surface series records 1,883 hectares in 1958, 1,188 in
1979, and 1,974 in 2018.[^1]

## Wine character and site

Plantgrape calls Aligoté wines lively and nervy, while Bourgogne Wines
emphasizes freshness and fruity acidity. The structure is the most dependable
generalization: Aligoté commonly retains a marked, mouthwatering acidity, but
aromatic intensity and typicity vary with terroir, vintage, and production
level. Bourgogne Wines describes a range that can include white-fleshed fruit,
white flowers, lemon, hazelnut, and saline impressions.

Bouzeron is the clearest example of site being part of the grape's identity.
The appellation covers Bouzeron and Chassey-le-Camp in the Côte Chalonnaise,
where the vines grow on marly limestone slopes. INAO presents the appellation
as a particularly good fit between Aligoté's characteristics and those
slopes.[^2] Contemporary reporting also describes renewed interest in hillside
Aligoté and in old plantings, alongside the formation of Les Aligoteurs, a
producer-and-wine-lover group advocating for the variety.[^3]

## Where it is grown

Burgundy remains Aligoté's principal home. Bourgogne Wines reports about 2,000
hectares in France, more than 1,800 of them in Burgundy, extending from the
[Chablis](../regions/chablis.md) area to the Mâconnais; the figure belongs to that source's published
regional overview rather than to the dated Plantgrape series.[^4] Bourgogne
Aligoté is the broad regional expression, while Bouzeron is the distinctive
communal appellation devoted solely to the grape.

## In the cellar

Bourgogne Wines says some examples can be held for a few years, during which
varietal aromas may give way to more site-derived character.[^4] Producers have
traditionally used brief stainless-steel ageing for many Bourgogne Aligoté wines,
while some now use oak, including new wood. Those choices can broaden or mark
the wine.
