# Assyrtiko

Assyrtiko is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape closely associated with the Greek
island of [Santorini](../regions/santorini.md). It can accumulate substantial
sugar while retaining firm acidity in hot, dry conditions, producing dry wines
with both weight and a taut palate. It also contributes to sweet wines made
from sun-dried grapes.

The grape has spread through mainland Greece and to vineyards abroad.
Its restrained perfume and substantial texture are particularly apparent
on Santorini. Mainland examples often show more conspicuous citrus,
stone-fruit, and floral aromas.

## Acidity under heat and wind

Assyrtiko's acid retention gives growers scope to harvest ripe fruit
without necessarily producing a soft wine. Fermenting that fruit to
dryness can yield considerable alcohol, while the remaining
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) keeps the palate incisive. Harvest timing
still determines how much sugar, acid, and aroma development reaches the
press.

On Santorini, growers commonly train vines into low baskets called
*kouloures*, which shelter the fruit from wind and intense exposure.
A two-season study of Assyrtiko near Oia compared this system with upright,
trellised vines. The basket-trained vines maintained a less stressed water
status and suffered less damage from strong wind and a heatwave. During
hot weather, their fruit was less exposed to high midday temperatures.[^1]
The vineyards also differed in planting density, so the comparison concerns
the two vineyard systems together.

## Mainland vineyards and new plantings

In northern Greece, cooler nights and different growing conditions give
Assyrtiko another range of expression. Alpha Estate's Aghia Kiriaki block
in Amyndeon provides a concrete example: its trellised vines grow at about
535 metres in sandy clay loam over limestone conglomerates. The producer
describes fresh summer nights and gives citrus, flowers, and white
stone fruit as its wine's aromatic reference points.

Alpha ferments this wine in steel and keeps it on fine lees for about
eight months, with stirring. That [lees contact](../concepts/lees-aging.md)
contributes texture alongside the grape's own structure. Short skin contact
before pressing is another part of the producer's approach.

In Australia's Clare Valley, Jim Barry propagated cuttings obtained from
Santorini and planted Assyrtiko at Lodge Hill in 2012. Most of its vines
are trellised, with a small number trained as baskets. This planting places
the grape in a different rainfall regime and vineyard design from its
island home.

## Dry, mature, and sweet wines

Dry Assyrtiko can mature in steel or wood. Oak contributes aromas and
changes the wine's texture, while concentrated examples can develop
honeyed and riper-fruit impressions with bottle age. The initially taut
acidity can remain prominent as those aromas evolve.

For sweet wines such as Santorini's Vinsanto, drying grapes concentrates
the juice before fermentation. Assyrtiko's acidity supplies freshness
alongside the resulting sugar and weight. The dried-fruit character and
subsequent maturation give these wines a different aromatic course from
a young dry bottling.
