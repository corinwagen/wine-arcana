# Aglianico

Aglianico is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape central to the red wines
of Campania and Basilicata. Its main reference points include
[Taurasi](../regions/taurasi.md),
Taburno, and the Vulture area. The wines often combine substantial
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) with freshness, giving producers material
for both firm, ageworthy reds and fruitier wines released younger.

Aglianico's long growing season helps explain its strong attachment to
particular southern Italian sites. It buds early and ripens late. Growers
need enough warmth to finish ripening, together with healthy fruit and a
working canopy well into autumn.

## Completing the season

Aglianico grows vigorously, and excessive foliage can shade the bunches.
In cooler parts of its range, Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo recommends training
and green pruning that help the fruit ripen and reduce bunch-rot pressure.
Wet conditions increase susceptibility to grey rot and sour rot. Prolonged
drought can also stress the vine.

Elevation, water supply, and crop size interact. A large crop on a late site
requires the vine to support more fruit through a shortening season. An
initial year of Vulture observations reported by the regional agricultural
agency, ALSIA, found that altitude, soil texture, rootstock, and crop load
all influenced the harvested grapes. Vigorous rootstocks could aggravate
imbalance where crops were large and ripening conditions difficult.[^1]

## Taurasi and Vulture

Taurasi occupies hills and foothills along the Calore valley in the
province of Avellino. Its soils include varying mixtures of clay, sand,
and volcanic material. Hillside exposure and local temperatures affect how
the late-ripening grape completes its season within this relatively compact
area.

The Taurasi specification requires at least 85% Aglianico and a minimum
three years of maturation, including one in wood. Riserva requires four
years, including eighteen months in wood. These periods run from
1 December of the harvest year.[^2] The time in the cellar gives tannins
and aromas an opportunity to change before release.

Across the regional border in Basilicata, Aglianico del Vulture grows in
vineyards around Monte Vulture. Vineyard observations there show why the
volcanic setting needs to be understood alongside elevation, the soil's
physical properties, and farming. The nursery also distinguishes selections
associated with Taurasi, Taburno, and Vulture, with differences in vigour,
fruit, and wine composition.

## Structure and maturation

Aglianico can yield deeply coloured wines with red-fruit and floral
impressions. Skin contact during fermentation extracts tannin as well as
pigment, so extraction decisions help determine how imposing the young
wine feels. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) changes texture
and aroma according to the vessel and duration chosen.

Clonal technical descriptions include selections suited to younger,
fruitier wines and others suited to prolonged maturation. Ripe fruit,
extraction, and the intended drinking age therefore belong together in
cellar decisions. The name Aglianico encompasses a wider range than the
long-matured wines of Taurasi alone.
