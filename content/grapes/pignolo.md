# Pignolo

Pignolo is a red grape of [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](../regions/friuli.md), especially the hills around
Rosazzo, Buttrio, and Premariacco. It is associated with deeply structured
wines whose firm [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) often leads producers to
allow substantial maturation before release.

## Growing a demanding local red

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes small berries with thick dark skins,
late ripening, and low but relatively consistent yields. The vine can grow
vigorously even while producing little fruit. Growers therefore need to
manage vegetative growth as well as crop size: a small yield does not by
itself ensure an open, well-exposed canopy.

The same nursery identifies susceptibility to [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md) and recommends
careful work on the green canopy. Its profile helps explain why maintaining
Pignolo requires sustained vineyard attention. Contemporary production
continues a local recovery centered on the Colli Orientali, where the
consortium includes it among the area's distinctive red varieties.

## Structure and maturation

Young Pignolo can feel compact and strongly tannic. Maturation changes the
relationship between that structure and its fruit, with the outcome depending
on ripeness, extraction, and the producer's use of wood. Its reputation for
[bottle aging](../concepts/bottle-aging.md) should be understood through those
choices: substantial tannin supplies structure, while a successful mature
wine also needs sufficient fruit and balance.

Its local importance lies in this sustained commitment to an individual
Friulian red wine style. It offers a useful contrast with the region's
international varieties, including [Merlot](merlot.md), without requiring
a claim that one expresses the place more authentically than the other.
