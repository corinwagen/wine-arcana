# Pignoletto

Pignoletto is a white grape with several regional identities. Around Bologna
it is closely associated with sparkling and lightly sparkling wine; around
Rimini it is known as Rebola; in [Umbria](../regions/umbria.md), Grechetto di
Todi is an important name for still whites. These traditions belong to the
same variety, with different growing conditions and cellar choices shaping
the wines.

Its alternative name Grechetto Gentile needs particular care. This is a
different grape from [Grechetto di Orvieto](grechetto-di-orvieto.md), even
though both can appear in discussions of Umbrian Grechetto. The biological
identity, the local grape name, and the appellation on the bottle are three
separate pieces of information.

## Names and identity

The Italian Vitis Database records genetic identification of Pignoletto,
Grechetto di Todi, and Rebola Riminese as the same variety. It lists Grechetto
Gentile and Alionzina among the registered synonyms.[^identity] Nursery
references also place the Umbrian G5 selection under Pignoletto, alongside
selections used in Emilia-Romagna.

A shared identity does not make every planting identical. Selection preserves
variation within a grape, while soil, water supply, harvest date, and the
intended wine influence how growers manage it. The persistent local names
also record separate regional wine cultures.

## Growing the grape

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes a vine of moderate vigour, with small,
compact bunches, thick-skinned berries, and moderately late ripening. Guyot
training is recommended, although spur-pruned cordons are also used. Its
technical account identifies drought susceptibility, making water supply
important when choosing a site and managing summer growth.

That sensitivity matters in comparing hillside vineyards with different soil
depths and capacities to retain water. A slope's exposure and drainage can be
helpful while a shallow rooting zone still limits the water available during
a dry season. [Canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) must balance
fruit exposure with the leaves needed to support ripening.

Rauscedo's clonal descriptions emphasize acidity and floral or fruit aromas,
with suitability for both still and sparkling production. In a sparkling
base wine, retained [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) helps maintain freshness
through a second fermentation and subsequent maturation. A still wine can
instead place more emphasis on texture and the character of the harvested
fruit.

## Bologna and Emilia-Romagna

The hills around Bologna give Pignoletto a longstanding regional focus.
Colli Bolognesi Pignoletto encompasses lightly sparkling *frizzante*, more
fully sparkling *spumante*, and still wines. The regional consortium also
emphasizes the still Classico Superiore as a style intended for maturation.
The grape's connection with bubbles therefore describes one important use,
rather than a limit on its possibilities.

The name also appears within more than one appellation. The broader
Emilia-Romagna DOC's November 2025 specification names
Pignoletto wines in still, frizzante, spumante, passito, and late-harvest forms
and requires at least 85% of the grape.[^rules] Colli Bolognesi Pignoletto DOCG
is a separate denomination. This article uses Pignoletto as the grape name,
while the full appellation identifies the wine's geographical and production
framework.

Folicello's 2025 Pignoletto Emilia-Romagna DOC gives a documented example of
the tank method, often called Charmat. The producer presses gently and makes
the wine sparkling in a pressure vessel. Trapping the carbon dioxide produced
by fermentation creates the bubbles. This differs from the
[traditional method](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md), in which
the second fermentation takes place inside the bottle.

## Grechetto di Todi in Umbria

Todi provides a useful still-wine comparison. Roccafiore's Fiorfiore is made
from Grechetto di Todi, identified by the producer as the G5 selection. Its
published method begins fermentation in temperature-controlled steel, then
completes it in the vessels used for maturation. The wine spends twelve
months in amphorae and large Slavonian oak on its fine lees, without
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md).

These choices build texture through [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md)
and vessel contact while retaining malic acid. The result follows a different
cellar path from tank-fermented frizzante. Comparing the two regional uses
helps explain how one grape can support both a lively sparkling wine and a
more substantial still white.
