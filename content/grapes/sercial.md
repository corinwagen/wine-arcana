# Sercial

Sercial is a Portuguese white grape best known for the dry end of
[Madeira](../styles/madeira.md)'s fortified-wine tradition. Its marked acidity
helps give those wines a firm, refreshing structure despite their alcohol and
retained sugar. On the Portuguese mainland, Esgana Cão is another name for the
same variety; an official European Union publication for Vinho Verde lists
the pairing as “Sercial - Esgana-Cão.”[^1]

## The grape on Madeira

The Instituto do Vinho, do Bordado e do Artesanato da Madeira (IVBAM), the
island's regulatory institute, describes small, compact, conical bunches with
greenish-yellow berries. It emphasizes both high acidity and astringency in
its cultivar account. Sercial grows on the island's northern side and in
Câmara de Lobos, with plantings reaching higher elevations on the southern
coast than in the north.[^2]

## What dry means here

Sercial's association with dry Madeira reflects a production tradition.
[Fortification](../concepts/fortification.md) interrupts fermentation at the
producer's chosen point; letting yeast consume more sugar first gives a drier
result. The grape's acidity then contributes to the balance of the finished
wine.

Madeira uses its own sweetness vocabulary. IVBAM's indicative sugar figures
allow appreciable [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) in wines
called dry, and the institute explicitly presents these figures as guidance.
A Sercial Madeira can consequently contain sugar while tasting relatively dry
within the island's range.[^3] Acidity and sweetness are separate sensations
that modify one another's prominence.

## Maturation and the bottle

A Sercial Madeira also reflects the island's distinctive maturation methods.
In *canteiro*, wine ages in casks in warm parts of the cellar, developing
through heat and [oxidation](../concepts/oxidation.md). Other Madeiras undergo
controlled heating through *estufagem*. These processes add a major source of
developed aroma beyond the starting grape.[^4]

Age indications and harvest dates provide a further distinction between
bottles. Sercial identifies the variety, while a blend's age indication or a
harvest-dated designation identifies a different part of the wine's history.
