# Erbaluce

Erbaluce is a white grape of northern Piedmont, especially the Canavese
around Caluso. It supports three distinct wine styles: dry still white,
[traditional-method sparkling wine](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md), and sweet passito made from [dried grapes](../concepts/appassimento.md).
Pronounced freshness connects them, while the handling of the fruit and wine
creates considerable differences in weight and texture.

## Place and expression

The Canavese includes the glacially formed morainic hills around Ivrea.
Lakes, Alpine air, and varied exposures shape the local growing environment.
This is a different geographical setting from the Langhe hills associated
with [Barolo](../regions/barolo.md), farther south in Piedmont.

Dry Erbaluce commonly combines restrained floral fruit with firm
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and a lightly bitter finish. Some examples
develop greater breadth with bottle age. In sparkling wines, a second
fermentation in bottle and subsequent [lees aging](../concepts/lees-aging.md)
add another source of texture to the grape's freshness.

For Caluso passito, producers select sound bunches and dry them in ventilated
rooms before pressing. The consortium describes both racks and hanging
bunches. Concentrating the grapes supplies a richer must, from which producers
make sweet wines with dried-fruit and honeyed associations. Their retained
acidity helps balance that concentration.

Comparing the three styles is more informative than assigning Erbaluce a
single level of body or sweetness. A dry white, a sparkling wine, and a
passito share a grape and regional home while expressing very different
production decisions.
