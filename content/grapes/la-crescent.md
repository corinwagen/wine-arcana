# La Crescent

La Crescent is an aromatic white grape from the University of Minnesota's
cold-climate breeding programme. Its apricot and citrus-like perfume and
pronounced acidity often suit off-dry or sweet wines. Minnesota and the
northern United States provide its main frame of reference, with Vermont
offering a useful contemporary comparison.

## Fruit, foliage, and winter

The breeder's account describes vigorous vines, early budbreak, and berries
that can fall from the bunch as they ripen. Picking time therefore involves
both wine composition and the risk of losing fruit. The berries can resist
splitting in wet conditions, but [downy mildew](../concepts/downy-mildew.md) remains an important concern.

Healthy foliage helps the vine prepare for winter. The university connects
late-season disease management with subsequent cold survival, showing why
hardiness depends partly on the preceding growing season. It cannot be
reduced to a single temperature printed beside a variety's name.

## Balancing perfume and acidity

[Residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) can soften the impression of
La Crescent's [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Lincoln Peak's 2024 bottling
in Vermont provides a documented example. The estate combined fruit given
about a day of skin contact with immediately pressed fruit, fermented in
stainless steel, and stopped fermentation by chilling when it liked the
balance. Its technical sheet records measurable remaining sugar.[^1]

The vineyard account also describes vigorous growth on the Champlain
Valley's silty clay soils. The resulting wine connects a specific site,
canopy, and fermentation decision with the grape's aromatic potential.

[^1]: Lincoln Peak Vineyard, “2024 La Crescent ~ Release Notes,”
    22 May 2025.
