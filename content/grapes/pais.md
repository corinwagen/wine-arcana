# País

País is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape whose regional names connect
Chile's older vineyards with California's Mission grape and Argentina's
Criolla Chica. Listán Prieto is its Spanish name. The same cultivar has
acquired different roles through its movement across the Americas.

Its contemporary relevance includes Chilean heritage vineyards, renewed
interest in local wines, and the conservation of surviving California
plantings. Understanding País means separating that history from the farming
and cellar choices that shape a particular bottle.

## Identity and movement

Foundation Plant Services at the University of California, Davis records País,
Criolla Chica, and Listán Prieto among Mission's synonyms. Its account cites
DNA analysis published in 2007 establishing Mission's identity with Listán
Prieto.[^identity] The Spanish connection supplies biological evidence behind
a history of vines carried into the Americas through colonial and mission
networks.

[Listán Negro](listan-negro.md) is a different cultivar. The similar names
have caused confusion, but Listán Prieto's identity should be kept separate
when reading Canary Island or American vineyard records. País also has a
family connection with Argentina's whites: Foundation Plant Services records
[Torrontés Riojano](torrontes-riojano.md) as a cross of Criolla Chica and
[Muscat of Alexandria](moscatel-de-alejandria.md).[^family]

## Chilean vineyards and contemporary uses

In [Itata Valley](../regions/itata-valley.md), País forms part of a farming
tradition alongside [Cinsault](cinsault.md) and Muscat of Alexandria. INIA's
regional work treats these varieties as agricultural resources whose
commercial future can benefit from research into sites and products.

One documented experiment revisited *Terciopelo*, a red blend. INIA fermented
País and Cinsault separately before assembling them, using fruit from several
Itata locations. This illustrates a role for País beyond a varietal label:
a grower can preserve the grape within a blend whose components have different
properties and are handled separately.

[Maule](../regions/maule-valley.md) supplies another contemporary context.
Bouchon's País Salvaje Blanco
2023 came from vines growing among native woodland at Mingre. The producer
describes hand harvesting with ladders, vertical pressing, and fermentation
of the juice with native yeasts. This unusual planting is a specific example,
not a description of ordinary País vineyards.

## Skin contact and wine style

País can supply red wine or, when its juice is separated from the dark skins,
a much paler wine. [Pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) and
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) determine how much colour and skin
material enters the fermenting juice. Bouchon's documented white production
and INIA's red blend make that distinction tangible. Their wines begin with
the same grape while using it in different ways.

California adds a conservation context under the name Mission. Foundation
Plant Services maintains material collected from several older vineyards,
including a selection donated by Gypsy Canyon's Deborah Hall from Santa
Barbara County. The record connects surviving vines with propagation and
restoration work, showing how a historically displaced grape can continue
to matter through living plant material.
