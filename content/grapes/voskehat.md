# Voskehat

Voskehat is an old white wine grape strongly associated with Armenia.
It supports a broad range of wines, from fragrant, textured dry whites
to sparkling blends and historically sweet fortified styles. Modern
[Vayots Dzor](../regions/vayots-dzor.md) examples make the relationship between mountain fruit and
cellar choices especially clear.

The University of California, Davis grape registry lists Voskeat as its
principal name and Voskehat, Khardji, and Kharji among its synonyms. Its
commentary suggests a western Armenian origin and also records the grape
in Azerbaijan under the name Kharji.[^1]

## Zulal and still Voskehat

Zulal's 2019 Voskehat offers a specific view of the grape in Vayots Dzor.
Importer Storica describes fruit from a south-facing plot in Aghavnadzor,
at an [altitude](../concepts/altitude.md) of about 1,400 metres, with
limestone and volcanic material in the soil.
The wine was made entirely from Voskehat, fermented in stainless steel,
and bottled from tank.[^2]

The importer describes peach-blossom and apricot aromas and emphasizes
texture. Fermentation and maturation in tank allow those characteristics
to be considered without an added layer of oak flavour. A substantial
white wine can derive its weight from ripe grapes and fermentation even
when it has had no barrel maturation.

Yacoubian-Hobbs also grows Voskehat in Vayots Dzor and describes its
thick-skinned berries. Skin thickness is a vineyard characteristic;
extracting compounds from those skins is a separate winemaking choice.
A pressed white and a [skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md)
can therefore differ considerably even when made from the same grape.

## Sparkling wine and the harvest

Keush sources fruit from Khachik's high vineyards and uses Voskehat with
Khatouni for traditional-method sparkling wine. Its Origins bottling is
listed as 60% Voskehat and 40% Khatouni. The blend places Voskehat within
an active Armenian sparkling-wine tradition as well as the growing range
of varietal still whites.

Grapes for a sparkling base wine are selected for a balance that can
support a second fermentation and later
[lees aging](../concepts/lees-aging.md). The intended style changes the
harvest decision: preserving [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) for sparkling
wine and pursuing fuller fruit for a still white place different demands
on the same variety.
