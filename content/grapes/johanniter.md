# Johanniter

Johanniter is a white grape bred at Freiburg for improved resistance to
fungal diseases. It often combines a restrained fruit aroma with lively
acidity. Its use in the Netherlands shows how German breeding work has
become part of a developing northern European wine culture.

## Breeding and vineyard behaviour

The State Viticultural Institute in Freiburg identifies Riesling as one
parent, with Seyve Villard 12-481, [Pinot Gris](pinot-gris.md), and Chasselas in the other
side of its pedigree.[^1] Its profile describes upright growth, compact
bunches, and moderate resistance to [downy mildew](../concepts/downy-mildew.md), [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md), and
botrytis. That last distinction matters: the word *resistant* describes a
reduction in susceptibility, with different results for different pathogens.

The institute describes peach-like fruit and a style that can recall
[Riesling](riesling.md). The comparison is useful for aroma and
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), while harvest timing and fermentation
still determine the individual wine's balance.

## A Dutch example

Avendorp's Johanniter 2023 is documented as entirely varietal. The same
West Frisian producer uses Johanniter in its sparkling Parel wines; the
2025 version brings together fruit from Avendorp and nearby Saalhof.[^2]
These bottlings provide specific examples of both still and sparkling uses.

For a sparkling base, acidity has a different role from that in a still
white: it must support the finished wine after the choices involved in
making and finishing the sparkling wine. A grape's regional usefulness
can therefore extend beyond one familiar style.
