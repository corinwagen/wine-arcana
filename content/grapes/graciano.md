# Graciano

Graciano is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* variety especially associated with
[Rioja](../regions/rioja.md). It contributes colour, aroma, tannin, and
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) to blends with
[Tempranillo](tempranillo.md), while varietal bottlings make those qualities
more directly visible. Its late ripening helps explain both its historical
minority role and renewed interest in warmer growing conditions.

The names Tintilla de Rota in southern Spain, Tinta Miúda in Portugal, and
Morrastel in France are associated with the same variety.[^names] A bottle
using one of those names belongs to a different regional tradition, even
when the vine's identity is shared.

## Ripening and wine structure

Rioja's regulatory council describes Graciano as late-ripening and relatively
low in fertility. Completing its season requires sufficient warmth and time;
the intended harvest must also account for autumn weather and fruit health.
The council identifies its acidity and phenolic content as important reasons
for its use in wines intended for maturation.

Those contributions help explain its blending role. A smaller Graciano
component can change the balance of a Tempranillo-led wine without becoming
the dominant grape. Varietal wine gives the winemaker a different task:
bringing acidity, extraction, fruit ripeness, and tannin into proportion
within Graciano itself. Deep colour is one feature of the grape, rather than
a measure of how firmly or gently the wine was made.

Viñedos Real Rubio's 2018 organic Crianza provides a documented Rioja blend
of Tempranillo and Graciano. The grapes were destemmed before fermentation,
and the wine matured in French and then American oak. That sequence shows
how grape blending and [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md)
operate together: the companion variety supplies grape-derived structure,
while wood and time further change the finished wine.

## A contemporary home in Lodi

[Lodi](../regions/lodi.md), California, gives Graciano a sustained varietal
context outside its historical homes. Markus and Liz Bokisch planted it
alongside Albariño and Tempranillo in the late 1990s, and released Spanish-variety
wines including Graciano in 2001. Their continuing regional program connects
Lodi's warm growing season with deliberately selected Iberian plant material.

Randy Caparoso's account of Lodi's vineyard wines places Bokisch's Las Cerezas
planting in the sandy eastern part of the Mokelumne River district. Graciano
grows there alongside Albariño and Tempranillo. The example connects a
Spanish-variety program to a specific Californian growing place, whose soils
and coastal-air access differ from the grape's Rioja settings.

## Climate interest and clonal variation

A 2025 Rioja study compared commercial Graciano and Tempranillo clones and
examined 13 additional Graciano selections over the 2020–2022 seasons.
The researchers found traits of interest under warming conditions, including
later phenology and relatively high must acidity, alongside meaningful
variation among Graciano selections.[^clones]

This supports interest in a local minority grape and in diversity within
that grape. It is not a forecast that Graciano will succeed at every warmer
site. The trial concerned a particular vineyard, plant material, and
management regime, including irrigation after véraison. Sunburn, available
water, crop balance, and disease remain practical constraints.
