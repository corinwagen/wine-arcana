# Rotgipfler

Rotgipfler is a white Austrian grape concentrated in the [Thermenregion](../regions/thermenregion.md) south
of Vienna. Its wines can combine ripe pear or melon-like fruit with a broad
palate and sufficient acidity to keep that breadth lively. The name refers
to reddish shoot tips, rather than a red-wine use.

## A local grape and its partner

The regional wine organization identifies it as a natural crossing of
[Savagnin](savagnin.md), also known as Traminer, and Roter Veltliner.[^1]
Its close regional association with [Zierfandler](zierfandler.md) developed through both
vineyard cultivation and blending. The traditional name Spätrot-Rotgipfler
combines Zierfandler's synonym Spätrot with Rotgipfler's own name.

Gumpoldskirchen is an important reference point. Historically, wines bearing
that place name could bring several local varieties together. Modern varietal
bottlings give a different opportunity: they allow Rotgipfler's contribution
to be considered on its own, while blends preserve another regional tradition.

## Warmth, ripeness, and body

The regional profile describes a grape that benefits from warm sites and
moderately heavy soils, including calcareous ground. Flowering is relatively
late, and its local harvest commonly extends into October. The useful question
is whether a site offers enough warmth and a sufficiently sound autumn crop.

Rotgipfler can accumulate substantial richness. Picking date therefore matters
to the balance between [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), alcohol, and fruit.
Tank or older-wood maturation can keep varietal fruit prominent, while
[oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) supplies an additional source
of flavour. Riper, fuller examples should be compared with lighter ones as
different harvest and cellar decisions, rather than as fixed quality classes.

[^1]: Thermenregion wine organization, “Rotgipfler,” origin account;
