# Coda di Volpe

Coda di Volpe is a white grape of Campania whose name, “fox's tail,” refers
to the shape of its bunch. It contributes to regional blends and also makes
varietal wines, especially in Irpinia. Those separate bottlings offer a useful
way to understand a grape often encountered as a supporting component beside
[Greco](greco.md).

## Vineyard behaviour

Rauscedo's technical description records modest vigour, late budbreak and
mid-to-late ripening. Large, compact bunches make the health of the fruit
important as harvest approaches; the profile notes susceptibility to bunch
rot and sour rot in wet conditions. The practical challenge is to complete
ripening while keeping those crowded berries sound.

The nursery describes golden wines with floral character, generally intended
for relatively early drinking. That is a useful starting point, but producer
selections show why a grape's common role cannot define every wine made from
it. Vineyard selection and the treatment of the finished wine can create a
different balance of freshness, concentration and texture.

## Irpinia's varietal expression

Vadiaperti's Torama is a documented example of a more deliberate maturation
route. Its importer describes a selection of older, lower-yielding vines in
the Pietradefusi and Torre le Nocelle area. The grapes are pressed as whole
bunches, fermented in temperature-controlled tanks and kept on fine lees for
up to two years. The account concerns this bottling, rather than prescribing
a method for all Coda di Volpe.

Two distinctions help explain that sequence.
[Whole-bunch pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) separates the juice before
fermentation; it is different from fermenting a red wine with whole bunches.
[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) then keeps the wine in contact with
yeast sediment after fermentation, allowing texture to develop without a
requirement for oak flavour.

Torama thus makes an instructive comparison with a young blended white.
The variety's contemporary importance includes both roles: contributing to
established Campanian wines and providing producers with a distinct local
white to explore alongside [Fiano](fiano.md) and [Falanghina](falanghina.md).
