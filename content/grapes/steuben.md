# Steuben

Steuben is a dark-skinned American [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md)
whose history connects fresh fruit with wine. Released in New York, it has
an established table-grape identity in Japan and also supplies contemporary
[Finger Lakes](../regions/finger-lakes.md) wines. The grape's use depends on
harvest and processing as
well as the reasons it was originally selected.

## New York breeding, Japanese table grapes

Cornell's variety register records Steuben as Wayne × Sheridan, released
in 1947, with the selection number 12696.[^identity] Its 1979 New York
guide describes bluish-black fruit, compact bunches, and sweet, spicy
flavour, and explicitly notes an interest for wine as well as fresh use.

Tsuruta's regional Steuben account documents its subsequent Japanese
adoption. In that setting, sweetness and storage life are central: autumn
fruit can be refrigerated and sold into winter. A variety selected and
handled for fresh consumption can consequently develop a different cultural
identity from the same grape fermented into wine.

The distinction matters in tasting. Sugar in a fresh berry contributes
directly to sweetness; fermentation converts much of that sugar into alcohol.
Skin contact, pressing, and the fermentation endpoint then determine a new
balance of aroma, [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), and texture.

## A Finger Lakes rosé

Usonia's 2025 Pompliano Pink uses Steuben grown by Corey Christie near
Naples, New York. The producer describes foot-crushing, overnight contact
with the skins, and pressing before fermentation and maturation in neutral
barrel.[^pink]

This makes the wine a useful example of [rosé](../styles/rose.md) through
limited extraction from a dark grape. The overnight contact differs from
fermenting a red wine with its skins throughout; the neutral barrel supplies
a maturation environment without making new-oak flavour the defining feature.

[^identity]: Cornell University, “Cornell Grape Varieties,” Steuben entry.
[^pink]: Usonia, “Pompliano Pink 2025,” fruit source and production account.
