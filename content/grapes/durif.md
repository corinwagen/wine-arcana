# Durif

Durif is a red grape whose parents are [Syrah](syrah.md) and Peloursin. It is
widely known as Petite Sirah in California and as Durif in Australia. It can
produce deeply coloured, firmly tannic wine, making it useful both as a
blending component and as the basis of a substantial varietal red.

Petite Sirah requires some historical care: the name on an old vineyard record
does not always identify the same vines that a modern nursery would supply
under that name.

## History and identity

François Durif found the variety in his experimental vineyard in eastern
France in the nineteenth century. Foundation Plant Services' historical
account treats a spontaneous seedling as the probable explanation, rather
than assuming a documented deliberate cross. Later genetic work established
Peloursin as the seed parent and Syrah as the pollen parent.[^1]

Material reached California in the 1880s, where Petite Sirah and the spelling
Petite Syrah entered use. Over time, the name was applied to plantings
containing Durif, Peloursin, Syrah, and sometimes other varieties. Historical
usage and mixed vineyards account for much of the apparent contradiction in
older descriptions.

Current US regulations recognize Durif and Petite Sirah as alternative names
for wine labelling. This does not require every bottle to contain only that
grape: the general federal varietal-labelling rule requires at least 75% of the
named variety, with other conditions and exceptions.[^2] A modern label and a
historical planting record therefore answer different questions about identity
and composition.

## Viticulture

Plantgrape describes Durif as moderately vigorous and fertile enough for
short pruning. Its relative tolerance of downy mildew does not amount to broad
disease resistance: grey rot and black rot can still present serious problems.
A dry ripening period is useful where keeping fruit sound is difficult.

Heat brings another constraint. Durif can suffer berry shrivel, so a warm site
does not remove the need to manage exposure and water stress. Its use in hot
regions should be understood alongside this vulnerability. Winter frost is
another reported weakness.

## Wine character and regional uses

Colour and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) explain Durif's longstanding value
in California blends. Varietal wines bring that structure to the foreground.
Ripeness and extraction affect whether the result feels dense and cohesive or
drying; the variety's capacity to supply tannin makes
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) decisions particularly consequential.

Rutherglen in northeastern Victoria is an important Australian home. Its warm
continental climate and long, generally dry autumn give growers an opportunity
to mature red grapes before harvest weather deteriorates. Rain during that
period and spring frost remain regional hazards. Durif's rot and heat
susceptibilities help explain why even this established association depends
on site and management.

The comparison with Syrah is best grounded in identity and structure rather
than the word “Petite.” Durif is a separate offspring, and that name does not
promise a lighter or smaller-scale version of Syrah wine.
