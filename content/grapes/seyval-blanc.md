# Seyval Blanc

Seyval Blanc is a white [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) associated with eastern North
American vineyards and [England](../regions/england.md). It can make fresh still whites and
[traditional-method sparkling wines](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md). Those two uses reveal how a relatively
light wine can gain texture through different cellar methods.

## Crop and wine balance

Purdue's cultivar guide describes a vine prone to carrying too much fruit,
with bunch rot an important concern. Thinning the crop can help the remaining
grapes ripen and keep the vine's growth in balance. The guide also describes
relatively light-bodied wines and the use of fermentation or maturation
choices to develop their texture.

Missouri's wine organization places dry and semi-dry Seyval within its
current regional range. It notes that barrel fermentation supplies a
different expression from a fresh, unoaked wine. The distinction involves
both [oak flavour](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) and the handling of the
wine during fermentation and maturation.

Seyval also contributes to later breeding. Cornell used it as a parent of
[Cayuga White](cayuga-white.md) and [Chardonel](chardonel.md), crossing it
with Schuyler and Chardonnay respectively. The resulting grapes have
their own growing requirements and wine identities; sharing one parent
does not make them interchangeable with Seyval.

## England and sparkling wine

Breaky Bottom in East Sussex offers a sustained example of Seyval's
sparkling role. The estate's account describes its move from still whites
to bottle-fermented sparkling wines, with Seyval remaining central to the
range. Its published cuvées distinguish varietal Seyval from wines based
on [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md) and the Pinot grapes.

This gives a useful comparison within English sparkling wine.
[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) and time in bottle develop texture
and aroma around the base wine's [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), while
the chosen grape continues to influence the starting material.
