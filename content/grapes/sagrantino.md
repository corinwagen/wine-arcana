# Sagrantino

Sagrantino is a dark-skinned grape closely associated with
[Montefalco](../regions/montefalco.md) in central [Umbria](../regions/umbria.md).
Its substantial [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) is central to understanding
both the dry red wine and the sweet *passito* made from dried grapes. Sweetness
can change the balance of a Sagrantino, but it does not remove its structure.

The grape's precise origin remains uncertain. Its contemporary identity is
centred on Umbria: Montefalco Sagrantino gives it a varietal expression, while
Montefalco Rosso combines it with a larger share of
[Sangiovese](sangiovese.md). These are distinct wines, with different starting
points for extraction and ageing.

## In the vineyard

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes Sagrantino as a late-ripening variety of
moderate vigour, with thick-skinned berries and production that can be
irregular. Its technical guidance favours medium to long pruning and attention
to green growth. The practical objective is a manageable canopy and crop that
can ripen during a long season.

Late maturity makes the closing weeks of the season important. Growers must
weigh the development of fruit and skins against autumn conditions and the
health of the bunches. The nursery identifies downy mildew as a particular
susceptibility.

## Tannin and maturation

A study of commercial Sagrantino wines found substantial condensed tannins
still present in samples aged six to eight years. The work also examined the
composition of those tannins, underscoring that concentration alone does not
fully describe a wine's texture.[^tannins] Concentration, tannin composition,
and the rest of the wine's matrix all contribute to the sensation in the mouth.

[Maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) determines how much material is
extracted from skins and seeds during fermentation. Time, temperature, and
handling all matter, so an already tannic grape gives the winemaker important
choices about how much further structure to seek. Later maturation changes
aroma and mouthfeel, although substantial astringency can persist.

Antonelli's dry Montefalco Sagrantino illustrates a patient approach: extended
skin contact, large-oak maturation, and a subsequent period in glass-lined
cement. This approach gives the wine time to develop after extraction, with
large vessels limiting the prominence of oak flavour.

## Dry and sweet expressions

Montefalco Sagrantino DOCG permits both dry and passito wines made entirely
from Sagrantino.[^rules] For passito, selected harvested bunches undergo
[appassimento](../concepts/appassimento.md), losing water before fermentation.
Concentrated sugar, skin extraction, and retained sweetness create a different
balance from the dry wine.

Scacciadiavoli dries its passito grapes on racks into December, then ferments
in steel and matures in barriques. This gives a specific example of the
post-harvest work behind the style. Drying, fermentation, and maturation are
three separate decisions; the word *passito* does not by itself describe the
wine's oak treatment.

## Beyond Umbria

Sagrantino has also found a place in Australian vineyards. Chalmers uses it
in its 2023 Top Block blend from Heathcote in Victoria, alongside
[Aglianico](aglianico.md), [Nero d'Avola](nero-davola.md),
[Piedirosso](piedirosso.md), and a little [Falanghina](falanghina.md). The producer describes
Sagrantino as contributing power and structure within that blend. This gives
a contemporary example of the grape serving a compositional role outside
Montefalco's appellation framework.
