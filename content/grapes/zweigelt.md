# Zweigelt

Zweigelt is an Austrian red *Vitis vinifera* grape created by crossing
St. Laurent and [Blaufränkisch](blaufrankisch.md). It makes wines ranging
from fresh, cherry-scented reds for early drinking to concentrated examples
matured in oak. Its productive vines and adaptability helped it become
widely planted across Austria.

## Breeding and name

Friedrich Zweigelt made the cross at Klosterneuburg in 1922. After the
Second World War, grower Lenz Moser promoted its cultivation, helping it
spread alongside his system of high-trained vines. Rotburger is an
accepted alternative name.

The namesake was a committed Nazi who joined the party in 1933 and headed
the Klosterneuburg school during Nazi rule. Historian Daniel Deckers
documents his antisemitic propaganda and efforts to remove staff he
regarded as politically or religiously undesirable. The promotion of the
grape under his name developed after the war, with Moser advocating the
name in the 1950s.[^1]

## Crop and ripening

The vine grows vigorously and bears generously. Managing the crop and
canopy matters because a heavy crop can delay or weaken ripening. Pruning,
shoot management, and selective thinning give growers ways to adjust how
much fruit the vine carries and how well its leaves support that fruit.

Zweigelt buds early and ripens in the middle part of the season. Its good
winter frost resistance does not remove the risk to young shoots after
budbreak: Klosterneuburg's cultivar description records weak regeneration
after spring frost. The grape is also susceptible to powdery mildew.
Its relatively compact bunches require attention to fruit condition as
harvest approaches.

One particular difficulty is *Traubenwelke*, a physiological berry-shrivel
disorder that interrupts normal ripening and can reduce the usable crop.
Zweigelt is especially affected. Klosterneuburg researchers describe its
causes as unresolved and investigate environmental influences and changes
in the fruit. Nutrient balance, including potassium supply, has received
attention, but a single deficiency should not be assumed to explain every
case.[^2]

## Fruit, texture, and regional styles

Sour cherry is a frequent aroma association, and firmer examples can have
a substantial tannic structure. The grape's range allows producers to
choose between wines that emphasize youthful fruit and wines with the
concentration to support longer maturation.

In a lighter style, restrained extraction can keep
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) supple. A riper crop and greater extraction
can produce more weight and grip, while
[oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) adds another influence on
aroma and texture. These choices help explain why two varietal Zweigelts
can differ markedly in body and readiness to drink.

Carnuntum and the country around Lake Neusiedl are associated with fuller
examples, although site and producer decisions create substantial variation
within each area. Zweigelt also enters blends, allowing its fruit and
structure to be combined with other red varieties.
