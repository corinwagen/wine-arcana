# Petit Manseng

Petit Manseng is a white grape of southwestern France whose small berries can
accumulate considerable sugar while retaining marked [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md).
That combination underlies the sweet wines of Jurançon and Pacherenc du
Vic-Bilh, where fruit may be left to concentrate on the vine. It also makes
substantial dry wines. Harvest date and the amount of sugar fermented matter
as much as the variety's association with sweetness.

## Identity and home

Plantgrape places Petit Manseng's origin in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques. Its
genetic summary describes a close relationship with
[Savagnin](savagnin.md), without specifying parentage.[^1] The regional name
Manseng also appears in [Gros Manseng](gros-manseng.md) and the dark-skinned
[Manseng Noir](manseng-noir.md); these are separate varieties.

Petit and Gros Manseng frequently share a wine, but their practical roles
differ. Petit Manseng's smaller berries and capacity for concentration favour
late-harvest sweet wine. Gros Manseng is more productive and has a particularly
important role in dry whites. Neither distinction confines the grapes to one
style.

## Viticulture and concentration

Petit Manseng has small bunches of very small, thick-skinned berries. It ripens
late and can resist grey rot relatively well, making extended hanging possible
when autumn weather permits. Resistance is not immunity: the condition of the
fruit and the weather still determine how far a grower can delay picking.
Plantgrape describes a vigorous vine suited to trellising and long pruning.

For sweet wine, *passerillage* means dehydration of berries on the vine. As
water is lost, the juice becomes more concentrated. This route does not require
noble rot, although rot may occur in the vineyard.
Successive passes allow pickers to take suitable bunches while leaving others.
The grape's retained acidity gives concentrated juice a useful counterweight to
sugar, but does not by itself guarantee balance.

## Dry and sweet expressions

In dry Petit Manseng, fermentation converts more of the accumulated sugar to
alcohol. A later harvest can therefore produce a powerful dry wine rather than
a sweet one. Barrel fermentation and lees ageing may broaden its texture;
citrus and tropical-fruit aromas can accompany the firm acid structure.

Two Jurançon wines illustrate the range. Domaine de Malarrode's Découverte
2023 is dry, entirely Petit Manseng, and fermented and matured in barrel.
Domaine Capdevielle's Noblesse d'Automne is also entirely Petit Manseng, but
uses successive late picks of fruit concentrated on the vine and is matured
in tank.[^2] They show why the grape name alone cannot establish sweetness or
oak influence.

Experience outside France also demonstrates the importance of climate. Writing
about Tablas Creek's California plantings in 2017, Jason Haas emphasized acid
retention in hot, sunny Paso Robles, whereas resistance to rot was particularly
useful in humid Virginia. These are different reasons to grow the same grape,
and need not lead to the same harvest date or finished style.
