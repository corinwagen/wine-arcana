# Blanc du Bois

Blanc du Bois is a white [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) developed
in Florida for conditions where [Pierce's disease](../concepts/pierces-disease.md)
can make susceptible wine grapes difficult to sustain. Its role extends
through the southeastern United States and into Texas, particularly the
Gulf Coast. Floral, citrus and sometimes Muscat-like character can appear
in wines ranging from dry whites to sparkling and fortified styles.

The important qualification is that tolerance of a bacterial disease does
not remove fungal-disease problems. Blanc du Bois can survive a threat that
defeats many other vines while still demanding considerable protection.

## Florida origins

John A. Mortensen's release circular records a 1968 cross between Florida
D6-148 and Cardinal. After selection and trials, the University of Florida
released Blanc du Bois in 1987. Its name honours Emile DuBois, a French
immigrant who grew grapes and made wine in Florida.[^origin]

This is a warm-region breeding story. It differs from the emphasis on
deep-winter survival associated with grapes such as
[Frontenac](frontenac.md), even though both belong under the broad hybrid
heading.

## What tolerance does and does not mean

University of Georgia guidance explains that the Pierce's disease
bacterium can occur within Blanc du Bois without the decline expected
in susceptible vines. It also records serious susceptibility to
anthracnose and other fungal diseases. Tolerance is therefore a specific
biological advantage, rather than a promise of healthy fruit without
management.[^disease]

Texas A&M describes vigorous growth and the use of high-wire, divided
canopies to accommodate it. Fruit set can vary, and tight bunches in
heavy-set years increase rot concerns. [Training](../concepts/pruning-and-training.md)
and [canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) help organize
growth, expose fruit and allow access for vineyard work.

The vine is often grown on its own roots, but alkaline soils can make
rootstock choice useful. [Grafting](../concepts/grafting.md) in that case
addresses soil adaptation; it is not the source of the fruiting variety's
Pierce's disease tolerance.

## Wine and harvest

Blanc du Bois ripens over a relatively short season. Texas guidance warns
that extending hang time can cause berries to fall from their clusters,
so waiting for ever-higher sugar is not a cost-free choice. Its juice also
browns readily through [oxidation](../concepts/oxidation.md), although it
can lighten during fermentation.

The variety's southern relevance includes Georgia as well as its Florida
origin and Texas use. Georgia's extension account describes still,
sparkling and Madeira-style fortified possibilities. The latter refers
to a method and stylistic ambition; it does not make a Texas or Georgia
wine Madeira. Across these uses, harvest condition and cellar decisions
help determine how its aromatic fruit reaches the bottle.

[^disease]: University of Georgia, “Blanc du Bois,” disease and pest account.
