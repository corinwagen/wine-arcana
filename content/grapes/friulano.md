# Friulano

Friulano is a white grape closely associated with
[Friuli](../regions/friuli.md) in northeastern Italy.
Its dry wines often combine a rounded middle with a gently bitter, almond-like
finish. Texture and savoury persistence are usually more useful guides than
an expectation of forceful perfume or sharply cutting acidity.

## Names and identity

The grape formerly labelled Tocai Friulano in Italy is also Sauvignonasse.
Foundation Plant Services at the University of California, Davis, records
Tocai Friulano as the Italian synonym of that variety. It is distinct from
[Sauvignon Blanc](sauvignon-blanc.md), despite the similar French name.[^1]

The older Tocai name also causes confusion with [Tokaj](../regions/tokaj.md),
the Hungarian wine region associated especially with [Furmint](furmint.md).
Those are different subjects. Modern Italian wine labels generally use
Friulano in Friuli and Tai in Veneto, while older bottles and technical
literature retain Tocai Friulano.

## Growing for balance

Rauscedo describes a vigorous, fruitful vine with late budbreak and
mid-season ripening. Long pruning suits its fruiting pattern, but abundant
growth makes the relationship between leaf area, crop and exposure important.
The nursery also records susceptibility to bunch rot. Ventilation and the
condition of the fruit matter in a wet harvest season.

The grape can accumulate enough sugar to make a substantial white while
retaining only moderate acidity. Picking decisions therefore influence whether
the wine feels fresh and rounded or broad and heavy. Clonal descriptions show
variation in aroma and structure; moderate acidity does not mean that every
Friulano tastes soft or lacks the capacity to mature.

## A living Friulian tradition

The Colli Orientali consortium describes Friulano as a regional flagship,
commonly bottled on its own. Its green-apple, floral and almond associations
give readers a useful starting point, rather than a diagnostic list that
every bottle must satisfy. Within the region it offers a different emphasis
from the brisker expressions of [Ribolla Gialla](ribolla-gialla.md).

Across the border in Slovenia's Brda, Kabaj bottles the grape as Ravan,
identifying it as Zeleni Sauvignon in its Slovenian technical account.
The estate describes seven days of skin contact followed by two years in
French oak and further bottle maturation. This provides a contemporary
[skin-contact](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md) expression alongside
Friuli's more conventionally pressed whites. The shared grape crosses a
border, while cellar choices give it a different texture and development.

[^1]: University of California, Davis, Foundation Plant Services,
