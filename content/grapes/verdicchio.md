# Verdicchio

Verdicchio is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety closely associated with Marche
in central Italy. Its two principal local expressions,
[Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi](../regions/castelli-di-jesi.md) and
[Verdicchio di Matelica](../regions/matelica.md), show how the same grape can
respond to different relationships between hills, mountains, and sea.

The wines commonly retain marked [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) alongside enough substance for
ageing. Citrus and orchard-fruit impressions and an almond-like finish are
useful reference points. Harvest timing, site, and maturation determine
whether the result is a brisk young white or a broader, more developed wine.

## Names and identity

Genetic and morphological research places Verdicchio, Trebbiano di Soave,
and the material studied as Trebbiano di Lugana or Turbiana within the same
variety. A 2012 study also found matching DNA profiles among samples called
Verdello and Verduschia. Regional names and selections preserve their own
viticultural histories within that shared identity.[^1]

Trebbiano di Soave contributes to [Soave](../regions/soave.md) blends alongside
[Garganega](garganega.md). Around the southern end of Lake Garda, the names
Turbiana and Trebbiano di Lugana connect the grape with
[Lugana](../regions/lugana.md) wine.
These geographical names help explain why a variety associated with Marche
also has an important life in northern Italy.

## Jesi and Matelica

The Castelli di Jesi area follows hills around the Esino valley toward the
Adriatic. Its range extends from sites closer to the coast to higher inland
ground. Marine air moderates parts of this landscape, while the differences
in elevation and exposure give growers a range of ripening conditions.
Marly and clay-rich soils, limestone, sandstone, and sand occur across the
area.

Matelica lies farther inland in a valley running roughly north–south. The
Apennines stand to the west and Monte San Vicino to the east, limiting the
sea's moderating influence. Cold winters, warm summers, and pronounced
day-to-night temperature changes give the area a more continental setting.
Its wines often combine firm acidity with substantial fruit and body.

These are regional tendencies. A high site in the Jesi hills and a warm
Matelica slope can complicate a simple comparison, as can the vintage and
the producer's preferred harvest maturity.

## Harvest and wine styles

Earlier harvesting favours a lighter, more acid-led style. Allowing grapes
to ripen further can develop greater fruit weight and alcohol, which must
remain balanced by the wine's acidity. The variety supports still wines,
sparkling wines, and sweet passito styles made with dried grapes.

The DOC specifications for both Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi and
Verdicchio di Matelica require at least 85% Verdicchio. Each area also has
a separate Riserva DOCG denomination.[^2] The legal names therefore describe
several production traditions in addition to origin. The label's category
and the producer's maturation choices provide useful context for the wine's
weight and development.
