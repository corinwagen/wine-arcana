# Malvasia de Colares

Malvasia de Colares is the white grape associated with the Atlantic vineyards
of [Colares](../regions/colares.md), near Sintra in Portugal. It makes dry
white wines whose texture and aromatic development can owe much to cellar
work. The full local name is useful: Malvasia appears in the names of numerous
grapes, and the word alone does not establish a shared identity or wine style.

## Viticulture

The traditional sand vineyards have vines on their own roots. Growers root
ungrafted canes beneath the surface sand, and the sandy environment protected
the vineyards during the nineteenth-century
[phylloxera](../concepts/phylloxera.md) epidemic. As with red
[Ramisco](ramisco.md), this is a feature of the growing environment, rather
than proof that the grape itself resists the pest.

Adega Regional de Colares describes low, spreading vines sheltered behind
cane screens, with clusters raised on small supports in late summer. Its
2011 Malvasia was harvested in the last week of September. Cool maritime
conditions, frequent mist, and wind help explain both the cultivation method
and the importance of bringing fruit through a long coastal season.

## Wine character

Cellar work helps explain the wine's texture and aromatic development.
Fermentation, contact with yeast sediment, and wood maturation each provide
ways to shape a dry white beyond the character of the grapes at harvest.

The coöperative's 2011 technical sheet describes citrus and herbal notes
alongside wax and honey, while recording less than two grams per litre of
residual sugar. Honey as an aroma therefore need not indicate a sweet wine.

## In the cellar

Two documented bottlings show the range of production choices. Adega
Regional's 2011 Malvasia received six hours of skin contact in the press,
overnight settling, and fermentation in stainless steel. Twelve months in
tank with regular stirring of the yeast sediment preceded six months in
wood.[^arenae]

The Infinitude Malvasia Branco 2021 technical description instead records
juice fermentation after settling and five months in a reconditioned
500-litre French oak barrel previously used for Burgundy Chardonnay.[^infinitude]
[Lees aging](../concepts/lees-aging.md), brief skin contact, and vessel choice
give producers different ways to develop the wine's texture. Neither example
should be treated as a fixed rule for the grape.
