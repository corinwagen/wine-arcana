# Silvaner

Silvaner is a white grape closely associated with Germany's
[Franken](../regions/franken.md) and Rheinhessen regions and, under the
spelling Sylvaner, with [Alsace](../regions/alsace.md). Its restrained fruit
and herbal aromas accompany wines ranging from light and brisk to broad and
concentrated.

## Identity and vineyard behaviour

Plantgrape, the French research institutions' grape catalogue, reports genetic
evidence for a crossing of [Savagnin](savagnin.md) and Österreichisch Weiß.
It places the variety's origin in central Europe, probably Austria.[^1]
Germany's wine institute records a planting at Castell, in Franken, in 1659.
That documented introduction helps explain the grape's long regional history.

Silvaner can crop regularly. Spring frost can damage the young growth, and
the shoots sometimes harden incompletely before winter. Plantgrape also
identifies susceptibility to powdery mildew, downy mildew, and grey rot.
Growers must bring a sound crop to
ripeness while managing these risks.

Yield and site matter to its range of styles. Plantgrape describes lively,
delicately aromatic wines and notes that suitable conditions and controlled
yields can produce fuller, more complex results.

## Germany

Franken gives Silvaner a particularly strong regional identity. Shell limestone
and Keuper, a group of sedimentary formations including marl and clay-bearing
rocks, are prominent in its vineyard landscape. Rheinhessen has a larger area
of the grape, and the Palatinate is another established home. The German Wine
Institute describes a spectrum extending from light summer wines through
creamy, barrel-matured examples to concentrated dry and sweet wines.[^2]

Herbal notes and relatively quiet fruit are common reference points. A lighter
wine may emphasize freshness; a riper, more concentrated example can put more
weight and texture around the same subdued aromatic core.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) adds another source of flavour
and texture, so a broad Silvaner may reflect both its fruit and its treatment
in the cellar.

## Alsace

Alsatian Sylvaner is commonly presented as a fresh, dry wine with discreet
citrus, floral, or green herbal notes. The regional wine council describes a
combination of lively acidity and a rounded impression in the mouth.[^3]
German examples are often described as having milder acidity. Site, ripeness,
and winemaking determine how much acid a wine retains and how strongly it is
perceived alongside its body and fruit.
