# Aromella

Aromella is an aromatic white [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md)
released by Cornell in 2013. Its Muscat-like perfume can give a varietal
wine a conspicuous identity, while small proportions can also change the
aroma of a blend. Its contemporary
[Finger Lakes](../regions/finger-lakes.md) uses extend from semi-dry
white wine to rosé and red blends.

## Breeding and an early regional wine

Cornell records Aromella as [Traminette](traminette.md) × Ravat 34, from
a cross made in 1976. It was tested as NY76.0844.24 before receiving its
name.[^origin] Cornell's release coverage emphasizes winter hardiness and
productivity alongside its aromatic wine character.

In 2014, Cornell documented Goose Watch's release of a varietal Aromella
after years of vineyard testing with the Peterson family. The reported
semi-dry style gave the grape's perfume a setting in which remaining sugar
and acidity could contribute to balance.

## Aroma as one component of a blend

Usonia's 2025 Soon Different combines Aromella with [Regent](regent.md)
and [Aravelle](aravelle.md). Aromella received one night of skin contact,
followed by pressing and separate fermentation. The producer then blended
the components into rosé. The approach lets the winemaker handle the
aromatic white fruit separately from the portion carrying red pigment.

Its 2025 Good Spirit takes another route: Aromella joins
[Chancellor](chancellor.md) and
Traminette in a whole-cluster cofermentation, with separately fermented
[Baco Noir](baco-noir.md) added after pressing. Aroma, extraction, and
blending decisions
therefore give the same grape different roles within the cellar.

[^origin]: Cornell University, “Cornell Grape Varieties,” pedigree and
    selection number; Cornell CALS, “New wine from Cornell's latest grape
    variety,” 2014, crossing and testing history.
