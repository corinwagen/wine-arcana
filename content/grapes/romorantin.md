# Romorantin

Romorantin is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety of the central Loire. Galinié
(2023) identifies it as the sole grape of the Cour-Cheverny appellation and
notes plantings in nearby parts of Loir-et-Cher.[^2] The variety was once more
widely planted in France, but Plantgrape's historical series records a fall
from 680 hectares in 1958 to 79 hectares in 2018.[^1]

## History and identity

DNA analysis indicates that Romorantin resulted from a cross between Pinot
teinturier and Gouais blanc.[^2]

The variety's name has also acquired a misleading royal story. A 1518 record
reimburses Pierre La Parque for the purchase of 80,000 *complants de Beaune*,
the labor, and the completed transport from Beaune via Digoin and Tours to
Romorantin, where the vines had already been replanted. Galinié identifies the
plants as a red Pinot, not this white grape. The name Romorantin is not
documented before 1827, and the variety was probably created by crossing in a
Loire vineyard, possibly under another name before its modern identity was
settled.[^2]

## Viticulture and wine character

Romorantin is fairly fertile and productive, with an erect growth habit. It
can be pruned either long or short, but Plantgrape recommends adapted, early
terroirs. In its French reference collection it breaks bud at about the same
time as Chasselas and reaches mid-season maturity roughly two and a half to
three weeks later. Its medium-to-large bunches carry small berries. The variety
is moderately susceptible to grey rot and grape moths, so site and fruit
condition matter at harvest.[^1]

Plantgrape describes the resulting wines as fairly fine, fruity, and pleasant.

## Where it is grown

Cour-Cheverny is Romorantin's defining home, with additional plantings among
growers in the surrounding Loir-et-Cher area. Plantgrape also records a
conservatory of about 50 clones planted there in 2007, alongside four certified
clones numbered 466, 873, 928, and 929.[^1]
