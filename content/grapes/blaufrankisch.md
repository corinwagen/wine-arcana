# Blaufränkisch

Blaufränkisch is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape whose firm acidity,
tannin, and dark-fruit character give it an important place in Central
European red wine. It is known as Kékfrankos in Hungary, Lemberger or Blauer
Limberger in Germany, and Frankovka in several neighbouring countries.
Austria's Burgenland is a major reference point for varietal wines. It is
also a parent of [Zweigelt](zweigelt.md).

Genetic research identifies its parents as Blaue Zimmettraube and Weisser
Heunisch, also known as Gouais Blanc. The researchers combined this result
with historical evidence to propose Lower Styria, in present-day Slovenia,
as its place of origin.[^1] That geographical conclusion remains an
inference from the surviving vines and records.

## A long growing season

Blaufränkisch buds early and ripens late. The combination exposes young
shoots to spring frost and requires enough warmth late in the season to
finish ripening. Protected, warm sites therefore have practical advantages,
especially toward the cooler limits of cultivation. Cold conditions during
flowering can also reduce fruit set.

The vine can grow vigorously and needs attention to canopy conditions and
fungal disease. Powdery and downy mildew are relevant risks. Opening the
fruiting zone can improve ventilation, while the amount of exposed fruit
must suit the site's heat and sunlight.

Deep soils that retain water can support the vine through dry summers.
In Mittelburgenland, clay-rich loams are an important part of this setting,
alongside warm conditions and shelter from surrounding hills. The balance
between available water, crop, and season affects whether the resulting
wine feels taut or more generously ripe.

## Across borders and landscapes

Austrian examples come from several parts of Burgenland, including
Mittelburgenland, Leithaberg, and Eisenberg, as well as Carnuntum in Lower
Austria. These places vary in elevation, exposure, and soil. Their wines
can consequently differ in weight and ripeness even when they share the
grape's recognizable acidity and tannin.

In Germany, Lemberger is particularly associated with Württemberg. The
German Wine Institute describes a range from lighter, fruit-driven wines
to concentrated examples with substantial tannin. It also appears in
blends with Trollinger. The different name on a German label refers to
the same variety.

## Structure in the glass

Cherry, blackberry, and other dark-berry impressions are common, sometimes
accompanied by spice. [Acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) helps maintain a
lively palate, while [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) can make young,
concentrated wines feel firm. Fruit ripeness and extraction influence how
these elements balance one another.

Fermentation and maturation offer room for several styles. A wine intended
for early drinking may emphasize fruit and supple texture. More structured
examples can spend time in wood and develop with bottle age.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) can contribute its own
aromas, so the spice in a finished wine need not come entirely from the
grape.
