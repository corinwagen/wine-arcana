# Moschofilero

Moschofilero is a pink- to grey-skinned Greek *Vitis vinifera* grape usually
made into aromatic white wine. Its main reference is the cool Mantinia
plateau in the Peloponnese. Floral and citrus aromas can accompany a light
body and pronounced [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), giving it a different balance from a broadly
textured aromatic white such as [Viognier](viognier.md).

The coloured skins also allow [rosé](../styles/rose.md) and more extended skin-contact styles.
As with [Pinot Gris](pinot-gris.md), the berry's appearance does not dictate
the finished wine's colour: pressing and time with the skins determine how
much pigment reaches the juice.

## A cool plateau and a late harvest

Moschofilero ripens late in its mountain setting. Mantinia's cool conditions
help retain the acidity that gives many of its wines their brisk shape.
The grape's perfume can be expressive even in wines of modest weight, so
aromatic intensity and body need to be considered separately.

Still whites are an established regional expression, but sparkling wine is
also important. For a sparkling base, picking earlier preserves a different
balance of sugar and acidity from a harvest intended for a fuller still
wine. Blending provides another outlet: Moschofilero can supply perfume to
less aromatic partners.

## Three ways to handle the fruit

Prompt pressing and fermentation away from the skins favour a pale white
wine. The resulting emphasis is on fragrance and freshness. Keeping juice
with the skins changes both colour and texture through
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md).

Troupis's Hoof & Lur makes this second approach concrete. The winery describes
a varietal Moschofilero from Mantinia made with skin contact and spontaneous
fermentation, bottled without fining or filtration. It classifies the wine
as a dry rosé. This offers a local comparison with the region's pale whites,
without requiring a different grape or a distant growing area.

Tselepos's Amalia Brut takes a third route. The producer harvests early,
presses chilled whole grapes, and ferments the base wine in steel before a
second fermentation in bottle. This [traditional
method](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md) creates the bubbles;
subsequent contact with the yeast deposit adds another source of aroma and
texture alongside Moschofilero's own perfume. The producer's published method
specifies at least nine months for that second fermentation and maturation.
