# Marquette

Marquette is a red wine grape released by the University of Minnesota
in 2006. It gives cold-climate growers a route to dry reds with berry-like
fruit and moderate body, including wines matured in oak. Its usefulness
rests on a combination of winter survival, ripening, and wine character.

## The limits of hardiness

The university describes reliable performance in its Zone 4 setting, with
more serious winter injury possible farther north or on wet sites.
Marquette also breaks bud relatively early, exposing new shoots to [spring
frost](../concepts/spring-frost.md). A vine's dormant wood and its tender
green growth face different
hazards.

Well-drained ground and [canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md)
are important. Fertile sites can drive vigorous growth, while the university's
profile records only moderate resistance to several fungal diseases.
Harvest in Minnesota generally precedes [Frontenac](frontenac.md), although
the actual sequence depends on site and season.

## Wine and regional use

The breeder describes cherry, berry, and sometimes pepper-like impressions.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) can add flavour and alter the
texture, so an oaked example should be distinguished from a young,
fruit-focused bottling.

Québec also provides an established setting. Domaine de Pontiac Village
includes Marquette among the hardy red varieties in its vineyard. This
illustrates why the grape matters beyond Minnesota: growers can use it
within a local mix of varieties selected for a demanding continental climate.
