# Tsitska

Tsitska is a white grape of Imereti in western Georgia, used for dry whites,
blends, and sparkling wine. Its capacity to retain
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) helps explain that range: it can bring
freshness to a still blend or provide the firm base needed for a second
fermentation in bottle.

## Imereti and its blends

The National Wine Agency describes Tsitska as a thick-skinned grape grown
throughout upper and central Imereti, susceptible to both powdery and downy
mildew. Its dry wines can suggest pear or quince. Regional blending partners
include [Tsolikouri](tsolikouri.md) and [Krakhuna](krakhuna.md).

Baia's Wine in Obcha provides a contemporary example of the blend with
Tsolikouri. Its importer describes the two grapes fermented together in
qvevri, the Georgian earthenware vessels, with part of the skins retained.
After three months, the wine moves to stainless steel. This gives the
[skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md) a different
route to texture from simply pressing all the juice off its skins before
fermentation. The proportion of skins and length of
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) both matter.

## Sparkling Tsitska

Khareba winemaker Vladimer Kublashvili's portfolio describes a sparkling
wine made entirely from Imeretian Tsitska by the
[traditional method](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md), with
its second fermentation in bottle.[^1] This is a useful counterpart to the
qvevri blend: the same regional grape supports two quite different cellar
traditions.

For sparkling wine, the balance of sugar and acid at harvest shapes the
base wine before bubbles or maturation contribute their own effects.
[Lees aging](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can then add texture and yeast-derived
aromas. Those characteristics depend on production choices as well as the
grape, so a sparkling Tsitska and a still example need not share the same
aromatic emphasis.

[^1]: Vladimer Kublashvili, “Sparkling Wine – Tsitska Méthode Traditionnelle,”
    winemaker's portfolio, accessed 21 September 2026.
