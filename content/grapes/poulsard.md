# Poulsard

Poulsard is a red *Vitis vinifera* variety native to the [Jura](../regions/jura.md) vineyards of
eastern France. It is one of the grapes that makes Jura's red and rosé wines
recognizable: its wines are often lightly coloured and fine-boned rather than
deeply pigmented and forcefully tannic.

## Identity and naming

Poulsard is the canonical name used by the French varietal catalogue. In
France, *Ploussard* may officially be used for plant propagation material, and
the Côtes du Jura specification calls it the local name. These are names for
the same variety, not separate Jura grapes. Plantgrape describes Poulsard as
native to the Jura; genetic analyses suggest a close relationship to Rèze.[^1]

The variety has a long documentary presence in the region. The Côtes du Jura
specification says that Poulsard is recorded in the Jura from 1620, while
describing it, [Trousseau](trousseau.md), and [Savagnin](savagnin.md) as typically Jurassian varieties.
Plantgrape records 257 hectares in France in 2018.[^2]

## Viticulture and site

Poulsard is a delicate vine in the practical sense that its crop and health
are easily disrupted. Plantgrape identifies sensitivity to spring frost,
coulure (poor fruit set), and heat, as well as particular susceptibility to
powdery mildew. It requires long pruning and trellising. The record places it
especially well on marly and limestone soils, conditions common in the Jura
where marl, clay, limestone, slope, and exposure form a patchwork of vineyard
sites.

In the Vassal collection, it budbreaks about one day before Chasselas and
reaches maturity roughly a week and a half after it. Small bunches and
medium-to-large berries are characteristic of the variety.

The Jura's climate and topography sharpen these differences. The Côtes du
Jura area is a cool, rainy, oceanic-influenced region with continental effects,
and its vineyards occupy exposed hills between roughly 300 and 450 metres.
Marl and clay can retain coolness at depth, while limestone scree helps warm
and drain the surface.

## Wine character in the Jura

Plantgrape describes Poulsard wines as aromatic, not very full-bodied, and
marked by finesse, with variable colour that can occasionally reach an
onion-skin shade. In the Côtes du Jura specification, Poulsard contributes a
characteristic light-ruby colour and fruit aromas to red and rosé wines. The
same document contrasts the darker colour of [Pinot Noir](pinot-noir.md) and the more tannic,
often more coloured character of Trousseau. Those are useful regional
comparisons, not a fixed sensory scale for every Poulsard wine.

## In the cellar

Within Côtes du Jura, Poulsard is a principal variety for red and rosé wines,
and it may also be used in *[vin de paille](../styles/vin-de-paille.md)*.[^3]
