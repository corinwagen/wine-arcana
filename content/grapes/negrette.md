# Négrette

Négrette is a dark-skinned southwestern French grape most closely associated
with Fronton, near Toulouse. It can combine deep colour and a distinctive
floral perfume with relatively gentle tannin and acidity. Its usefulness in
both red and [rosé](../styles/rose.md) wine comes from that combination of
aromatic presence and supple structure.

## Fronton and the origin story

A familiar local story says the Knights Hospitaller brought Négrette from
Cyprus after the Crusades. The Fronton wine body's own grape profile treats
this as legend and points instead toward a local origin around the Tarn.
Plantgrape identifies the variety as southwestern French.[^1] The presence
of the medieval order in Fronton does not establish the provenance of the
modern grape.

Fronton is the principal place to encounter Négrette, in varietal bottlings
as well as blends. Smaller presences survive in Vendée and the Atlantic
islands of Ré and Oléron. The Fronton body also records cultivation in
California.

## A sensitive vine

Négrette buds relatively late and reaches mid-season maturity in Plantgrape's
comparative observations. It is vigorous and fertile with short pruning,
but poor fruit set and uneven berry development can make yields irregular.
Grey rot and powdery mildew are particular concerns.

These traits make healthy fruit an important constraint on harvest decisions.
Waiting for further aroma development must be weighed against the condition
of the bunches. [Canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) also
affects the environment around the fruit, linking disease control with the
conditions in which aromas develop.

## Perfume, colour, and texture

Violet is a useful characteristic aroma because it has a researched chemical
basis. The Institut français de la vigne et du vin identifies beta-ionone as
the principal contributor to violet notes in Négrette wines. Its research
has examined how leaf removal and water supply influence the aroma's
precursors and eventual expression.[^2] The perfume therefore depends partly
on vineyard conditions, alongside the grape's inherent aromatic potential.

Fruit and spice can accompany the floral note. Their relative prominence
changes from wine to wine, so a single recognizable aroma gives only part
of the picture. A perfumed wine may still have modest freshness if its
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) is low.

Plantgrape describes abundant anthocyanins, the red grape pigments, alongside
generally modest tannin. That distinction helps explain why a dark Négrette
can feel soft. Its general profile favours early enjoyment, while a particular
blend or more structured bottling may develop for longer. For readers
exploring southwestern reds, the comparison with [Tannat](tannat.md) is useful:
colour alone cannot predict the drying grip of either wine.
