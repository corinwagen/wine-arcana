# Kotsifali

Kotsifali is a dark-skinned Greek *Vitis vinifera* variety closely associated
with Crete, particularly the vineyards around Heraklion. Its wines often
combine generous alcohol and aromatic fruit with relatively modest colour,
soft tannin, and moderate or low [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). This combination helps explain
both its traditional role in blends and the appeal of varietal bottlings.

Colour, weight, and grip need to be assessed separately. A relatively pale
Kotsifali can still feel full because alcohol and fruit contribute to
weight, while [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) supplies a different kind of
structure. Extraction and harvest ripeness change how those elements fit
together.

## The Cretan blending tradition

Kotsifali's established partner is Mandilaria, also called Mandilari on
Crete. Mandilaria supplies deeper colour and firmer tannin, while Kotsifali
adds fruit, breadth, and a more supple texture. The combination is closely
associated with the reds of Peza and Archanes. The two grapes complement
each other because their structural tendencies differ.

Producers also blend Kotsifali with international varieties, including
[Syrah](syrah.md). Such wines can retain a Cretan grape as an important
part of their character while drawing colour, tannin, or a different
aromatic contribution from the partner. Proportions and extraction matter
as much as the mere presence of either variety.

## Varietal wine and present-day Crete

Varietal bottlings offer a clearer view of Kotsifali's own balance.
Lyrarakis, for example, describes a dry wine made entirely from Kotsifali
grown at about 500 metres. This documented example places the grape in an
upland setting and presents it without a blending partner.

Crete's mountain sites and maritime exposure create substantial local
variation. Recent reporting describes renewed attention to varietal and
vineyard-focused wines across the island, alongside its enduring blends.
Kotsifali belongs to that movement as well as the older regional tradition.

For fuller reds, [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) and more
extraction can add structure and change the aromatic balance. A lighter
handling can keep the wine closer to its fruit and softer texture. The
choice should follow the fruit available and the intended wine, rather
than a requirement to make every Kotsifali dark or heavily tannic.
