# Areni

Areni is an Armenian red wine grape particularly associated with the
mountain vineyards of Vayots Dzor. Often sold as Areni Noir, it can make
reds with fresh acidity, red-fruit flavours, and a peppery edge. Its
contemporary range includes tank-made reds, barrel-matured wines, and
sparkling rosé.

## Mountain vineyards and modern wine

Yacoubian-Hobbs describes Areni as late ripening in its Vayots Dzor
vineyards. The mountain setting matters because ripening depends on both
the available warmth and the length of the season.
[Elevation](../concepts/altitude.md), exposure,
and harvest date help explain how ripe fruit and fresh
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) can coexist in the region's wines.

Zulal gives this regional discussion a concrete example. Founded by Aimee
Keushguerian in 2017, the winery sources Areni from Aghavnadzor and Khachik.
Its importer describes vineyards between 1,400 and 1,750 metres, with a
mixture of limestone and volcanic material, and fermentation in stainless
steel for the classic bottling.[^1] That approach keeps oak flavour out of
the comparison and emphasizes the fruit and fermentation character.

Zulal's Areni Reserve offers a different treatment of the same variety,
with barrel maturation adding another source of texture and spice.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) changes the wine through
oxygen exposure as well as compounds extracted from wood. The classic
and Reserve bottlings therefore provide useful points of comparison
without implying that one is the definitive expression of Areni.

Keush uses Areni for a traditional-method sparkling rosé. Its published
composition is entirely Areni, showing a further use for the grape within
Armenian production. Harvest decisions and
[traditional-method winemaking](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md)
can direct it toward a markedly different balance from a still red.

## The cave and the cultivar

The Areni-1 cave complex provides evidence of winemaking around
4100–4000 BCE. The excavators documented grape remains, a pressing basin,
a vat, and chemical residues consistent with wine.[^2] This establishes
a remarkably old regional winemaking context.

It does not by itself identify the grapes as today's Areni variety.
Archaeological evidence of cultivated grapevines and genetic identification
of a modern cultivar answer different questions. The grape's present
importance rests on the vineyards and wines of Vayots Dzor as well as the
region's much older history.
