# Verdelho

Verdelho is a Portuguese white grape associated with Madeira and the Azores,
and also established in Australia. Its uses span fortified wine and fresh,
fruit-led whites. On [Madeira](../styles/madeira.md), the name commonly points
toward a medium-dry style with somewhat more body and less pronounced acidity
than [Sercial](sercial.md). Australian examples often
emphasize the grape's herbal and tropical-fruit aromas.

## Identity and similar names

Verdelho and Spain's [Verdejo](verdejo.md) are distinct grape varieties.
Foundation Plant Services at the University of California, Davis, explicitly
separates them despite their similar names and appearance.[^1]

Historical Portuguese usage creates another ambiguity. Verdelho was also
used for the grape now called Gouveio in Portugal and
[Godello](godello.md) in Spain. Foundation Plant Services distinguishes that
usage from the Verdelho of Madeira, the Azores, and Australia.[^2] Older
references therefore need their regional context before the name can be
interpreted as a cultivar identity.

## Madeira

The Instituto do Vinho, do Bordado e do Artesanato da Madeira (IVBAM), the
island's regulatory institute, describes Verdelho as having small to
medium-sized bunches and elongated berries with resistant skins. Its Madeira
account places cultivation around 400 metres in elevation and associates the
variety with off-dry wine.[^3]

The familiar medium-dry style combines the fruit's composition with the
producer's fermentation decisions. [Fortification](../concepts/fortification.md)
retains sugar by interrupting yeast activity. Madeira's later heat treatment
or warm cask ageing then develops the wine's mature character. The grape name
is therefore useful alongside the sweetness and age information on the
bottle.

## Australia

Verdelho has a contrasting life in Australian white wine. Wine Australia
identifies it among the principal varieties of South Burnett, northwest of
Brisbane in Queensland. The region has a warm subtropical climate moderated
by elevation, maritime influence, and cool nights. Its Verdelhos are commonly
aromatic, with herbal and tropical-fruit character.[^4]

Manjimup, in Western Australia, supplies another setting. Indian and Southern
Ocean influences moderate the climate, while its elevation gives it more
continental conditions than nearby Margaret River. Wine Australia describes
Manjimup Verdelho as combining fresh acidity with tropical flavour and a
rounded mouthfeel.[^5]
