# Sabrevois

Sabrevois is a dark-skinned [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md) bred
by Elmer Swenson in Wisconsin. Its
[winter hardiness](../concepts/winter-hardiness.md) helped it find a place
in northern vineyards, including Québec, whose village of Sabrevois supplied
its eventual name. Contemporary wines also connect it with the
[Finger Lakes](../regions/finger-lakes.md).

Its colour can be much more substantial than its body or tannin. Harvest
and extraction matter particularly because the grape's appealing berry fruit
can be accompanied by increasingly pronounced smoky or savoury aromas.

## Breeding and northern adaptation

Iowa State's cultivar review records Sabrevois as selection ES 2-1-9, from
E.S. 283 × E.S. 193, the same parental combination that produced St. Croix.
These are distinct seedlings with different growing and wine characteristics.
The review traces Sabrevois to Swenson's work at Osceola, Wisconsin, and
documents its later naming in Québec.[^identity]

Iowa State's updated regional guidance includes Sabrevois among wine grapes
suited to all parts of Iowa. It describes vigorous growth, early-to-middle
season harvest, and useful disease resistance. That regional recommendation
helps establish its practical northern role; individual vineyards still need
to match growth and crop to their season.

## Ripeness, colour, and aroma

The cultivar review describes modest sugar accumulation and relatively little
body and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md), even in deeply coloured wines.
Its potential alcohol therefore differs from that of a grape accumulating
more sugar at a comparable harvest stage. Blending can adjust the balance,
while an earlier [pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) offers a route to rosé.

Trout Brook Vineyard's grower account makes the harvest choice concrete.
It describes a preference for picking before the smoky side becomes
overwhelming, accepting less sugar in exchange for the aromatic balance it
wants. The same account values lighter-bodied reds as well as blending uses.

## A New York example

Jamie Goode's October 2026 account of Usonia identifies its 2024 Pompliano
Purple as Sabrevois from biodynamically farmed fruit. The bottling places
the grape within contemporary Finger Lakes wine culture alongside the
region's vinifera and other hybrids.[^usonia]

Read alongside Usonia's [Steuben](steuben.md) rosé, it also illustrates the
range hidden by the word *hybrid*. Grape identity, fruit source, and cellar
handling explain the difference between these individual wines.
