# Mujuretuli

Mujuretuli is a red grape associated with Racha in western Georgia,
especially the semi-sweet wine [Khvanchkara](../regions/khvanchkara.md),
where it is blended with
[Aleksandrouli](alexandrouli.md). It also appears in dry varietal wines,
which provide an opportunity to encounter it outside that established
partnership.

## A vineyard partner

Georgia's National Wine Agency describes Mujuretuli as commonly
interplanted with Aleksandrouli around Ambrolauri and Tsageri, with
single-variety vineyards less usual. The two names identify distinct
grapes, even though their shared vineyard and cellar history makes them
easy to discuss together.

Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo describes Mujuretuli as late-ripening, with
elongated dark berries, colourless pulp, and susceptibility to both downy
and powdery mildew. Late ripening gives the grower a long season over
which to protect the crop. In the cellar,
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) extracts colour from the skins;
Mujuretuli is not a red-fleshed [teinturier](../concepts/teinturier.md).

## Dry Mujuretuli

Gonadze Vineyards' 2022 Mujuretuli supplies a documented example of a dry
wine made entirely from the grape. Its International Wine & Spirit
Competition entry identifies it as 100% Mujuretuli, with stainless-steel
maturation.[^1] This establishes a concrete alternative to encountering
the grape only in a sweet blend, without requiring a fixed claim about
what each partner contributes to every Khvanchkara.

The comparison is useful because [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md)
affects how acidity and tannin are perceived. A dry varietal bottling and a
semi-sweet blend differ in composition and fermentation outcome; their
contrast cannot be attributed solely to the presence or absence of
Aleksandrouli. For readers exploring Racha, both styles belong in the
picture of the region's wine.

[^1]: International Wine & Spirit Competition, “Gonadze Vineyards,
    Mujuretuli 2022,” entry details, accessed 21 September 2026.
