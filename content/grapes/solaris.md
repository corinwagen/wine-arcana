# Solaris

Solaris is an early-ripening white grape that has become useful to growers
in northern Europe. Its capacity to accumulate sugar in a short season helps
explain its role in Swedish wine, while [disease resistance](../concepts/disease-resistance.md) addresses a
separate challenge: keeping the crop healthy in a humid climate.

![Green grape bunches hang along a trained vine in a sunlit vineyard.](../../media/images/grapes/solaris.jpg "Solaris at Chateaux Luna vineyard in Lysekil, Sweden.")

## Vineyard traits

The State Viticultural Institute in Freiburg records the pedigree as
Merzling crossed with the breeding selection Gm 6493.[^1] Its profile describes
very early ripening, vigorous growth, large leaves, and a tendency toward
a dense canopy. Early budbreak also makes the timing of spring cold relevant.

Managing that growth helps expose the crop and maintain airflow. Early
maturity gives a grower options, but the harvest decision still has to
balance sugar, [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), aroma, and berry condition.
The breeder describes wines with body and fruit, including the possibility
of sweet styles.

## Swedish expressions

Kullabergs Vingård gives Solaris a central role in several wines. The estate
explains that it uses the grape for ripeness and depth in blends, alongside
[Souvignier Gris](souvignier-gris.md) and aromatic Muscaris. It also documents
Solaris in a traditional-method sparkling blend and in separately selected
botrytized wines.[^2]

The estate's 2025 vintage account records a cold start to summer, humid
conditions, canopy opening, and a lower-than-expected crop. This is a useful
counterweight to an uncomplicated story of northern opportunity: a variety
that ripens early still faces difficult flowering weather and wet summers.
Swedish Solaris represents an established set of grower and cellar choices,
with considerable variation between seasons and styles.
