# Nerello Cappuccio

Nerello Cappuccio is a dark-grape name closely associated with
[Etna](../regions/etna.md), where it accompanies
[Nerello Mascalese](nerello-mascalese.md) in red and rosé wines. Its customary
role is to bring deeper colour and a gentler tannic contribution to a blend
whose principal variety is Mascalese. The name also carries an important
identity problem: historically, different vines have been called Cappuccio.

That makes both the local wine tradition and the identity of the actual
planting relevant. A description of a named producer's Cappuccio is firmer
ground than a universal account of every vine bearing the name.

## A name with more than one identity

The 2021 *Parentage Atlas of Italian Grapevine Varieties* explains that
Nerello Cappuccio in its dataset corresponds to [Carignan](carignan.md).
The authors cite a Sicilian survey that identified approximately 70% of
vineyards carrying the Cappuccio name as Carignan, with other cultivars in
the remainder.[^identity] This does not establish the identity of every
present-day estate's planting. It does mean that treating Cappuccio as an
unambiguous biological variety, or treating every occurrence of the name as
an interchangeable synonym for Carignan, loses information.

The Etna consortium also records the name Nerello Mantellato, referring to
the covering formed by the foliage. Neither that traditional explanation
nor the shared word *Nerello* establishes a genetic relationship to Mascalese.

## Etna blends and varietal wine

The consortium's published account allows Cappuccio as a minority component
of Etna Rosso and Rosato, with Mascalese predominant. It describes Cappuccio
wines as relatively deeply ruby-coloured, fresh, and gently tannic, with
herbal, spicy, and fruit notes. Those are regional descriptions of the
material used under this name, rather than a substitute for identification.

Benanti provides a useful contemporary reference through its varietal Terre
Siciliane bottling. Its technical sheet places the vineyard in Contrada
Cavalieri on Etna's southwestern side, at about 900 metres. The estate
describes October harvesting, fermentation with roughly two weeks of
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), and maturation in stainless steel.
The absence of barrel maturation makes it a useful counterpart to oak-aged
Etna blends when considering fruit, acidity, and tannin.

## Site and cultivation

For Benanti's site, elevation, ventilation, and a relatively dry southwestern
exposure are central to the growing conditions. This is a mountain vineyard
within Sicily: [altitude](../concepts/altitude.md) helps explain why a broad
assumption of uniformly hot island viticulture is inadequate. The estate
uses spur-pruned cordons, illustrating a contemporary training choice rather
than evidence that all old Etna plantings share one form.
