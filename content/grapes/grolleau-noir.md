# Grolleau Noir

Grolleau Noir is a Loire Valley grape closely associated with light-bodied
[rosé](../styles/rose.md). Its generous production and relatively modest colour
and alcohol have made it useful for approachable wines, including sparkling
wine. With controlled crops, it can also give supple, fruit-led reds.

## A Loire grape in retreat

Plantgrape places its probable origin in Touraine. The grape remains associated
with the middle and western Loire, including Anjou, with smaller presences in
Loire-Atlantique and Vendée. The name Grolleau commonly means this dark-skinned
variety; Grolleau Gris is a separate name encountered in the region's vineyards.

Its twentieth-century contraction was substantial. Plantgrape records about
11,400 hectares in France in 1958 and 2,070 in 2018.[^1] Those dates describe
a long decline in planted area, even though individual growers continue to
find uses for the grape outside its familiar rosé role.

## Productivity and wine character

Grolleau is fertile enough that short pruning and a limited crop load are
important. Its young shoots are vulnerable to wind, and stalk rot and
excoriose, a fungal disease affecting shoots, are concerns. It can still crop
after spring frost, a useful trait in the Loire, though that does not protect
the initial growth from damage.

The wines tend toward light colour and body. Red-fruit aromas can be
accompanied by a spicy note, but the central distinction is the wine's supple
structure. In blends, Grolleau can lighten the firmer impression of
[Cabernet Franc](cabernet-franc.md). This gives the grower a reason to retain
it even where Cabernet Franc is the better-known red variety.

Rosé winemaking emphasizes a different part of that range. Separating the
juice from skins early limits extraction; keeping the skins through red
fermentation gives more colour and texture. The sweetness of a rosé is a
further cellar decision, determined by [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md).
A grape used in a soft, sweetish rosé can also produce a dry red.

## A place for light reds

Growers' interest in Grolleau red wine is visible in individual bottlings.
For example, importer Lo-Fi Wines documents a version of Domaine des
Sablonnettes' Les Copains d'Abord made entirely from Grolleau, with destemming,
skin contact, and tank maturation.[^2] This is an example of a production
approach, rather than a specification for every vintage of that wine.

Such wines make Grolleau's lightness a useful starting point. Crop control
provides fruit with enough substance for red winemaking, while the duration
and force of [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) shape the extraction.
