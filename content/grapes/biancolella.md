# Biancolella

Biancolella is a white grape of coastal Campania and its islands, especially
[Ischia](../regions/ischia.md). It makes fresh, fruit-scented wines whose expression can range from
a direct young white to a more textured, lees-matured bottling. The island's
steep vineyards are central to understanding the effort behind these wines.

## Farming an island landscape

Rauscedo describes a vigorous vine with early budbreak and ripening toward the
middle to later part of the season. Its recommendations distinguish shorter
pruning on dry ground from longer pruning where soil moisture and fertility
support more growth. The practical question is how much crop the site can
bring to maturity, a relationship explored in [crop load](../concepts/crop-load.md).
The profile also records susceptibility to downy mildew.

Casa D'Ambra's Frassitelli vineyard makes the island terrain tangible.
The producer describes steep, south-to-southwest-facing slopes supported by
*parracine*, dry-stone walls built from green tuff. A rack railway helps move
people and materials through the vineyard. Its published elevation range is
400–550 metres.[^1] This is hillside farming above the sea, with the
infrastructure and labour that steep slopes require.

## Blends and a single-site wine

D'Ambra's regular Biancolella provides a useful example of a varietal-labelled
wine that includes other grapes: its sheet lists 85% Biancolella, with
[Forastera](forastera.md), San Lunardo and Uva Rilla supplying the remainder.
Frassitelli, by contrast, is listed as entirely Biancolella. These figures
describe the producer's blends, rather than a summary of appellation law.

For Frassitelli, the estate describes temperature-controlled white
vinification followed by prolonged contact with yeast sediment and
*bâtonnage*, the stirring of those lees. Maturation takes place in steel.
That approach can develop palate texture through
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md), while keeping wood flavour out of
the comparison.

Biancolella's contemporary role on Ischia therefore includes both the island
white blend and a more narrowly identified vineyard wine. Its floral and
fruit character offers a starting point, but site, accompanying varieties
and cellar treatment explain why one bottle can feel different from another.

[^1]: Casa D'Ambra, “Frassitelli,” vineyard description and technical sheet.
