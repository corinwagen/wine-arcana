# Melon de Bourgogne

Melon de Bourgogne is a white *Vitis vinifera* variety whose public identity is
closely tied to [Muscadet](../regions/muscadet.md), the group of appellations around Nantes. That
association can obscure the grape itself: the name on the bottle usually
identifies the place and wine category, while the variety supplies a relatively
discreet aromatic frame for freshness, site, and élevage. Its most distinctive
expression is therefore a meeting between a restrained grape and the Loire
vineyards where it became established.

## History and identity

Plantgrape places Melon’s origin in Bourgogne–Franche-Comté and reports that
published genetic analyses indicate a cross between Pinot and Gouais blanc.[^1]

The variety is commonly styled Melon de Bourgogne in English. *Muscadet* names
the appellation and wine category here; in the INAO record for Muscadet Coteaux
de la Loire, its wines are made from the single variety Melon B.[^2]

## Viticulture

Melon has small to medium-sized clusters and small berries. Plantgrape places
budbreak about two days after Chasselas and maturity in the second period,
about two weeks after Chasselas. It describes the variety as suited to
clay-siliceous soils and fairly cool areas, with moderate long pruning or, in
some circumstances, short pruning. Its disease profile is uneven: it is fairly
susceptible to downy mildew and very susceptible to grey rot, while being less
susceptible to powdery mildew and eutypa dieback.[^1]

Those traits help explain the Loire association. Fairly cool conditions suit the
variety and can preserve the freshness expected in dry Muscadet, while its
sensitivity to rot makes weather and harvest conditions important. Plantgrape
describes the wines as fresh, light, balanced, and discreetly aromatic.[^1]

## Wine character

Melon has discreet varietal aromas. This gives production and site more room to
show: harvest conditions, fermentation, lees contact, and the vineyard setting
can change the wine’s texture and aromatic detail without a strong varietal
perfume dominating it.

In Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire, INAO describes delicate, mainly floral aromas,
occasional mineral notes, and a balance oriented toward freshness.[^2]

## Where it is grown

Melon’s defining modern home is the Muscadet area in the southern Loire, near
Nantes. INAO describes Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire as extending along the
sloping banks of the Loire between Nantes and Angers, where poor, stony soils
and the local climate are associated with fresh wines and delicate aromas.[^2]
The appellation was recognised in 1936, and the wider Muscadet family includes
other appellations.

## In the cellar

Plantgrape says that dry-white vinification has good aptitude for conservation
on [lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md).

For Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire, *sur lie* wines remain on their fine
fermentation lees until bottling or their first shipment from the winemaking
cellar. Under the amendment published in April 2026, bottling takes place
within the appellation area or its defined nearby area, between 1 March of the
year after harvest and 30 June of the second year after harvest.[^3]

Lees ageing can give Melon's light wines more roundness and texture. The
appellation document connects this effect with mannoproteins and other
compounds released as yeast cells break down, while limited handling and
retained carbon dioxide help protect the wine from [oxidation](../concepts/oxidation.md).[^3]
