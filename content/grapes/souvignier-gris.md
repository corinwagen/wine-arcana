# Souvignier Gris

Souvignier Gris is a pink-skinned grape used principally for white wine.
Bred at Freiburg, it combines useful [disease resistance](../concepts/disease-resistance.md) with the ability
to make wines ranging from relatively quiet and textural to more aromatic.
The colour of the berry also makes skin-contact methods an interesting
part of its contemporary use.

## Identity and cultivation

The State Viticultural Institute's current profile identifies the parents
as [Seyval Blanc](seyval-blanc.md) and Zähringer.[^1] Older material naming [Cabernet Sauvignon](cabernet-sauvignon.md)
and Bronner preserves an earlier pedigree and should be read with that
correction in mind.

The breeder describes upright, vigorous growth, firm berries, relatively
late budbreak, and the tendency to produce secondary bunches. The crop and
[canopy](../concepts/canopy-management.md) consequently need attention even
when resistance reduces the disease burden.

French experimental work compiled by the resistant-variety observatory
records wines ranging from citrus-like to more tropical impressions.
It also reports that [powdery mildew](../concepts/powdery-mildew.md) can progress under strong pressure
in untreated vines. Resistance provides an agricultural advantage with
limits set by the pathogen and the growing conditions.

## An Alpine cellar example

Thomas Niedermayr in [Alto Adige](../regions/alto-adige.md) describes using Souvignier Gris for still
wines, skin-fermented wines, and blends. The estate emphasizes its relatively
neutral character among the resistant varieties it grows. This leaves room
for texture and the effects of [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) to
shape the wine.

That range helps explain its appeal beyond the breeding station. Growers
can pursue reduced disease susceptibility while making stylistic choices
that belong to their own place and cellar.

[^1]: Staatliches Weinbauinstitut Freiburg, “Souvignier Gris,” current breeder
    profile; compare the older pedigree in the French observatory sheet.
