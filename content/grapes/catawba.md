# Catawba

Catawba is a pink-to-red-skinned American grape with an important place
in nineteenth-century wine history and a continuing role in regional
white and [rosé](../styles/rose.md) wines. It connects the early Ohio wine industry with
current producers exploring American grape traditions.

![Historical colour plate labelled Catawba, showing reddish grape berries and leaves.](../../media/images/grapes/catawba.jpg "Catawba plate from The Grapes of New York (1908).")

## Ohio's sparkling wine

In Julia Fine's historical account for Harvard's Arnold Arboretum,
John Adlum helped distribute Catawba cuttings and Nicholas Longworth
built a substantial wine business around the grape near Cincinnati.
Sparkling Catawba became particularly successful in the mid-nineteenth
century.[^1] The variety's exact place of origin remains less certain
than that documented commercial history.

Disease was also central to the story. [Black rot](../concepts/black-rot.md) damaged crops and
contributed to the decline of the Cincinnati industry. Catawba's American
ancestry did not make it immune to an American pathogen.

## Continuing regional styles

Missouri's wine organization describes fragrant pink wines with
strawberry-like impressions and noticeable sweetness. The University
of Missouri also records dry and sweet uses, together with late ripening
and vigorous growth. Those are useful reminders that a grape does not
have one compulsory fermentation endpoint.

Fine's account discusses renewed interest from Cincinnati's Skeleton
Root and New York producers. These projects place the grape's history
inside contemporary wine culture. Comparing their wines with a sweet
regional rosé draws attention to pressing, fermentation, and
[residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md), while the risks of
[canopy crowding](../concepts/canopy-management.md) remain relevant in
the vineyard.

[^1]: Julia Fine, “Catawba: Back to the Future of the American Wine Industry,”
    *Arnoldia*, historical account and cited correspondence.
