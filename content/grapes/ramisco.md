# Ramisco

Ramisco is a dark-skinned Portuguese wine grape closely associated with
[Colares](../regions/colares.md), near Sintra on the Atlantic coast west of
Lisbon. Its wines can combine substantial [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md)
with firm, astringent [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md). Long maturation is
central to understanding the traditional red: the wine released to drink may
already have spent years in the cellar.

## Viticulture

Colares's sand vineyards preserve ungrafted vines, rooted in the underlying
layer beneath the sand. Their survival through the nineteenth-century
[phylloxera](../concepts/phylloxera.md) epidemic reflects the sandy vineyard
environment, rather than demonstrated resistance in Ramisco itself.[^roots]

The vines spread close to the ground, sheltered from Atlantic winds by
screens. Adega Regional de Colares describes lifting the clusters with small
cane supports toward the end of summer. This labour keeps the fruit above the
sand while retaining the protection of a low vine form. Maritime humidity,
mist, and moderate temperatures make ripening and healthy fruit important
late-season concerns; the coöperative harvested its documented 2008 Ramisco
in the final week of September.

## Wine character and maturation

Ramisco's young astringency can soften with age. Its colour also changes from
ruby toward browner tones, while fruit aromas develop alongside more mature
aromatic impressions. Wines of Portugal emphasizes the combination of acid,
tannin, and ageing capacity, with the qualification that successful long
ageing depends on a well-made wine.

Adega Regional's 2008 bottling illustrates how much work precedes release.
It fermented in stainless steel with controlled temperature and pumpovers,
then spent five years in large wooden vats and another year in used small
oak barrels before bottling in February 2016.[^cellar] These are that
bottling's choices, rather than a universal Ramisco recipe. The age, size,
and type of vessel help determine the wine's exposure to wood and oxygen;
extended [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) need not produce a
dominant new-oak flavour.

## Where it is grown

Colares remains Ramisco's defining home. Wines of Portugal identifies housing
pressure and demanding vineyard work as threats to its small growing area.
Continued cultivation therefore depends on maintaining the coastal vineyard
system as well as preserving the variety.

Ramilo provides a contemporary example alongside the coöperative: its Quinta
do Cameijo has low, ungrafted vines in Colares sand, exposed to the region's
wind and humidity. The family also farms a separate estate inland toward
Mafra. That distinction matters when reading producer information: a winery
working in Colares may also make wines from other places and grapes.
