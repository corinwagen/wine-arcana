# Baco Noir

Baco Noir is a French-bred red [hybrid grape](../concepts/hybrid-grapes.md)
with an enduring Canadian identity. Ontario offers a particularly useful
introduction: Henry of Pelham continues to bottle it from established
vineyards, showing how a hybrid can become a house speciality rather than
simply a substitute for another grape.

Its dark colour can suggest a heavily tannic wine, but colour, acidity and
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) are separate properties. Baco can combine
intense colour with relatively modest tannin and conspicuous freshness.

## Origins

François Baco crossed Folle Blanche with *Vitis riparia* in France in 1902.
The selection subsequently became Baco No. 1, or Baco Noir. UC Davis's
Foundation Plant Services records this parentage; it is an interspecific
cross, combining a European wine variety with an American species.[^identity]

That history explains the name and broad ancestry. It does not establish
that the vine resists every disease, or that all sites will produce the
same kind of wine.

## Growth and ripening

Perennia's Nova Scotia guidance describes a vigorous vine with early
budbreak, making [spring frost](../concepts/spring-frost.md) a concern.
It recommends the province's warmest sites and careful canopy work for
ripening. Those are regional qualifications: Canadian cultivation does
not mean that Baco fits every short growing season.

[Canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) concerns the relationship
between leaves, light and fruit. A vigorous vine needs enough exposed leaf
area to ripen its crop without burying the bunches in shaded growth.

## Ontario and the cellar

Henry of Pelham provides a concrete example in the
[Niagara Peninsula](../regions/niagara-peninsula.md). Its 2025 Old Vines
Baco Noir account traces some plantings to 1984. The accompanying Reserve
technical sheet describes silty clay soils, gentle cap management during
fermentation, exclusion of press wine from the final blend and maturation
in American oak. These are choices for that bottling, not a compulsory
Baco recipe.[^pelham]

They also show why vineyard and cellar descriptions belong together.
[Maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) determines how the fermenting wine
interacts with skins; [pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) yields another
fraction that the winemaker can keep separate. Oak then contributes a
further layer. The producer's dark-fruit and toasted-oak description
therefore describes both grape and handling, rather than a flavour that
every Baco wine must reproduce.
