# Nerello Mascalese

Nerello Mascalese is a dark-skinned *Vitis vinifera* grape most closely
associated with the slopes of [Mount Etna](../regions/etna.md) in eastern Sicily. It can make
relatively pale reds with substantial tannin and acidity. The Italian Vitis
Database reports modest anthocyanin pigmentation alongside a larger total
phenolic content, helping explain why colour gives an incomplete impression
of the wine's structure.

The name refers to Mascali, an old centre of viticulture on the mountain's
eastern side. Cultivation also extends to the Messina area, where it
contributes to Faro, elsewhere in Sicily, and across the Strait of Messina
in Calabria.

## The vine on Etna

Nerello Mascalese is vigorous and can produce a large crop, although
productivity varies from year to year. Poor weather can leave small,
incompletely developed berries, while excessive vigour can interfere with
fruit set. Growers have traditionally used bush vines with short pruning;
trellised systems also suit the variety when pruning keeps crop potential
under control.

Its berries have substantial skins. The nursery Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo
reports susceptibility to grey rot and sour rot in damp conditions, as
well as stress under prolonged drought. A dry site still needs sufficient
water to carry the vine through ripening. Bunches and ripening uniformity
also vary among the material cultivated under the name.

Etna extends the season beyond the harvest dates familiar in warmer parts
of Sicily. Its official geographical account places the start of Nerello
harvesting generally in October, with the highest vineyards sometimes
continuing into November. Elevation and exposure affect both the warmth
available for ripening and the weather the fruit faces before picking.

## Wine character and extraction

Red-fruit and floral impressions are common, with spice contributing in
some wines. The combination of freshness and firm
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) can make young examples feel more forceful
than their translucent colour suggests. Anthocyanins provide much of a
young red wine's colour; tannins contribute a different property, the
drying sensation called astringency.

Maceration extracts both groups of compounds from the grapes. The duration
and intensity of skin contact therefore help shape the balance between
fruit and grip. [Maturation](../concepts/bottle-aging.md) changes that
balance further as pigments and tannins react and fresh-fruit aromas
evolve. Wines made for earlier drinking and wines given longer maturation
can both express the variety.

## In blends and other styles

Etna Rosso and Rosato require at least 80% Nerello Mascalese. Nerello
Cappuccio is permitted as a blending partner. The
specification also requires at least 80% Nerello Mascalese for Etna's
sparkling wines, which can be white or pink.[^1] Pressing the dark grapes
with little skin contact allows a pale juice to form the base of a white
sparkling wine.

A vineyard survey discussed in a 2021 genetic study found several varieties
grown under the name Nerello Cappuccio, chiefly [Carignan](carignan.md).
The name therefore needs care when identifying plant material.[^2]
