# Schioppettino

Schioppettino is a red grape of [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](../regions/friuli.md),
especially Prepotto,
Albana, and the Cialla valley near the Slovenian border. Also called Ribolla
nera, it makes dry reds in which peppery aroma and freshness can be as
important as weight or color.

## Place and revival

The Colli Orientali consortium identifies Prepotto as a central home of the
grape and describes its present-day expression as a still, dry wine.
Ronchi di Cialla's account of the local revival credits the Rapuzzi family,
the Nonino family, and local support in the 1970s. This producer history is
useful for understanding the deliberate effort behind the grape's continuing
presence, without treating survival as the work of one person alone.

Cialla's wooded hills and relatively cool conditions provide an important
local reference. Ronchi di Cialla describes vineyards on the more favorable
exposures, with forests covering much of the surrounding valley. The
landscape combines the need to achieve ripeness with a tradition that values
a fresh, spicy expression.

## Pepper and structure

The characteristic pepper note has a chemical basis. Research by Fulvio
Mattivi and colleagues identified substantial concentrations of rotundone,
a potent pepper-smelling compound, in Schioppettino wines.[^1] This provides
a useful connection with [Syrah](syrah.md) and
[Grüner Veltliner](gruner-veltliner.md), while leaving considerable variation
between vineyards and bottles.

Within Friuli, Schioppettino offers a useful comparison with
[Pignolo](pignolo.md) and [Tazzelenghe](tazzelenghe.md). The latter two are
particularly associated with forceful tannic structure; Schioppettino's
peppery aromatic identity gives readers another way to approach the region's
local reds.

[^1]: Fulvio Mattivi and colleagues, “Vini particolarmente pepati: da conoscere,
    comunicare, valorizzare,” *Infowine* (2012), discussion of rotundone in
    Schioppettino and other varieties.
