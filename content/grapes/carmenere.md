# Carménère

Carménère is a red Bordeaux variety that became closely associated with Chile
after vineyards thought to contain [Merlot](merlot.md) were found to include
it. Its wines can combine substantial colour and texture with a distinctly
herbal aroma. The balance between fruit and that green character depends on
the growing season, planting material, and harvest decisions.

## History and identity

The University of California's Foundation Plant Services describes
[Cabernet Franc](cabernet-franc.md) and Gros Cabernet as its likely parents.
That qualification matters: the proposed pedigree is less secure than some
widely established grape parentages.[^1]

Recognition of Carménère among Chilean Merlot during the 1990s changed how
existing vineyards were understood. Chile's Instituto de Investigaciones
Agropecuarias (INIA) distinguishes the
ampelographic identification by Jean-Michel Boursiquot from the subsequent
genetic work involving INIA, the University of California, Davis, and
Montpellier. Chile's modern Carménère story therefore includes the
reidentification of vines already in production, alongside later planting.

Confusion also occurred in Italy with Cabernet Franc. Foundation Plant Services
records Italian material imported under that name and subsequently identified
as Carménère by DNA analysis. Similar-looking vines and inherited nursery
names could conceal a different variety well beyond Chile.

## Viticulture

Carménère is vigorous, with relatively unfruitful basal buds that make pruning
choices consequential. Poor flowering weather can reduce fruit set. An
abundant canopy consequently does not guarantee an abundant crop, and
[canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) must account for both
growth and fruit exposure.

Chilean regional guidance emphasizes its long ripening cycle and the value of
a dry end to the season. This is a regional comparison: Plantgrape classifies
it as midseason against its French reference variety, Chasselas. Ripening
categories depend on the reference and conditions; they are not universal
harvest dates.

Ripe fruit can yield deeply coloured, tannic wines with relatively low [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md).
Plantgrape also notes possible bitterness and herbal character when maturity
is inadequate. Waiting longer has to be weighed against the balance of the
fruit already on the vine.

## Wine character

Methoxypyrazines help explain Carménère's green, vegetal aroma. Research by
Alfredo Belancic and Eduardo Agosin found substantial differences between
clones and growing seasons, with climate especially important in their study.
Harvest maturity alone did not explain the variation.[^2] A later picking date
is consequently an incomplete answer to a strongly green wine.

This aromatic component can form part of the wine's character alongside ripe
fruit. Its prominence matters: a restrained herbal accent and an overwhelming
vegetal aroma produce very different impressions even when both wines are
correctly identified as Carménère.

## Where it is grown

Chile is its defining modern association. Cachapoal and Colchagua, the two
principal subdivisions of the Rapel Valley, both have important plantings.
Cachapoal includes cooler Andean-influenced sites and warmer areas toward Lake
Rapel and the coastal hills. Colchagua likewise varies with coastal influence
and local exposure. These differences give growers a range of ripening
conditions within the
variety's principal Chilean growing area.
