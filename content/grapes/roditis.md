# Roditis

Roditis is a pink-skinned Greek *Vitis vinifera* grape used principally for
white wine. It is widely grown, with an important home around Patras in the
northern Peloponnese and further plantings in central and northern Greece.
Its wines range from light everyday whites to more concentrated examples
from mountain vineyards and restrained crops.

This range helps explain its uneven reputation. A productive vineyard can
supply plentiful juice without much concentration. In a suitable site,
controlled yields and careful harvesting can give the grape enough fruit
and [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) to sustain a more distinctive wine. Citrus character and a
relatively unobtrusive perfume leave room for site and cellar choices to
shape the result.

## Site, crop, and ripening

The Winemakers of North Greece account describes substantial variation
within Roditis and among the climates where it grows. Elevated vineyards
and soils of moderate fertility are useful settings, and ripening is
relatively late. A regional name therefore supplies only part of the
picture: the particular planting, crop, and harvest date matter.

Patras provides a reference for varietal white wine. Elsewhere, Roditis is
used in blends with grapes including [Savvatiano](savvatiano.md) and
[Assyrtiko](assyrtiko.md), and it also contributes to retsina. Those uses
place it within several local traditions rather than one narrowly defined
wine style.

## Mountain fruit in the cellar

A contemporary example is Tetramythos's Roditis from the Peloponnese.
Importer Œnopole's technical account for the 2025 wine describes fruit from
rocky limestone soils at 850 metres, fermentation with indigenous yeast in
steel, completed [malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md),
and six months in steel.[^tetramythos] The wine illustrates an approach in
which the vineyard supplies freshness and the cellar moderates its acid
profile without adding barrel flavour.

Other producers use short [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) to build
texture. Lees contact and [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) are separate choices: a white can
gain breadth while remaining unoaked. Roditis's coloured skins also permit
[rosé](../styles/rose.md) or more skin-derived colour when extraction is extended, although
white vinification remains its principal role.

[^tetramythos]: Œnopole, “Domaine Tetramythos Roditis 2025,” vintage-specific
    vineyard and production sheet, accessed 17 September 2026.
