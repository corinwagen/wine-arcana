# Chinuri

Chinuri is a white Georgian wine grape closely associated with Kartli.
Its fresh [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) suits both still and sparkling
wines, while skin contact gives another route to texture. It is also
known as Chinebuli.

Kartli is a useful regional counterpoint to the Kakhetian wines made from
[Rkatsiteli](rkatsiteli.md) and [Kisi](kisi.md). Chinuri's contemporary
range includes pale dry whites and amber qvevri wines, so grape, region,
and extraction all deserve attention.

## Ripening and sparkling wine

Chinuri is harvested relatively late in Kartli, commonly around the end
of September and beginning of October. Harvest timing affects the balance
between retained acidity and accumulated sugar. Grapes intended for
sparkling wine can be picked with a different balance in mind from those
destined for a fuller still wine.

The variety has an established role in Atenuri sparkling wines, alongside
[Goruli Mtsvane](goruli-mtsvane.md). The partnership places Chinuri within
a local blending tradition, as well as its modern varietal bottlings.

## One grape, two approaches to skins

Iago's Wine provides a particularly clear comparison. The winery's
Chinuri White is made without skin contact, while its Chinuri Amber
spends six months with skins in qvevri.[^1] Its descriptions contrast
fresh apple and citrus in the white with dried apricot and a tannic
element in the amber wine.

These are useful examples because the variety remains the same while a
major production choice changes. [Maceration](../concepts/maceration.md)
allows compounds from skins and seeds to enter the wine. It can add
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) and deepen colour, producing the grip
associated with [skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md).
Pressing before that extended contact keeps the emphasis closer to juice
and fermentation.

Chinuri need not produce an especially heavy amber wine. The Georgian
National Tourism Administration describes light to medium-bodied examples
and notes how skin contact changes their fruit and tannic character.
The amount of solids, ripeness, and handling help explain differences
within either approach.

[^1]: Iago's Wine, “Chinuri White” and “Chinuri Amber,” producer accounts,
