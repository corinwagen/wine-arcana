# Recantina

Recantina is a dark-skinned grape of the Asolo and Montello hills in Veneto.
Its revival adds a local red to a landscape internationally associated with
[Glera](glera.md) and Prosecco, and with established plantings of Merlot and
Cabernet. The wines combine dark colour, fruit and spice with a noticeable
tannic grip, although cellar choices can make them quite approachable young.

## Recovering a particular vine

The historical name needs care. Veneto Agricoltura's recovery work distinguished
three vines called Recantina: *peccolo rosso*, *peccolo scuro*, and *Forner*,
named after the holding where it was preserved. The Forner material is the
subject of the modern revival discussed here. References to Recantina in old
documents therefore cannot all be assigned automatically to the same vine.[^1]

That distinction illustrates what saving an old grape involves: finding
surviving plants, checking their identity, propagating them, and testing the
wines. Keeping a familiar name in circulation is only part of the work.

## Vineyard and wine

The regional technical profile describes medium vigour, fairly loose bunches,
thick dark skins and ripening in the middle-to-later part of the season. It
favours long pruning and records useful sugar, acid and pigment accumulation.
Those observations come from recovered material under local conditions, rather
than establishing performance in every climate.

Colour and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) make extraction and maturation
important stylistic decisions. A deeply coloured Recantina need not spend
years in new oak: at Serafini e Vidotto, the published method uses October
harvesting and at least six months in steel. Its description emphasizes fruit,
pepper and slightly rustic tannins. This offers a concrete example of a wine
intended to retain youthful fruit while allowing some settling before release.

## A contemporary local revival

The Asolo Montello consortium includes Recantina among the local varieties of
Montello Asolo DOC. Its present significance is concentrated in these hills,
where producers have given a scarce grape a commercial reason to survive.
Pat del Colmel explicitly uses the Forner name on its Recantina bottlings;
Serafini e Vidotto offers another current varietal expression.

Together, these wines provide a useful comparison with Veneto's
[Raboso Piave](raboso-piave.md): two local red-grape traditions whose identities
are clearer when named individually than when absorbed into a generic account
of northeastern Italian reds.

[^1]: Veneto Agricoltura, *Vecchi vitigni del Veneto*, “Recantina Forner,”
    distinguishing three recovered varieties under the historical name.
