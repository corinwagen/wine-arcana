# Müller-Thurgau

Müller-Thurgau is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape bred by Hermann Müller,
a Swiss researcher from the canton of Thurgau. Early ripening, generous
crops, and a lightly muscat-like perfume helped it spread through Germany
and neighbouring wine countries. Its wines commonly emphasize fresh fruit
and relatively gentle [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), with styles spanning dry and lightly sweet.

## Breeding and names

Müller developed the variety at Geisenheim in the 1880s, and work continued
at Wädenswil in Switzerland. The Vitis International Variety Catalogue
records [Riesling](riesling.md) and Madeleine Royale as its genetically
confirmed parents.[^1]

The names Rivaner and Riesling-Silvaner preserve the earlier belief that
[Silvaner](silvaner.md) supplied the second parent. Both denote
Müller-Thurgau. German producers often use Rivaner for a youthful, dry,
light style; the name itself identifies the variety, while the label's
sweetness indication describes the finished wine.

## Early ripening and water supply

Müller-Thurgau can ripen in sites where a later grape would struggle to
finish its season. Its productivity and relatively modest need for a warm
exposure made it useful across a wide range of German vineyards. Deep,
fresh soils suit it better than dry, shallow ones because the vigorous
vine is sensitive to water shortage.

The early harvest brings its own decisions. In hot years, acidity can
fall enough to give a broad, heavy impression. In cooler conditions, the
fruit may retain a livelier balance. Growers therefore follow aroma,
acidity, and berry condition alongside sugar accumulation when choosing
when to pick.

Bunches range from loose to compact, and the grape is susceptible to
botrytis and downy mildew. A ventilated canopy helps fruit dry after rain.
The Bavarian viticultural institute describes selection for looser bunches
and lower yields as a priority in clonal work.

## Perfume and drinking style

The characteristic aroma is gently floral, often with apple or pear and
a muscat-like note. Steel fermentation and maturation are common choices
for preserving that youthful perfume. Most ordinary bottlings are intended
for drinking in their first few years; some richer or sweet examples
have a longer life.

[Residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) and acidity jointly shape
the impression of softness. A wine with modest acidity needs relatively
little remaining sugar to taste rounded. Some producers also use
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) to develop texture while retaining
the grape's fruit.

Germany's principal growing areas include Rheinhessen, Baden, Pfalz,
[Franken](../regions/franken.md), and the [Mosel](../regions/mosel.md).
Cultivation also extends into Switzerland, Austria, and northern Italy.
