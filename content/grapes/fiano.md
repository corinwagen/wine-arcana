# Fiano

Fiano is a white *Vitis vinifera* grape most closely associated with the
hills around Avellino in Campania. It makes dry wines that can combine a
substantial middle palate with freshness, and whose aromas often develop
with bottle age. Its usefulness extends from young, fruit-scented whites to
wines in which floral, nutty, and honeyed impressions unfold over time.

Fiano di Avellino identifies its protected inland wine. The grape also grows
elsewhere, including other parts of Campania.

## Hills, climate, and the vine

The Avellino hills experience cold winters, spring frost, and marked summer
differences between daytime and nighttime temperatures. Their inland setting
helps explain how a southern Italian white can retain freshness through
ripening. Clay-rich soils in parts of the zone also hold water through the
dry summer, although drainage, soil depth, and slope vary between vineyards.

Fiano is vigorous. Vivai Cooperativi Rauscedo recommends comparatively dry,
less fertile hillsides and suitable pruning to manage its growth. Fertile
sites can demand more work on the [canopy](../concepts/canopy-management.md).
The grape has fairly substantial skins, and its bunches vary in compactness.
Wet spring weather can produce uneven berry development, while powdery
mildew deserves particular attention.

Harvest timing varies with site and season. Rauscedo's clonal descriptions
also differ in [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and suitability for longer maturation, reflecting
the variation in plant material available to growers.

## Aroma and development

Young Fiano can suggest flowers and fresh fruit. Hazelnut is a familiar
association, but studies assembled by the University of Naples and Regione
Campania show a broader and changing aromatic profile. In their bottle-ageing
observations, apple and banana impressions became less frequently cited,
while several floral, nutty, and honeyed impressions became more prominent.
Those results describe the wines studied, without establishing a timetable
for every bottle.[^1]

Aroma depends partly on compounds already present in grapes and partly on
compounds formed or released during fermentation and maturation. The same
research programme examined both free aroma compounds and bound precursors
that can yield odorants later. This provides a useful explanation for Fiano's
ability to change after its initial fruit aromas subside. Nutty or honeyed
aroma can occur in a dry wine.

## Fiano di Avellino

The production specification published by the Irpinia consortium requires
at least 85% Fiano, with a limited balance of [Greco](greco.md),
[Coda di Volpe](coda-di-volpe.md), and
[Trebbiano Toscano](ugni-blanc.md). Its area includes Lapio, Montefredane, Summonte, and other
communes around Avellino.[^2]
