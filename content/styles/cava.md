# Cava

Cava is a protected Spanish sparkling wine made by the [traditional
method](traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md): the second fermentation occurs in
the bottle, followed by a period on the yeast sediment and its removal through
disgorgement. Its identity combines geographical origin, grapes, and production
requirements.

The Penedès area of Catalonia is its principal historical and production centre,
but the denomination includes delimited areas elsewhere in Spain. The protected
name applies to wine from those defined areas.

## Grapes and base wines

[Macabeo](../grapes/macabeo.md), [Xarel·lo](../grapes/xarel-lo.md), and
[Parellada](../grapes/parellada.md) form the familiar traditional combination.
Their different growing habits and base-wine properties give producers options
for blending. Chardonnay and other authorized varieties broaden the range, while
red grapes including Pinot Noir, Garnacha, Monastrell, and Trepat contribute in
applicable wines.

Fruit is harvested for the intended sparkling base, with attention to acidity,
potential alcohol, aroma, and sound skins. Pressing, clarification, and the
first fermentation establish much of the wine before bubbles are created.
Producers may keep varieties, vineyards, and press fractions apart to assess the
lots before [blending](../concepts/blending.md).

A pale base wine can come from dark-skinned grapes if pressing limits pigment
extraction. Rosé production follows its own permitted practices.

## Second fermentation and maturation

At tirage, the base wine is bottled with yeast and fermentable sugar. The sealed
fermentation generates carbon dioxide, which dissolves under pressure. The wine
then remains with the sediment before riddling moves it toward the neck and
disgorgement removes it.

[Lees contact](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can add maturation character and
modify texture. Its effects depend on the base wine, time, temperature, and
yeast. A wine can retain fruit expression while developing more yeast-derived
notes.

The final sweetness is another choice. Dosage and the finished wine's
composition distinguish styles such as Brut Nature, Brut, and sweeter
categories. A longer ageing category does not itself describe the amount of
residual sugar.

## Ageing categories

The specification linked by the regulatory board and consulted in October 2026
sets minimum periods from tirage to disgorgement of nine months for Cava, 18 for
Reserva, 30 for Gran Reserva, and 36 for Paraje Calificado.[^rules]

The current category framework groups Reserva, Gran Reserva, and Paraje
Calificado under Guarda Superior. Additional conditions concern production,
traceability, and category eligibility.

## Geographical origin and comparison

The consulted specification names four zones: Comtats de Barcelona, Valle del
Ebro, Viñedos de Almendralejo, and Requena. More focused subzones and qualified
sites can supply further origin information.[^rules] Their climates and
vineyards add differences before the winemaker chooses a blend.

[Champagne](../regions/champagne.md) shares the bottle-fermentation method,
using different grapes in a different growing environment. Most
[Prosecco](prosecco.md) undergoes its second fermentation in a tank, although it
also has bottle-refermented traditions.

[^rules]: Consejo Regulador de la DOP Cava, *Pliego de condiciones*,
    sections 3–4 and 7, version linked from its legislation directory on
    28 October 2026. Periods run from tirage to disgorgement, not from harvest.
