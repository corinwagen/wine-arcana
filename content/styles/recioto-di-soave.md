# Recioto di Soave

Recioto di Soave is a sweet white wine made from grapes dried after harvest
in the [Soave](../regions/soave.md) area of Veneto, east of Verona. It is based
on [Garganega](../grapes/garganega.md) and belongs to Italy's passito tradition.
Drying concentrates the fruit before pressing, while fermentation leaves
substantial sweetness in the finished wine.

The style joins ripe and dried-fruit impressions with a broad texture and
acidity that keeps the sweetness lively. Harvest selection, the conditions
in the drying room, and subsequent fermentation and maturation all influence
its character.

## Selecting and drying the fruit

Bunches intended for recioto need to remain sound through a prolonged period
after picking. Producers select ripe fruit carefully, often favouring loose
bunches whose berries have room for air to circulate. The grapes rest on racks
or in shallow containers in a ventilated drying room, traditionally called
a *fruttaio*.

As the berries lose water, their sugar and other dissolved components become
more concentrated. The berries also remain biologically active during
withering. Their metabolism and any microorganisms present can change the
composition of the fruit, affecting what later reaches the fermentation vat.
Temperature, humidity, ventilation, and the duration of drying consequently
matter alongside the amount of water lost.

Pieropan's Le Colombare offers a documented example. The producer selects
Garganega from a hillside vineyard in Soave Classico and spreads the bunches
on bamboo-cane racks. Natural-air drying usually continues until the end of
February. The estate describes a very low juice yield after this period,
illustrating how much material a long drying regime can remove before the
wine is made.

## Withering and noble rot

Under suitable conditions, [noble rot](botrytized-sweet-wine.md) can develop
during withering and contribute to the wine's aromatic character. Its extent
varies with the fruit and drying conditions. Pieropan reports its development
in the production of Le Colombare.

Research on Garganega has compared normally withered berries with fruit held
under more humid conditions to encourage *Botrytis cinerea*. The treatments
produced distinct grape compositions and different volatile compounds in
experimental wines.[^1] This helps explain why two lots dried to a similar
concentration can still yield different wines. Water loss and fungal activity
change different aspects of the material.

The producer must monitor fruit health throughout. Humidity that encourages
unwanted decay can spoil the selected grapes, so sorting and ventilation
remain important even when a limited noble-rot contribution is welcome.

## Fermentation and maturation

Pressing the withered grapes yields concentrated must. Yeast converts part
of its sugar into alcohol, leaving the
[residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) that defines the sweet style.
Sugar, acidity, and alcohol jointly determine the wine's balance. A wine with
marked acidity can carry considerable sweetness while retaining freshness.

Cellar choices add another source of variation. Pieropan describes
fermentation of Le Colombare in glass-lined cement and subsequent maturation
in large oak and acacia casks for about two to three years, followed by time
in bottle. This extended process gives the concentrated wine time to develop
texture and aromas alongside its fruit.

## The denomination

Recioto di Soave DOCG requires at least 70% Garganega. Trebbiano di Soave can
supply the remainder, with a small allowance for other authorized white
varieties within that share. The specification consolidated on 24 October
2019 sets a minimum residual reducing sugar content of 70 grams per litre
for both the still and sparkling versions.[^2]

Classico identifies eligible still wine from the delimited historic zone
within Soave and Monteforte d'Alpone. Spumante has its own category, using
fermentation to produce the bubbles. The DOCG was established in 1998,
giving this regional dried-grape tradition a separate regulated name.
