# Cap Classique

Cap Classique is South African sparkling wine made through a second
fermentation in the bottle. It applies the
[traditional method](traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md) to grapes from
different Cape regions. The name describes a wine and production tradition;
the stated grape origin identifies where a particular bottle's fruit grew.

## Cape history and grapes

The Cap Classique Producers Association traces the founding example to
Simonsig's Kaapse Vonkel, made from Chenin Blanc in the 1971 harvest and
released in 1973. The association and the Cap Classique name followed
in 1992.[^history]

[Chenin Blanc](../grapes/chenin-blanc.md) consequently belongs to the
style's early history. Contemporary production commonly uses
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) and
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md), with Meunier in some blends.
White varietal wines, blends, and rosés provide different expressions.

## Base wine and bottle fermentation

Picking for a sparkling base usually prioritizes retained acidity and
moderate potential alcohol. Whole-bunch pressing and juice selection
help control the material entering fermentation. Separate parcels and
varieties give the cellar components that can later be blended.

Kleine Zalze's Brut NV illustrates a combination of origins: Chardonnay
from Robertson and Pinot Noir from coastal Stellenbosch sites. Its account
describes separate tank-made components, blending, and malolactic
conversion before bottling under a crown closure.

The second fermentation generates carbon dioxide, retained by the sealed
bottle. Yeast then forms the lees on which the wine matures. Kleine Zalze
describes twelve to eighteen months before disgorgement and dosage for
this bottling. The association's published production description identifies
twelve months as its minimum bottle fermentation and maturation period,
while producers also make wines with substantially longer lees contact.[^lees]

## Time, sweetness, and regional variation

[Lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) changes the base wine over time,
with yeast breakdown contributing compounds that affect texture, aroma,
and foam. Riddling gathers the deposit in the neck; disgorgement removes
it. Dosage and topping precede the final closure.

Finished sweetness depends on the wine and any sugar added at dosage.
Acidity and dissolved carbon dioxide change how that sweetness feels.
A longer-matured wine and a younger, fruit-led example can therefore
have different balances even at similar sugar levels.

[Stellenbosch](../regions/stellenbosch.md),
[Elgin](../regions/elgin.md), and
[Hemel-en-Aarde](../regions/hemel-en-aarde.md) supply different vineyard
contexts for the style. Franschhoek's cellars add another connection,
while a wine's origin needs to be distinguished from the address of
the producer. Vineyard source, base-wine choices, and time in bottle
together explain the individual Cap Classique.
