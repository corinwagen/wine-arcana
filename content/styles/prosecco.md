# Prosecco

Prosecco is a family of geographically protected Italian wines based chiefly on
[Glera](../grapes/glera.md). The name encompasses the broad Prosecco DOC and the
more focused Conegliano Valdobbiadene and Asolo DOCG denominations.

Most familiar examples acquire their bubbles through a second fermentation in a
pressurized tank, the Martinotti or Charmat method. There are also still wines,
lightly sparkling versions, and bottle-refermented traditions.

## Place and grape identity

The Prosecco DOC production area spans specified provinces of Veneto and [Friuli
Venezia Giulia](../regions/friuli.md). Conegliano Valdobbiadene centres on a
hill landscape, while Asolo supplies a separate DOCG origin. The complete
designation tells the reader which production area and rules apply.

Glera was historically called Prosecco; modern grape and wine terminology need
to be kept separate. Planting Glera elsewhere does not by itself confer the
protected geographical wine name. Within the Prosecco family, different sites
and harvest choices provide different starting balances of sugar, acidity, and
aroma.

The DOC specification consulted in October 2026 requires at least 85% Glera for
white Prosecco. Its sparkling rosé combines 85–90% Glera with 10–15% Pinot Noir
vinified as red wine.[^doc]

## How tank fermentation supplies the bubbles

White grapes are pressed and the juice clarified before the first
[fermentation](../concepts/alcoholic-fermentation.md). The resulting still base
wines can be assessed and blended. Yeast then ferment additional sugar in a
sealed pressure tank, retaining the carbon dioxide in the wine. Filtration and
bottling under pressure preserve the bubbles while removing the yeast sediment.

This route allows producers to emphasize Glera's fruit and floral expression
without requiring a long period in individual bottles on the lees. Grapes,
yeast, temperature, and the duration of tank contact affect the aroma and
texture.

The [traditional method](traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md), used for
Champagne and Cava, instead carries out the second fermentation in the bottle
and later removes the sediment by disgorgement.

## Other forms and local traditions

*Spumante* identifies fully sparkling wine, *frizzante* a less effervescent
category, and *tranquillo* still wine. The exact designation determines which
forms are available and their production conditions.

Conegliano Valdobbiadene's *Sui Lieviti* category preserves a bottle-refermented
tradition with the yeast sediment retained. The consortium also discusses *col
fondo* and *sur lie* terminology in explaining this local practice.

## Sweetness and geographical detail

Sweetness terms can be counterintuitive: *Extra Dry* is sweeter than *Brut*
within the applicable sparkling-wine categories. Read the complete term instead
of treating the English word “dry” as an informal tasting judgement. [Residual
sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) and acidity jointly influence the
impression in the glass.

Within Conegliano Valdobbiadene, Rive and Cartizze draw attention to more
specific origins.
