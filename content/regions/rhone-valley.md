# Rhône Valley

The Rhône Valley is a wine region in southeastern France whose vineyards connect
the narrower river corridor south of Vienne with the broader Mediterranean
landscapes around Orange and Avignon. Its northern and southern vineyards differ
in climate, terrain, and grapes.

[Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md) defines the northern reds, while
[Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md) and blends play a larger role in the south.
White wines are made in both parts of the valley, with rosé also important in
the south.

## Northern slopes and southern landscapes

In the north, steep slopes and terraces constrain vineyard work and make
exposure important. Granite-derived soils are prominent in several districts,
although the region also contains other rocks and deposits. The river bends,
side valleys, soil depth, and shelter change conditions between vineyards.

The south opens into a wider landscape of hills, terraces, and plains.
Limestone, clay, sand, and stony deposits appear in different combinations.
Mediterranean summers bring heat and dry conditions, while the Mistral adds wind
exposure. [Wind](../concepts/wind-and-wine-growing.md) can dry foliage, but it
also changes water demand and the physical work of cultivating vines.

A shallow southern hillside and a deeper clay site store different amounts of
water. Elevation, aspect, and exposure can create cooler conditions within the
southern region.

## Northern wines

[Côte-Rôtie](cote-rotie.md) and [Hermitage](hermitage.md) are two northern Syrah
appellations, with different landscapes and production traditions. Extraction,
whole-bunch inclusion, and oak can alter tannin and aroma even when the grape
remains the same.

[Condrieu](condrieu.md) centres on [Viognier](../grapes/viognier.md), while
[Marsanne](../grapes/marsanne.md) and [Roussanne](../grapes/roussanne.md)
connect other northern white-wine traditions. White grapes also have specific
roles in some red appellations. The permission and practice belong to the
individual denomination; they should not be generalized to every northern red.

## Southern blends and white wines

Grenache is joined by Syrah, [Mourvèdre](../grapes/mourvedre.md),
[Carignan](../grapes/carignan.md), and [Cinsault](../grapes/cinsault.md), with
other varieties participating under local rules. Different ripening patterns and
wine properties give producers options when selecting sites and assembling the
final wine. [Blending](../concepts/blending.md) can combine separately fermented
lots; cofermentation brings fruit together earlier.

[Châteauneuf-du-Pape](chateauneuf-du-pape.md) illustrates the range of grapes
and soils within one southern appellation. Grenache Blanc, Clairette,
Bourboulenc, Roussanne, and other white varieties also support a substantial
white-wine tradition across the broader valley.

## Region, appellation, and bottle

The Rhône Valley and Côtes du Rhône are overlapping but different names. The
valley is the larger geographical reference; Côtes du Rhône is an appellation
with its own rules. Côtes du Rhône Villages and the individual crus supply
further legal designations. Other valley appellations have their own histories
and specifications.[^scope]

[^scope]: Inter Rhône's appellation directory and Côtes du Rhône account
    distinguish the regional appellation from the wider valley vineyards.
