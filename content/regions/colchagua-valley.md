# Colchagua Valley

Colchagua occupies the southern part of Chile's Rapel Valley. It is closely
associated with red wine, especially [Carménère](../grapes/carmenere.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md). The broad regional name contains hillside and
valley-floor vineyards, with different exposures to sun and cooling air.

## Apalta and the vineyard landscape

Apalta is a useful place to examine those differences. At Clos Apalta,
vineyards occupy an amphitheatre near the Tinguiririca River. The producer
describes predominantly south-eastern exposures and shelter from afternoon sun.
In the southern hemisphere, this orientation can moderate the heat received by
the fruit. Shade depends on the slope and surrounding hills as well as the
leaves that a grower retains.

Its soils also vary within the estate, from deeper granitic material to
shallower clay-containing profiles over weathered rock. Those differences
affect the volume of soil roots can explore and the water available between
rainfall or irrigation events. A single description such as “granite” cannot
capture all those growing conditions.

Montes's technical account of Wings 2020 offers a second Apalta example. It
describes a steep, south-west-facing vineyard with granitic and clay components,
contrasting deeper soils on flatter ground with shallower hillside profiles.
The exposure is part of the producer's strategy for moderating summer heat.

## Ripening and wine structure

Carménère's long ripening cycle makes the relation between warmth, canopy, and
harvest date particularly consequential. Sufficient time can soften the
impression of green flavours, while excessive heat or prolonged hang time can
increase alcohol and reduce freshness. [Canopy
management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) therefore involves balancing
fruit exposure with protection from strong sun.

The Wings 2020 record illustrates how a season can disrupt the expected
calendar. Montes reports a dry winter, limited water, and an unusually early
Carménère harvest. Those are observations about that vintage, rather than a
permanent timetable for Colchagua. The wine blended Carménère with
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md) and matured in French barrels.
The combination shows how a regional red's shape can arise from both grape
selection and [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md).

Clos Apalta places Carménère at the centre of its blends, accompanied by
Merlot, Cabernet Sauvignon, and [Petit Verdot](../grapes/petit-verdot.md).
Comparing these approaches helps separate the contribution of the vineyard
from the choice to assemble several varieties. Neither blend formula describes
every wine from the valley.

Colchagua also extends toward cooler coastal growing areas. That wider
geography creates room for white wines alongside the inland reds. A comparison
with [Maipo](maipo-valley.md) is useful for Cabernet, while
[Casablanca](casablanca-valley.md) provides a more explicitly maritime setting
for Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay.
