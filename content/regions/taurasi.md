# Taurasi

Taurasi is a red-wine appellation in Irpinia, the inland part of Campania
around Avellino. It takes its name from the town of Taurasi and extends across
seventeen communes in the hills around the Calore valley. Its principal grape,
[Aglianico](../grapes/aglianico.md), ripens late and produces wines with enough
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) to sustain prolonged maturation.

This is a mountain-influenced setting for southern Italian red wine. Elevation,
exposure, and autumn weather shape the growing season, with some vineyards
harvested into November. The combination of ripe fruit and a firm structure is
central to Taurasi's character.

## A long season in the hills

Aglianico buds early and ripens late. That long interval exposes the vine to
several different risks: spring frost around budburst, summer water stress, and
rain as harvest approaches. Vigor and crop load also matter. A shaded canopy or
an excessive crop can delay the ripening of a variety already dependent on a
long season.

The region contains substantial differences over short distances. Lower,
warmer sites can reach maturity while higher slopes are still developing their
fruit. Aspect changes the amount and timing of sunlight, while soil depth and
clay content influence the water available through summer.

Mastroberardino's two sources for its Radici Taurasi illustrate the range. The
producer describes Montemarano vineyards at roughly 500–650 metres, with
southeastern exposure and clay-rich soils containing calcareous stone. Ripening
there is slow and harvest can extend into November. Its Mirabella Eclano estate
lies lower, around 350–450 metres, with predominantly southwestern exposure
and deeper soils containing sand, clay, and volcanic material. It describes
this setting as milder and earlier ripening.

## Aglianico and maturation

The Taurasi DOCG specification amended on 13 October 2020 requires at least
85% Aglianico. Other eligible, non-aromatic red varieties may supply the balance.
The minimum ageing period is three years, including one year in wood. Riserva
requires four years, including eighteen months in wood; the periods begin on
1 December of the harvest year.[^1]

Those requirements give the producer time to work with a young wine's
astringency. During [maturation in wood](../concepts/oak-maturation.md),
reactions among tannins, pigments, and other components change both texture
and colour. The result depends on the starting
fruit and on oxygen exposure, vessel, and time. A larger, older cask supplies a
different environment from a small new barrel, whose wood compounds can make a
more obvious contribution to aroma and flavour.

Mastroberardino's Radici uses both barriques and larger casks, followed by
[bottle maturation](../concepts/bottle-aging.md). Its long
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), the contact between fermenting juice
and skins, is another producer choice with consequences for extraction. Across Taurasi,
harvest maturity and the management of skin contact help determine how much
fruit, acidity, and tannin enters that ageing period.

Young wines can show dark fruit with a pronounced drying grip. With sound
storage, some develop more savoury, earthy, and leathery impressions as their
texture changes. The legal ageing minimum sets a release framework; a wine's
subsequent development still depends on its composition and storage.

## Recognition

Taurasi received DOC recognition in 1970 and DOCG recognition in 1993.
