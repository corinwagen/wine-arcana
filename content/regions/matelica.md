# Matelica

Matelica is an inland white-wine area in Marche, central Italy, centred on the
upper Esino valley. Its principal grape is
[Verdicchio](../grapes/verdicchio.md), grown between mountain ridges in a setting
with cold winters, warm summers, and pronounced changes between daytime and
night-time temperatures. The wines often combine firm acidity with ample
fruit and body.

The appellation extends beyond the town of Matelica into parts of eight
communes in the provinces of Macerata and Ancona. Verdicchio di Matelica DOC
covers still, sparkling, and passito wines, while Verdicchio di Matelica
Riserva has its own DOCG denomination.

## An inland corridor

The upper Esino flows north through this part of its valley, roughly parallel
to the Adriatic coast. The Apennines stand to the west and the Monte San Vicino
ridge to the east. That eastern barrier limits the sea's moderating influence,
giving the vineyards a more continental climate than the open valley farther
downstream toward [Castelli di Jesi](castelli-di-jesi.md).

Vineyards occupy the hills around the valley, with many slopes facing south.
Elevation and exposure influence the time needed to ripen fruit. Warm days
support sugar accumulation, while cooler conditions can help preserve acidity
by slowing the grape's respiration of malic acid. Vintage weather and the
chosen harvest date determine how these processes balance in practice.

The ground also varies along the valley. Around Cerreto d'Esi to the north,
soils include loam and silt; marly and calcareous profiles occur around Matelica,
with more conspicuously calcareous ground toward Castelraimondo in the south.
Differences in soil depth and texture change the water available to roots.
These local conditions give growers several ways to arrive at ripe Verdicchio
within the broader inland climate.

## Freshness and substance

A young dry Matelica can show citrus, orchard fruit, and a slightly bitter
finish. More mature examples can develop nutty and honeyed impressions. The
wine's combination of acidity and fruit weight allows substantial maturation,
although the speed and direction of development vary with production and
storage.

Harvest decisions are especially important for wines intended to age. Sugar
alone gives an incomplete picture of maturity: growers also consider acidity,
pH, flavour development, and fruit health. More sugar generally means more
potential alcohol, which adds weight to a dry wine. The fruit needs sufficient
acidity to keep that weight balanced.

Belisario's published Cambrugiano production description illustrates how a
producer works with this material. Its source vineyard faces south at about
400 metres. The winery selects harvest timing by following sugar, total
acidity, and pH. It describes partial cold skin contact before fermentation
and divides maturation between stainless steel and a smaller portion in
barriques. This permits wood to contribute to one component before the wine
is assembled. The account documents a cellar approach; the exact proportions
and timing can change between releases.

## DOC and Riserva

The DOC specification amended on 24 June 2025 requires at least 85%
Verdicchio, with other eligible white varieties allowed for the remainder.
Spumante and passito are explicitly included alongside the still wine.[^1]
Sparkling production draws on the grape's acidity, while drying grapes for
passito increases their concentration and creates the raw material for a
sweet wine.

The separate Riserva DOCG also requires at least 85% Verdicchio. Under its
specification amended on 24 June 2025, the wine must age for at least eighteen
months, counted from 1 December of the harvest year.[^2] Wood maturation is
optional. Producers can develop a Riserva through tank, wood, bottle, and
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) in different combinations, choosing
how much texture and aromatic development to encourage before release.
