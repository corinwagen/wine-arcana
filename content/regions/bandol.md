# Bandol

Bandol is a coastal wine appellation in Provence, on the hills west of Toulon.
Its terraces face the Mediterranean through an amphitheatre of wooded
heights. [Mourvèdre](../grapes/mourvedre.md) gives the reds their firm structure
and capacity for ageing, and also plays an important part in the rosés. White
wine completes the appellation's range.

## Warmth, water, and terraces

The sea moderates temperature extremes, while the hills offer protection
from cold northerly winds. Growers have built dry-stone walls to retain soil
on the slopes, forming terraces called *restanques*. Marl and limestone
dominate the geology, with substantial variation in soil depth and stoniness.

This setting suits a grape with Mourvèdre's particular demands. It ripens
late and needs sustained warmth, including warm nights during maturation.
Plantgrape also emphasises its need for a limited but regular water supply.
Soils that allow roots to reach stored water help sustain ripening through
the dry summer. Exposure and water availability consequently matter alongside
the region's sunshine.

## Red, rosé, and white

Red Bandol must contain 50–95 percent Mourvèdre.
[Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md) and [Cinsaut](../grapes/cinsault.md) are the
other principal grapes. The wines require at least 18 months of maturation
in barrels or large wooden casks.[^1] Mourvèdre can supply substantial
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md), making grape maturity and extraction important
to the texture of a young red. Bottle age can bring more savoury aromas as
the fruit develops.

[Rosé](../styles/rose.md) uses the same principal red grapes, with a lower minimum Mourvèdre share
of 20 percent.[^1] These are dry wines that can retain noticeable structure
even at a pale colour. Blend, picking date, and the amount of contact with
skins help determine their weight and texture.

White Bandol centres on [Clairette](../grapes/clairette-blanche.md), alongside
[Bourboulenc](../grapes/bourboulenc.md) and Ugni Blanc. Clairette must account
for 30–95 percent of the blend.[^1] The wines often combine floral or
white-fruit aromas with a rounded palate.

The August 2026 specification also permits several adaptation varieties
under signed agreements, capped together at 5 percent of appellation
plantings and 10 percent of a blend.[^2] The principal grapes and their
different proportions remain a useful guide to the three colours.
