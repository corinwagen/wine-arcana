# Wiener Gemischter Satz

Wiener Gemischter Satz is Vienna's protected white wine from mixed-variety
vineyards. Its foundation is a [field blend](../concepts/field-blends.md):
several grapes grow in the same vineyard and are harvested and processed
together. The vineyard's composition and the shared picking date help shape
the wine before separate cellar lots could be assembled.

The designation received protected-origin status in 2013 under Districtus
Austriae Controllatus, or DAC. It preserves a local practice within a modern
system of protected
origin, connecting Vienna's urban vineyards and *Heurigen*, its traditional
wine taverns, with wines that can also identify a particular locality or site.

## Diversity in one harvest

Vienna's mixed white plantings have historically included
[Riesling](../grapes/riesling.md), [Rotgipfler](../grapes/rotgipfler.md),
[Weissburgunder](../grapes/pinot-blanc.md), and Traminer, among other varieties.
Their differing flowering times could spread the risk of poor weather during
flowering. Their differing ripening times also make a common harvest a
compromise: the desired balance is found across the planting rather than by
waiting for every variety to reach the same condition.

This makes the field blend a continuing agricultural choice. Changing the
proportions in a vineyard, replacing missing vines, or changing the picking
date can alter the finished wine. A varietal list alone cannot predict its
balance of aroma, acidity, and texture. Wieninger's 2025 regional bottling,
for example, is described by the producer as coming from twelve interplanted
varieties harvested together.

## What the designation requires

The current regulation requires at least three varieties in a qualifying
Viennese vineyard, jointly harvested and processed. The largest varietal
share must not exceed 50%, and the third-largest must be at least 10%.
All grapes must come from Vienna. The rules also permit up to 15% blending
with other Viennese quality wine; for a wine bearing a *Ried* name, that
allowance is restricted to Wiener Gemischter Satz DAC.[^dac] Joint processing
is consequently the
wine's foundation, without requiring every component of every bottle to have
been harvested together.

The regional, locality, and individual-vineyard levels describe progressively
narrower origins. *Gebietswein* identifies the regional wine; *Ortswein*
identifies a defined locality, such as Grinzing, Nußberg, or Mauer; *Riedenwein*
identifies a delimited vineyard, or *Ried*. The locality framework replaced
the previous use of broad vineyard-area names for this DAC, with the revised
labelling rules mandatory from the 2024 harvest.

These levels also have different alcohol and release conditions. A narrower
origin supplies more information about where the grapes grew, rather than a
complete account of how the wine will taste. The varieties present, vintage,
harvest decision, and cellar handling remain necessary context. The DAC
rules prohibit strongly perceptible wood character at the regional level,
with an exception for wines carrying a smaller geographical origin. This
leaves room to consider
texture and maturation without assuming that every wine must use the same
vessel.
