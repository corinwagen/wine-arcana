# Château-Chalon

Château-Chalon is a small appellation in France's [Jura](jura.md), devoted to
[vin jaune](../styles/vin-jaune.md). Its vineyards climb below a village on a
limestone promontory north of Lons-le-Saunier. The name joins a particular set
of slopes to a demanding cellar practice: making a dry white wine and maturing
it for years beneath a surface veil of yeast.

## Vineyard and grape

South- and west-facing slopes catch the sun beneath cliffs that shelter the
vines from cold winds. Limestone debris covers the marl beneath them. The
combination of warmth, drainage, and water held at depth helps grapes ripen in
the Jura's wet climate. The steep ground also demands work to retain soil and
maintain access.

[Savagnin](../grapes/savagnin.md) is the defining grape. The specification
adopted in August 2026 names it as the principal variety and conditionally
permits Enfariné and Gringet under signed agreements. Their combined share is
normally limited to 5 percent of an estate's appellation plantings and
10 percent of a finished blend; both limits rise to 20 percent for estates
working less than three hectares of Château-Chalon.[^1]

## Maturation and character

The wine ages in oak without topping up to replace evaporation. A yeast veil
develops at the surface, reshaping its aroma during the long maturation. The
rules require ageing until at least 15 December of the sixth year after
harvest, including at least 60 months under the veil. Consumer release begins
on 1 January of the seventh year.[^2]

Château-Chalon is dry, with the savoury depth and walnut-and-spice aromas
associated with vin jaune. Cellar conditions contribute to the differences
between wines. Wink Lorch describes the area's typical maturation cellars as
partly below ground, with gentler seasonal temperature changes than the
attics traditionally used around [Arbois](arbois.md). She associates this with
less evaporation and a subtler spice character, while acknowledging overlap
between the two places.[^3]

The finished wine is presented in the squat 62-centilitre *clavelin*. Long
[bottle ageing](../concepts/bottle-aging.md) can further develop its aromas; producer, vintage, and storage
conditions determine how a particular bottle evolves.
