# Casablanca Valley

Casablanca is a Chilean wine valley where Pacific air and morning fog make
coastal cooling central to grape growing. It is especially associated with
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md),
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md), and
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md). The ocean helps explain why these grapes
have a substantial home at this latitude, but vineyards within the valley
receive different amounts of warmth and sunlight.

## The coast and the slope

Kingston Family Vineyards, in Las Dichas, describes cool, humid, cloudy
conditions at its coastal-range estate. Its Sauvignon Blanc parcels offer a
particularly useful comparison. Cariblanco comes from an older, warmer part of
the property; the producer's CJ's wine comes from a south-facing hillside
selected for slower ripening. Even on one farm, “cool climate” contains
different harvest conditions.

Slope orientation changes the sunlight received by leaves and fruit. In these
southern-hemisphere vineyards, a south-facing site can receive less direct sun
than a north-facing one. The resulting pace of ripening interacts with vine
vigour, crop, and harvest date. Coastal cooling gives a grower opportunities to
retain [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), while the particular site determines
how the fruit reaches the desired balance.

Casas del Bosque documents another coastal setting at the western end of the
valley. For its Bo 2019 sparkling wine, Chardonnay grew on red-clay slopes and
Pinot Noir on sandier ground. The producer reports frequent fog alongside warm
days and cold nights. Its account of a warm summer after a dry winter is a
reminder that a maritime vineyard still experiences substantial vintage
variation.

## From aromatic whites to sparkling wine

Casablanca's wines range beyond one model of crisp white wine. Kingston's
Chardonnay range includes Sabino, matured in neutral vessels such as concrete
and used barrels, and CJ's, with new-oak maturation. Those choices change
texture and aroma even when both wines begin with coastal-grown Chardonnay.
Its Pinot Noir range likewise distinguishes individual blocks, making parcel
selection part of the explanation for variation within a grape.

Casas del Bosque's Bo 2019 shows a different use of Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.
The grapes were pressed as whole bunches, the base wines fermented in used
barrels, and the second fermentation took place in bottle. Subsequent
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) adds texture and developed aromas to
the starting fruit. Coastal acidity supplies a useful foundation, while
pressing, fermentation, and maturation determine the finished sparkling style.

For a comparison within Chile, [Limarí](limari-valley.md) combines Pacific
cooling with a more arid northern setting. [Maipo](maipo-valley.md) gives
Cabernet Sauvignon a larger role in a valley extending into the Andean
foothills.
