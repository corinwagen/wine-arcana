# Kakheti

Kakheti is the principal wine-growing region of eastern Georgia, with vineyards
in the Alazani and Iori basins. [Saperavi](../grapes/saperavi.md) anchors its
red-wine identity; Rkatsiteli, Mtsvane Kakhuri, Kisi, Khikhvi, and other local
grapes supply several kinds of white and amber wine.

The region is especially associated with qvevri, large earthenware vessels used
for fermentation and storage. It also makes wines in tanks and other vessels.
Grape, skin contact, and maturation decisions matter alongside whether a wine
was made in clay.

## Valleys and vineyard origins

The Alazani Valley lies below the Greater Caucasus, while the Iori basin
provides another important vineyard landscape. River deposits, slopes, local
soils, and differences in exposure create distinct growing conditions.

Telavi, Kvareli, and Sighnaghi provide geographical reference points for
exploring the region. Smaller protected wine names identify particular origins
and production conditions. Tsinandali and Mukuzani are examples.

## White grapes and amber wines

[Rkatsiteli](../grapes/rkatsiteli.md) and [Mtsvane
Kakhuri](../grapes/mtsvane-kakhuri.md) connect the region's rapidly pressed
whites with its extended-skin-contact wines. [Kisi](../grapes/kisi.md) and
[Khikhvi](../grapes/khikhvi.md) broaden that range through different varietal
starting points.

In the traditional Kakhetian approach described by Georgia's National Wine
Agency, white must ferments with grape solids in qvevri and may remain with them
until spring. Skin, seed, and sometimes stem contact supply colour and phenolic
texture. The quantity and condition of the solids and the time of separation
matter to the finished wine.[^contact]

The vessel is buried, with a neck accessible for cellar work. Filling,
fermentation management, sealing, cleaning, and
[racking](../concepts/racking-and-topping.md) are practical parts of the
process. Clay use does not remove the need to manage microorganisms, oxygen, and
fruit condition.

## Saperavi and other wine routes

Saperavi has coloured flesh as well as coloured skins. That
[teinturier](../concepts/teinturier.md) property helps explain its deep colour,
while tannin extraction remains a separate decision. A wine can therefore have
intense colour without requiring the longest possible skin contact.

The National Wine Agency distinguishes the shorter contact often used for red
qvevri wines from the extended contact of traditional whites. Saperavi can also
be fermented or matured in other vessels. It supplies dry reds and rosés as well
as other styles.

## Amber wine and qvevri

A [skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md) and a quickly
pressed white may share a grape and origin while differing considerably in
colour and texture.

Likewise, *amber* describes a wine colour and production association; *qvevri*
identifies a vessel tradition.

[^contact]: National Wine Agency of Georgia, “Qvevri Wine,” regional
    differences and the distinction between red and white contact times.
    This is a description of practice, not a required schedule for every wine.
