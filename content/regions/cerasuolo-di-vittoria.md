# Cerasuolo di Vittoria

Cerasuolo di Vittoria is a red-wine appellation in southeastern Sicily,
centred on the country around Vittoria and Acate. It brings together
[Nero d'Avola](../grapes/nero-davola.md) and [Frappato](../grapes/frappato.md)
in a blend that can combine substantial fruit with a fragrant, lively
character. The production area extends into parts of the provinces of Ragusa,
Caltanissetta, and Catania.

The name *cerasuolo* refers to cherry colour. Here it identifies a dry red
wine, with cherry and floral impressions often prominent in its aroma. The
two grapes, their proportions, and the management of extraction give
producers considerable room to shape the finished wine.

## Between plateau and sea

The vineyards occupy southeastern Sicily's warm Mediterranean landscape,
between the Hyblaean uplands and the southern coast. The area includes low
coastal country and inland hills. Distance from the sea, elevation, and the
exposure of individual plots change the heat and ventilation experienced by
the vines.

Loose red sands are a conspicuous part of the landscape around Vittoria.
What lies below them matters to the vine's water supply. At Planeta's Dorilli
and Mogli vineyards, the producer describes sandy soils above a rock layer
roughly ninety centimetres down, which it identifies as important for water
availability. Surface sand can drain readily while the deeper profile
provides a different rooting environment.

The consequences become clear during a dry summer. Roots must reach enough
water to keep the canopy functioning and the fruit ripening. Soil depth,
rooting opportunities, and crop load affect that balance. Vineyards with
similar-looking red soil can therefore behave differently when their
subsoils or water reserves differ.

## The blend

The specification amended on 17 February 2020 requires 50–70% Nero d'Avola
and 30–50% Frappato.[^1] Nero d'Avola commonly supplies colour, body, and
structure; Frappato contributes a pronounced floral and red-fruit character.
Each grape also varies with its site and harvest maturity, so the proportions
alone give only part of the explanation.

Increasing Frappato's share can bring its perfume further forward. Riper
Nero d'Avola may give a broader middle palate. Producers can also adjust
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), including the time on skins and the
movement of fermenting juice through them. These choices determine how much
colour and tannin the blend acquires alongside its fruit.

Planeta's 2024 Cerasuolo used 60% Nero d'Avola and 40% Frappato. Its technical
sheet describes brief pump-overs during fermentation, followed by further
skin contact, malolactic fermentation, and maturation in stainless steel.
This is a concrete example of developing texture through extraction and time
while keeping wood aromas out of the wine's profile.

COS likewise describes a Cerasuolo made without barrel maturation, another
example of a producer emphasizing the blend through other cellar choices.

## Classico and time

Cerasuolo di Vittoria Classico comes from a more narrowly delimited historic
area and spends longer before release. Ordinary Cerasuolo can be released
from 1 June after the harvest. Classico must wait until 31 March of the second
year following harvest.[^1] A 2024 Classico therefore reaches its earliest
permitted release on 31 March 2026.

The additional time can allow a young wine's tannins and fruit to settle into
a more integrated texture. With further bottle age, some examples develop
more savoury impressions, while early-drinking wines can emphasize the
blend's bright fruit and fragrance. The DOCG was recognized in September
2005, following the earlier DOC established in 1973.

[^1]: Italian Ministry of Agriculture, Cerasuolo di Vittoria DOCG
    specification, amended 17 February 2020, articles 2, 3, and 5;
    accessed 11 September 2026.
