# Friuli-Venezia Giulia

Friuli-Venezia Giulia occupies Italy's northeastern corner, between the Alps,
the Adriatic and Slovenia. Wine writing often shortens its name to Friuli;
this overview covers the whole administrative region, including the Carso
around Trieste. Its wines bring together local grapes, long-established
French varieties and strikingly different approaches to making white wine.

A pale, freshly pressed white and an amber wine fermented with its skins can
come from neighbouring vineyards. Understanding the region therefore means
asking both where the vines grow and what happens to the grapes after harvest.

## Plains, border hills and Carso

The broad gravelly plains of Friuli Grave differ from the eastern hills of
Friuli Colli Orientali and Collio. Farther south, the Isonzo has its own
gravelly vineyard landscape, while Aquileia, Annia and Latisana bring the
coastal plain into the picture. These names help locate a bottle more
precisely than the broad Friuli appellation.

Collio lies along the Slovenian frontier. Its folded slopes offer different
exposures, and its characteristic layered marl and sandstone are commonly
called *ponca*. Site selection matters even within this relatively small
district: the direction a slope faces changes its exposure to sunlight,
while soil depth and structure influence rooting and water supply.

The Carso, or Karst, introduces limestone terrain and exposure to the bora
wind. [Vitovska](../grapes/vitovska.md) gives this landscape a distinctive
white-grape identity. The region's geography is therefore more varied than
an account centred entirely on Collio's hills would suggest.

## Grapes and their different roles

[Friulano](../grapes/friulano.md) is a useful starting point for the dry
whites: it often combines a rounded palate with a gently bitter almond-like
finish. [Ribolla Gialla](../grapes/ribolla-gialla.md) can emphasize a more
citrus-led freshness. [Malvasia Istriana](../grapes/malvasia-istriana.md)
adds another local white-wine tradition shared with neighbouring territories.
[Pinot Grigio](../grapes/pinot-gris.md),
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) are also established parts of regional
production, appearing in varietal wines and blends.

The red wines deserve their own attention. Alongside
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), the local repertoire includes
[Refosco dal Peduncolo Rosso](../grapes/refosco-dal-peduncolo-rosso.md),
the spicy [Schioppettino](../grapes/schioppettino.md) associated with Prepotto,
and the firmly structured [Pignolo](../grapes/pignolo.md) and
[Tazzelenghe](../grapes/tazzelenghe.md). Extended maturation can be especially
important for the latter two, whose young tannins may feel forceful.

Sweet wine supplies another regional expression. Ramandolo is associated
with [Verduzzo Friulano](../grapes/verduzzo-friulano.md), whose sweetness can
coexist with noticeable tannic texture; Picolit provides a separate sweet-wine
tradition. These wines make sense as distinct production goals, rather than
as richer versions of an ordinary dry white.

## How the modern styles developed

Mario Schiopetto helped establish temperature-controlled white winemaking
in the region during the 1960s. Cooling fermentation gave producers greater
control over a process that generates heat, helping them preserve a fresh
aromatic expression. Later experiments with small oak barrels developed a
different emphasis on texture and maturation.

The border area around Oslavia also became influential in the modern revival
of [skin-contact white wine](../styles/skin-contact-white-wine.md).
Fermenting white grapes with their skins extracts colour and phenolic
material, including tannin. That changes the wine's structure as well as its
appearance; amber colour alone does not identify a particular vessel or
length of ageing.

Gravner's current Ribolla practice provides a concrete example: fermentation
and maceration in buried Georgian clay vessels, followed by further vessel
ageing and years in large, used wooden casks. The importer’s 2026 release
account documents this continuing approach. It is one producer's method,
alongside the region's many directly pressed whites.

## Benchmark producers

- **Schiopetto** anchors the history of modern, carefully controlled white
  winemaking; Mario's work helps explain why cellar technique became central
  to Friuli's international reputation.
- **Gravner**, at Oslavia, offers a contrasting reference through Ribolla,
  extended skin contact and long maturation, illustrating the region's
  influence on contemporary amber wine.
