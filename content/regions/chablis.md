# Chablis

Chablis is a wine district in the Yonne department of northern Burgundy and
the name of its principal appellation. Its dry white wines are made exclusively
from [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md). A cool setting, a landscape cut
by the Serein river, and distinct local traditions make site and season
especially visible; pressing, fermentation, malolactic conversion, lees contact,
and maturation can then redirect the same raw material.

The familiar sequence—Petit Chablis, Chablis, Chablis Premier Cru, and Chablis
Grand Cru—describes delimited origins with different production conditions.
Vineyard position, weather, farming, harvest decisions, and the producer
remain consequential within each category.

## Landscape and climate

The vineyards occupy slopes, small side valleys, plateau edges, and some lower
ground around the Serein, east of Auxerre. This is the edge of the Paris Basin,
where erosion has exposed layers of Upper Jurassic sedimentary rock. Much
Chablis, Premier Cru, and Grand Cru ground lies over alternating limestone and
clay-rich Kimmeridgian marl, including beds containing small fossil oysters.
Harder Barrois limestone caps parts of the plateau, while other limestone,
colluvium, and differences in clay content complicate the picture.

Petit Chablis is associated especially with the exposed plateau edges and their
younger, harder limestone. That is a tendency, not a geological definition of
the appellation: its official parcels also include some slopes and footslopes,
while Chablis parcels can locally reach the harder plateau or lower limestone.
The names are fixed by parcel boundaries rather than by a rule assigning every
outcrop to one category.

Geology affects soil depth, drainage, water supply, and the way a site warms.
Aspect and slope alter light
and heat, while the folded network of valleys creates sheltered and exposed
positions over short distances. A sunnier slope may advance ripening in a cool
year, but it can also produce riper fruit in a warm one.

Chablis has a cool climate with oceanic weather modified by continental
influences. Chardonnay ripens early enough to succeed here, but its early
budbreak exposes young shoots to spring frost. Enclosed valleys can intensify
the hazard, and growers use measures including water sprinkling and vineyard
heating when conditions threaten. Earlier budbreak can offset an earlier last
frost as the climate warms. A national modelling study
projected increasing late-frost risk in Burgundy under two of three phenological
models, while the third gave the opposite result, so the direction and scale of
future risk remain model-dependent.[^1]

## Appellations and vineyard hierarchy

There are three appellations rather than four. Petit Chablis and Chablis Grand
Cru are separate appellations; within the Chablis appellation, qualifying wine
may carry the *premier cru* mention. Premier Cru can also be followed by one of
40 delimited *climats*—named vineyard areas—some of which may be grouped under
17 principal names. These sites occur on both banks of the Serein and encompass
different aspects, soils, and mesoclimates.[^2]

Chablis Grand Cru is confined to one right-bank hillside immediately north of
the town. Its seven official climats are Blanchot, Bougros, Grenouilles, Les
Clos, Preuses, Valmur, and Vaudésir. They share a generally southwest-facing
slope but differ in orientation, elevation, soil depth, and proportion of clay
and stone. A Grand Cru bottle may name one of them.

The rules become more selective in several measurable ways. Minimum natural
alcohol rises from Petit Chablis through Chablis and Premier Cru to Grand Cru;
permitted base yield is lower for Premier Cru and lower again for Grand Cru.
Grand Cru must also mature at least until 15 March after harvest and cannot be
released to consumers before 31 March.[^3]

## Wine and cellar choices

After pressing, Chablis may ferment in stainless steel or in barrel. Steel
contributes no wood flavour and permits close temperature control; a barrel
changes oxygen exposure and may add oak-derived compounds, depending especially
on its size, age, and previous use. The appellation rules prohibit wood chips,
not barrels. New oak is generally used sparingly, while older wood and partial
barrel maturation are more common for some Premier Cru and Grand Cru wines.
Other producers use vats throughout, including for classified sites.

Most Chablis undergoes [malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md),
which converts sharper malic acid to lactic acid and changes microbial stability
as well as balance. That choice does not automatically make the wine buttery:
the aroma compound diacetyl depends on bacterial strain and subsequent handling.
Maturation on fine yeast lees can add texture and modify aroma separately, and
stirring the lees changes both contact and oxygen exposure. A producer may use
these tools to broaden a naturally firm wine or handle them conservatively to
retain a more direct expression.

Cooler sites and seasons often emphasize [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and restraint; warmer
conditions, later harvest, extended lees contact, or barrel fermentation can
give broader texture and earlier openness. Bottle age adds another variable.

## Benchmark producers

**Domaine William Fèvre** is useful for comparing named climats across a
broad range of premier and grand cru holdings. Its modern cellar approach
uses seasoned barrels in place of new oak, a change documented in Andy
Howard's account of the domaine and maintained in its current description.
The wines illustrate how wood can contribute to maturation without making
new-oak flavour the defining feature.[^4]

**Domaine Louis Michel & Fils** provides a contrasting reference through its
exclusive use of stainless steel for vinification. Howard identifies the
domaine among Chablis growers attentive to individual terroirs. Comparing
its wines from different climats offers a view of those origins under a
consistent vessel choice; comparing the same climat across Michel and Fèvre
also brings producer decisions into the picture. Neither approach makes
one wine a purer expression of its site by definition.[^4]
