# Khvanchkara

Khvanchkara is a Georgian appellation for naturally semi-sweet red wine
from Racha, in the Rioni valley around Ambrolauri. Its two grapes,
[Aleksandrouli](../grapes/alexandrouli.md) and
[Mujuretuli](../grapes/mujuretuli.md), supply a wine whose sweetness comes
from grape sugar retained through incomplete fermentation.

## Place and grapes

The appellation takes its name from a village, but its vineyards cover a
wider group of settlements. Sakpatenti's 2017 published specification
places the core zone on slopes on both sides of the Rioni, between the
Lechkhumi and Racha ridges. It describes a humid climate with moderately
cold winters and warm summers, and associates the slopes' exposure with
the ripening of the local grapes.[^1]

The specification names Aleksandrouli and Mujuretuli as the permitted
varieties. Both also make dry wines, so the grape combination alone does
not define Khvanchkara. The appellation joins a delimited origin to a
particular semi-sweet style.

Blending proportions vary between producers. Marani lists equal parts
Aleksandrouli and Mujuretuli, while Royal Khvanchkara lists 60% and 40%
respectively. These are documented examples, not a universal recipe or a
fixed division of flavour between the two varieties.

## Preserving sweetness

During [alcoholic fermentation](../concepts/alcoholic-fermentation.md),
yeast consumes grape sugar. Stopping the process before the sugar is
exhausted leaves [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md) in the
wine. The appellation specification describes incomplete fermentation
with the grape skins; its 2017 text gives a finished sugar range of
30–45 grams per litre.[^1]

Marani's technical sheet gives a concrete production example. The grapes
ferment in stainless steel, with circulation of juice through the skins.
After several days of [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), the winery
cools the wine when the desired sweetness is reached, then keeps it cold
before bottling.[^2] Its sheet describes raspberry and strawberry aromas,
with a balance of sweetness and acidity.

Cooling suppresses yeast activity, but preserving a sweet wine also means
controlling the microorganisms that could consume its remaining sugar.
The relationship between sweetness and stability helps explain why cellar
temperature and bottling practices matter alongside vineyard ripeness.

## Reading the style in context

Khvanchkara's sweetness softens the impression of acidity and tannin,
while its red-grape extraction gives it a different structure from a sweet
white wine. The balance varies with ripeness, extraction, and the amount
of sugar retained. Comparing it with a dry Aleksandrouli–Mujuretuli blend
from Racha makes the role of fermentation especially clear: the same
regional grapes can produce substantially different wines.
