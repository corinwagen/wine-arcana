# Soave

Soave is a white-wine region east of Verona in Italy's Veneto. Its wines are
based on [Garganega](../grapes/garganega.md), grown across a landscape of
terraced hills, valley floors, and lower slopes. The name covers several
geographical and production categories, so the part of the region and the
method of making the wine both matter.

Fresh-grape Soave usually emphasizes dry white wine with gentle fruit, a
floral character, and an almond-like finish. Recioto di Soave develops a
separate tradition of sweet wine from grapes dried after harvest.

## Hills, valleys, and vineyards

The region lies where the southern foothills of the Lessini mountains meet
the plain. Ancient volcanic activity left basaltic formations, especially in
the east, while limestone and other sedimentary materials occur farther west.
Erosion and deposition have added another layer of variation between hillsides
and lower ground.

These differences affect soil depth, drainage, rooting, and the supply of
water during the growing season. A steep, shallow-soiled plot and a deeper
valley vineyard can present very different conditions even when they share a
grape variety. Exposure and the movement of air through the valleys further
alter ripening and the speed at which leaves and bunches dry after rain.

Growers have made the hills cultivable with terraces, retaining walls, and
water channels. The traditional pergola spreads shoots overhead and shades
the hanging fruit. Maintaining those structures and controlling erosion are
continuing parts of vineyard work. Soave's FAO agricultural-heritage nomination
documents this agricultural landscape and the small holdings that maintain it.

## Reading the geographical names

Under the Soave DOC specification consolidated in October 2019, *Classico*
identifies the delimited historic zone within the communes of Soave and
Monteforte d'Alpone. *Colli Scaligeri* identifies another delimited hill zone.
The broader DOC also includes lower land beyond these areas.[^1]

The distinction gives a first indication of origin. Within the hills,
individual sites still vary in geology, altitude, and aspect. The 2019
specification also recognizes named additional geographical units, allowing
eligible wines to identify their origins more closely.[^1]

Soave Superiore DOCG is governed by its own specification, with stricter
production requirements. Recioto di Soave DOCG is the sweet dried-grape
denomination. The words DOC and DOCG identify regulated categories; vineyard
and producer information help explain the style within each.

## Grapes and wine

Soave DOC requires at least 70% Garganega.
[Trebbiano di Soave](../grapes/verdicchio.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) may contribute within the remaining 30%,
with a limited allowance for other authorized white varieties.[^1] Some
producers work entirely with Garganega; others use the partners to adjust the
blend's acidity, texture, and aromatic balance.

Garganega's vigour and long ripening season make crop and canopy decisions
important. Harvesting a larger crop from fertile ground, selecting ripe
bunches from a hillside, and keeping fruit for drying lead to different raw
materials. Fermentation and maturation then extend the range. Tank-aged wines
can emphasize freshness, while longer contact with
[lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md) gives a producer another way to develop
texture. Wood contributes according to its size, age, and the time the wine
spends in it.

For recioto, selected bunches lose water in a drying period before pressing.
This concentrates their sugar and other components. Fermentation leaves
residual sweetness, giving a fuller wine whose balance depends on retaining
enough acidity.
