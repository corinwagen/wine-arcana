# Szekszárd

Szekszárd is a red-wine district in southern Hungary, on hills west of the
Danube. [Kékfrankos](../grapes/blaufrankisch.md), Kadarka, and the red blend
Szekszárdi Bikavér give it a distinctive identity. The range also includes
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md) and the Cabernets, but the renewed attention to
local grapes has made comparatively delicate reds an important part of its
contemporary story.

## Hills beside the plain

Loess covers much of the landscape, with clay layers and other local
variations. Valleys divide the hills into different exposures, creating
meaningful differences in warmth and ripening within a relatively compact
district. Sites open towards the Great Plain can receive considerable heat;
more sheltered or northerly exposures provide different growing conditions.

The contrast appears in Heimann & Fiai's vineyard accounts. The estate's
southeast-facing Baranya parcels tend to ripen earlier and give fuller wines,
while its north-facing, partly forest-sheltered Bati Kereszt parcel ripens
later. These are observations of particular vineyards, useful for understanding
why a single regional tasting description would be inadequate.

## Blending and varietal wines

Bikavér brings Kékfrankos and Kadarka into a blend with other red grapes.
[Egri Bikavér](eger.md) belongs to a different Hungarian district; the shared
name connects two blending traditions without making their wines identical.
Single-variety bottlings allow a more direct comparison of Szekszárd's grapes.

Heimann & Fiai documents its work selecting Kadarka material and sorting fruit
to preserve the grape's light, juicy character. Its use of some
[whole bunches](../concepts/whole-cluster-fermentation.md) in fermentation and
relatively neutral maturation vessels shows how cellar choices can support
that aim. The estate also identifies shrivelling in very hot, dry weather and
botrytis susceptibility as reasons to judge Kadarka's harvest timing carefully.
