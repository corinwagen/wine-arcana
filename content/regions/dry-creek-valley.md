# Dry Creek Valley

Dry Creek Valley is a narrow wine-growing valley northwest of Healdsburg in
[Sonoma County](sonoma-county.md), California. Its identity joins old
[Zinfandel](../grapes/zinfandel.md) vineyards with a sustained tradition of
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md). Those grapes offer distinct
ways to understand a region with warm days and access to coastal cooling.

The American Viticultural Area, or AVA, was recognized in 1983. Vineyard land
extends from the valley floor onto benches and hills, with different rooting
conditions and exposures within a relatively small district.

## Warmth and the landscape

Coastal hills shelter the valley from some ocean influence, allowing sunny
afternoons to supply substantial warmth. Cool air and fog still reach the
district, especially at night. The balance of shelter and cooling differs
from the more directly exposed vineyards of the
[Sonoma Coast](sonoma-coast.md).

The growers' association describes deep gravelly and sandy loams on the
valley floor, with gravelly clay loams on surrounding benches and hills.
These differences affect drainage, rooting, water storage, and vine growth.
They also help explain why neighboring blocks can require different canopy
and irrigation decisions.

## Zinfandel and mixed vineyards

Ridge's Lytton Springs supplies a documented connection between old planting
and current winemaking. Its Zinfandel vines grow alongside
[Petite Sirah](../grapes/durif.md), [Carignan](../grapes/carignan.md),
[Mourvèdre](../grapes/mourvedre.md), and [Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md). Ridge began making the wine
in 1972 and continues to ferment varieties and lots separately before
assembling the final blend.

That separation gives the cellar flexibility when grapes ripen at different
rates. Companion varieties contribute their own proportions of colour,
acidity, and tannin. A [mixed vineyard](../concepts/field-blends.md) therefore preserves several possible
components, as well as a historical planting pattern.

Zinfandel's uneven ripening creates an additional decision within the grape
itself. Picking later can include more shriveled berries and increase
potential alcohol; earlier picking can retain more freshness while leaving
some berries less mature. Sorting and [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md)
then shape the fruit that reaches the finished wine.

## Sauvignon Blanc and Fumé Blanc

The local growers' association connects the modern Sauvignon Blanc tradition
with David Stare and Lou Preston during the 1970s. Dry Creek Vineyard has
made Fumé Blanc since 1972, giving readers a continuing reference for the
valley's white-wine development.

Fumé Blanc is a wine name used for Sauvignon Blanc, with cellar treatment
varying by producer. Dry Creek Vineyard's 2024 bottling fermented entirely
in stainless steel. Its technical sheet records a small
[Sémillon](../grapes/semillon.md) component; the producer also describes
combining Dry Creek and [Russian River Valley](russian-river-valley.md) fruit. The wine consequently
carries the broader Sonoma County appellation.

This distinction helps with reading labels: a winery can have a durable
connection to Dry Creek Valley while making a particular wine from several
districts. The producer name, grape name, and geographical designation each
answer a different question.
