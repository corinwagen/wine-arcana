# Torgiano

Torgiano is a wine district in [Umbria](umbria.md), around the town of the same
name south of Perugia. [Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) anchors its red
wines, alongside white blends and varietal whites. It gives a different view
of Umbrian winemaking from
[Montefalco](montefalco.md), where Sagrantino has a defining role.

Two protected denominations share the place name. **Torgiano DOC** covers a
range of wines; **Torgiano Rosso Riserva DOCG** is a separate denomination for
Sangiovese-based reds with longer mandatory maturation. Reading the complete
name distinguishes these two sets of rules.

## The vineyard landscape

Vineyards occupy the hills around the Tiber and Chiascio rivers. Ancient lake
and river deposits have left combinations of sand, clay, and conglomerate,
alongside other sedimentary and volcanic material. A vineyard's drainage and
rooting conditions consequently depend on its particular position.

Both denominations restrict eligible land. The DOC excludes recent, wet
alluvial ground along the two rivers; the DOCG also excludes specified valley
bottoms and drainage lines on the northern side of the Brufa hill.[^land]

## Sangiovese and time in the cellar

Under the consolidated 2023 rules, Rosso di Torgiano DOC uses 50–100%
Sangiovese. Its production includes at least six months in wood. Torgiano
Rosso Riserva DOCG raises the Sangiovese minimum to 70% and requires at least
36 months' ageing, including 12 in wood and six in bottle, counted from
1 November of the harvest year.[^rules]

Those requirements leave room for different proportions of complementary
grapes and different approaches to [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md).
Large casks and small barrels expose wine to different amounts of wood
relative to volume. Extraction during fermentation also affects how much
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) enters the wine before maturation begins.

Lungarotti's Rubesco offers a long-running local reference, with its first
commercial vintage in 1962. The current Rubesco62 is entirely Sangiovese,
replacing the earlier Sangiovese–Colorino blend. Its published method uses
steel fermentation, skin contact of no more than 15 days, and less than a
year in barrel. It illustrates a producer pursuing a more immediately
accessible red within the DOC framework.

Terre Margaritelli's Freccia degli Scacchi supplies another documented
interpretation: a Torgiano Rosso Riserva made entirely from Sangiovese.
Comparing it with a DOC red brings both producer choices and the longer
DOCG ageing framework into view.

## White wine and texture

Bianco di Torgiano permits 20–70% Trebbiano Toscano, with other eligible
non-aromatic white grapes completing the blend.[^rules] This creates scope
for whites whose character depends on both grape selection and cellar method.

Lungarotti's Torre di Giano Vigna il Pino combines Trebbiano, Grechetto, and
[Vermentino](../grapes/vermentino.md). The estate describes fermenting one
third of the juice in barriques and the remainder in steel, followed by
blending and extended bottle maturation. The barrel component also spends
time on its fine lees. Dividing the juice between vessels allows wood
influence and [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) to contribute texture
while preserving a substantial component fermented without oak.

[^land]: *Torgiano DOC* and *Torgiano Rosso Riserva DOCG production
    specifications*, consolidated 2023 texts, articles 3–4.
[^rules]: The same specifications, articles 2 and 5. The DOCG's 36 months
    include its required wood and bottle periods.
