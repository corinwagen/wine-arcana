# Languedoc

Languedoc is a broad wine-growing region of southern France between the
Mediterranean coast and inland hills. Red blends, rosé, dry whites, sparkling
wine, and sweet fortified Muscat occupy different parts of its landscape. The
name is also used for a particular regional appellation, whose boundaries and
rules are distinct from the wider geographical idea.

Distance from the sea, elevation, wind, and local water supply vary widely. A
coastal plain, an exposed hillside, and a more sheltered inland valley offer
different growing conditions even when planted with the same grape.

## Mediterranean conditions with local variation

Summer heat and dry conditions make water availability important. The soil's
depth, texture, and access to fractured bedrock influence what a vine can draw
on through the season. [Wind](../concepts/wind-and-wine-growing.md) changes
humidity and canopy drying while also increasing physical exposure and water
demand.

The Pays d'Hérault account from the regional wine body emphasizes geological
complexity, changing elevations, and different distances from the sea. These
conditions affect rooting, water supply, and the timing of ripening.

Farther west, Limoux receives an Atlantic influence alongside its Mediterranean
setting and proximity to the Pyrenees. Its still and sparkling white-wine
traditions provide a valuable comparison with warmer districts associated
primarily with red blends.

## Grapes and wine routes

[Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md), [Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md),
[Mourvèdre](../grapes/mourvedre.md), [Carignan](../grapes/carignan.md), and
[Cinsault](../grapes/cinsault.md) are recurring red-wine grapes. Their
contributions depend on site, fruit condition, and extraction; the final
[blend](../concepts/blending.md) can balance lots that developed differently
through the same season.

White varieties include Grenache Blanc, Clairette, Bourboulenc, Picpoul,
Roussanne, Marsanne, Vermentino, and Viognier in different local contexts. A
white-wine appellation and a broader regional white do not necessarily permit
the same combination. Local specifications supply that detail.

Limoux's sparkling production connects Chardonnay, Chenin Blanc, Mauzac, and
Pinot Noir through distinct appellations and methods. Crémant de Limoux,
Blanquette de Limoux, and the ancestral-method tradition need separate names
because they describe different production frameworks.

## Several kinds of geographical label

The regional Languedoc AOP sits alongside more focused appellations such as
Faugères, Saint-Chinian, Pic Saint Loup, and Terrasses du Larzac. Each has its
own boundaries and production requirements.

IGP wines supply another important route, including varietal bottlings whose
labels make the grape especially visible. IGP wines also have geographical and
production requirements. The distinction is explained under [appellations and
wine labels](../concepts/appellations-and-wine-labels.md).
