# Arbois

Arbois is a wine appellation around the town of the same name in the northern
[Jura](jura.md), in eastern France. It brings together the region's principal
still-wine traditions: pale reds, firmer reds, fresh whites, whites matured
under yeast, and sweet wines from dried grapes. The appellation was recognised
on 15 May 1936.[^1]

## Slopes and grapes

Vineyards lie below the limestone edge of the Jura plateau and on nearby
hills. Marl and clay retain water beneath a covering of limestone debris that
helps drainage. In this rainy climate, growers favour sheltered slopes with
enough sun to ripen their grapes. Warm, gravelly sites are particularly useful
for [Trousseau](../grapes/trousseau.md).

[Poulsard](../grapes/poulsard.md), also called Ploussard, gives many of the
lightest-coloured reds. [Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) and Trousseau can
bring deeper colour and firmer structure. Producers bottle both individual
varieties and blends. Pupillin, just south of Arbois, has a strong association
with Poulsard; **Arbois-Pupillin** identifies wine meeting the rules for that
commune's supplementary geographical designation.[^2]

## White wines and cellar choices

[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) and [Savagnin](../grapes/savagnin.md)
anchor the white wines. The cellar method is especially useful information
when choosing between them. *Ouillé* means topped up: wine lost during
maturation is replaced to keep the vessel full. These wines can show the
grapes' fruit and acidity clearly, with further differences from site, barrel
use, and [time on the lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md).

For *sous voile* wines, producers allow a yeast veil to develop on the surface
of wine in partly filled barrels. This gives a more savoury, nutty character.
[Vin jaune](../styles/vin-jaune.md) is the long-matured expression of this
practice, centred on Savagnin. Arbois has a tradition of using lofts or attics
for part of its maturation, where seasonal temperature changes can be large.
Wink Lorch associates these conditions with more evaporation and an overt
walnut character, while stressing that cellars and wines vary.[^3]

[Vin de paille](../styles/vin-de-paille.md) concentrates grapes by drying them
after harvest. Arbois rules require at least six weeks of drying before
pressing; the resulting sweet wine follows its own extended maturation.[^2]
