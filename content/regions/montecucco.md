# Montecucco

Montecucco is a wine-growing area in southern Tuscany, extending from the
inland [Maremma](maremma.md) towards Mount Amiata in Grosseto province.
[Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) is its principal red-grape reference,
with [Ciliegiolo](../grapes/ciliegiolo.md) and other varieties contributing to
blends. Its hills provide another setting for Sangiovese alongside nearby
[Brunello di Montalcino](brunello-di-montalcino.md) and
[Morellino di Scansano](morellino-di-scansano.md).

Two names need distinguishing on a bottle: **Montecucco DOC** covers several
wine types, including red, white, Vermentino, rosé, and Vin Santo, while
**Montecucco Sangiovese DOCG** has its own specification. DOC and DOCG expand
to Denominazione di Origine Controllata and Denominazione di Origine
Controllata e Garantita. They identify production rules as well as origin.

## Hills, soils, and water

The district reaches from lower hills towards the slopes of Amiata. The
Sangiovese specification describes soils developed from sandstone and other
sedimentary formations, often stony, with variable water storage. It also
records a Mediterranean climate with substantial autumn and winter rainfall
and frequently dry summers. Soil depth, exposure, and rooting influence how
long stored water remains available to the vine.

ColleMassari provides a concrete site example near Cinigiano. Its estate
describes vineyards on a plateau facing the Tyrrhenian coast, with sandstone,
calcareous marl, and sandy clay among its soils. The estate also manages water
through ponds and wells. An inland hill setting can moderate growing
conditions while still requiring active attention to summer water supply.

## Reading the Sangiovese designation

The ministry's consolidated 2024 specification requires at least 90%
Sangiovese for Montecucco Sangiovese DOCG. The ordinary wine requires at least
12 months in wood and cannot be released before 1 April of the second year
after harvest. Riserva requires at least 30 months of maturation, including
24 in wood and six in bottle, with release no earlier than 1 September of
the third year after harvest.[^rules]

These requirements leave room for substantial cellar variation. Barrel size,
age, extraction, and the proportion of companion grapes still affect the
wine. A producer can also use Sangiovese alone.

## Two cellar paths

ColleMassari's Rigoleto, labeled Montecucco Rosso DOC, combines Sangiovese
with Ciliegiolo and [Montepulciano](../grapes/montepulciano.md). The producer
describes selecting earlier-harvested grapes and using stainless steel with
a brief passage in large oak. This gives a useful example of an approach
directed towards freshness and earlier drinking.

The same estate's Poggio Lombrone is a Montecucco Sangiovese Riserva DOCG
made entirely from Sangiovese. Fermentation in small wooden vats includes
manual punchdowns, followed by more than two years in large Slavonian oak
casks. Here [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) and extended
[maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) have a larger role in developing
structure before release.

[^rules]: Italian Ministry of Agriculture, *Montecucco Sangiovese DOCG
    production specification*, consolidated text of the 12 April and
    6 September 2024 decisions, articles 2 and 5.
