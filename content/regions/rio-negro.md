# Río Negro

Río Negro is an Argentine province with wine vineyards in northern Patagonia.
Its river valleys provide a different growing landscape from the high Andean
vineyards of [Mendoza](mendoza.md). It is especially interesting for old
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) vineyards, although
[Malbec](../grapes/malbec.md), [Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), and white varieties
also contribute to its wines.

## River valleys and wind

The regional geography follows the Río Negro and Colorado river basins across
the province. The Alto Valle is an important vineyard district, while lower
ground farther east receives more Atlantic influence. These places should
not be collapsed into one climate simply because they belong to Patagonia.

The Alto Valle's soils reflect river deposition, with mixtures of sand, clay,
loam, and gravel. Soil profiles can change substantially over short distances.
Wines of Argentina's regional dossier also identifies strong spring winds
and the use of poplar windbreaks. Wind can help dry the canopy, but exposed
shoots and fruit require protection from its force.

This setting makes vineyard layout part of wine growing. A windbreak changes
air movement; soil layers determine drainage and stored water. Neither a
southern latitude nor an old planting can explain the wine without those
details. The region's white grapes include
[Sémillon](../grapes/semillon.md),
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md), and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md), giving these conditions a role beyond
red wine.

## Old vineyards and current Pinot Noir

Bodega Chacra's range documents a close connection between vineyard history
and separate bottlings. Treinta y Dos comes from a vineyard planted in 1932,
while Cincuenta y Cinco comes from vines planted in 1955.[^vines] The producer
describes whole-cluster fermentation for the latter and maturation using
concrete and previously used barrels. The age of the planting identifies its
history; the harvest and cellar choices still determine extraction and
texture.

Chacra's Barda comes from younger vines, providing a comparison within the
same range. Its documented concrete fermentation and mixed maturation
vessels offer another route to Pinot Noir. These methods can keep wood aromas
from dominating, while [whole
bunches](../concepts/whole-cluster-fermentation.md) and skin contact remain
important to the red's structure.

In an August 2026 survey, Alejandro Iglesias reports regional growers
bringing harvest forward and reducing extraction or wood influence in pursuit
of fresher Pinot Noir. He includes Humberto Canale, Ribera del Cuarzo, and
Familia Miras among the Río Negro examples. This makes the region relevant as
both a home of established vineyards and a place where producers continue
to reconsider how to use their fruit.
