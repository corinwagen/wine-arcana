# Lugana

Lugana is a white-wine appellation around the southern end of Lake Garda,
straddling Lombardy and Veneto. Its principal grape is locally called Turbiana
or Trebbiano di Lugana, regional names associated with
[Verdicchio](../grapes/verdicchio.md). Dry still wines form its centre, alongside
sparkling, late-harvest, and longer-matured versions.

The combination of a moderating lake and clay-rich ground distinguishes this
setting from Verdicchio's homes in Marche. Lugana often joins citrus and almond
impressions with a substantial middle palate, giving producers scope to work
with both freshness and texture.

## The lake and the ground beneath it

The denomination reaches across Desenzano del Garda, Sirmione, Pozzolengo, and
Lonato del Garda on the Lombardy side and Peschiera del Garda in Veneto. The
lake stores heat and moderates nearby temperatures. Breezes move through the
vineyards, and the local climate has gentler temperature swings than more
continental inland locations.

Much of the core vineyard landscape is low-lying, with calcareous clays formed
from glacial and sedimentary materials. These soils can become hard in dry
weather and sticky when wet. Their water-holding capacity helps sustain vines
through dry periods, while their workability constrains when machinery can
enter a vineyard.

Farther south, toward Pozzolengo and Lonato, the land becomes more undulating.
Sand and gravel occur more prominently within the morainic terrain. Changes
in slope and soil texture alter drainage and the water roots can
reach.

## Turbiana

A 2012 study using genetic and morphological evidence found that samples named
Trebbiano di Lugana or Turbiana belonged to the same variety as Verdicchio and
Trebbiano di Soave.[^1] The local name retains an important regional identity,
including the history of selection and cultivation around Garda.

The DOC specification amended on 22 December 2014 requires at least 90%
Trebbiano di Soave, identified locally as Turbiana or Trebbiano di Lugana. Up to
10% other eligible, non-aromatic white grapes may be used.[^2] Wines made
entirely from Turbiana are common.

Compact bunches make fruit health important during humid harvest weather.
Waiting for greater ripeness means weighing the gain in fruit weight against
disease pressure and changes in [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md).

## Five expressions

The appellation includes Lugana, Superiore, Riserva, Vendemmia Tardiva, and
Spumante. Superiore requires at least twelve months of ageing. Riserva requires
twenty-four, including six in bottle; both periods run from 1 October of the
harvest year.[^2]

Tank maturation is widespread. Time on the
[lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can develop texture while preserving a profile
centred on fruit. Some producers also use wood, either alone or in combination
with tank. Vessel size, age, and duration of contact determine how strongly
wood affects the result. With bottle age, some wines develop a broader,
more savoury character alongside their initial citrus and floral impressions.

Vendemmia Tardiva uses grapes left to become overripe on the vine, commonly
harvested toward late October or early November. This gives the grower a
different route to concentration from drying harvested bunches indoors. The
finished wine's sweetness is balanced by the grape's acidity, with harvest
conditions and fermentation determining the proportions.

Spumante can undergo its secondary fermentation in tank or bottle. Tank
fermentation lends itself to a fresh fruit emphasis, while bottle fermentation
allows extended contact with yeast sediment.
