# Santorini

Santorini is a wine region in the southern Aegean, encompassing the Greek
islands of Santorini, or Thira, and neighbouring Thirasia. Its dry whites
centre on [Assyrtiko](../grapes/assyrtiko.md), combining firm acidity with
substantial flavour and body. Sweet Vinsanto develops another expression of
the local grapes through sun-drying and prolonged ageing in wood.

Vineyards extend from near sea level to terraces around 300 metres in
altitude. Wind, summer drought, and volcanic ground shape cultivation.
The low, basket-like coils of the traditional *kouloura* training system
protect bunches as they ripen close to the soil.

## Wind, stone, and the shape of the vine

Volcanic ash, pumice, sand, and other erupted material form much of the
vineyard ground. On slopes, stone terraces known as *pezoules* hold planting
areas and help capture rainfall. These parcels require work by hand, and
their accessibility differs from the flatter coastal vineyards.

The dry summer makes stored moisture valuable. Winter rain replenishes the
ground; humid air and sea mist also reach the vineyards during the growing
season. The effect depends on exposure and weather. A vine's rooting depth
and the moisture held within its particular soil help determine how it
endures the rainless period.

Strong northerly winds can cool the vineyard while battering exposed shoots
and bunches. In a kouloura, growers bend and weave canes into a low crown.
Fruit develops within the shelter of the resulting basket. Maintaining that
shape requires repeated skilled pruning; the vineyard's appearance follows
from the practical need to protect its crop.

Santorini also retains [ungrafted vines](../concepts/grafting.md). Its sandy, clay-poor volcanic soils
have protected the vineyard from the phylloxera devastation experienced in
many European regions. The survival of old vines and their local training
systems gives the island an unusual continuity of vineyard practice.

## Dry Santorini and Nykteri

The appellation was established in 1971. Its current dry-white blend requires
at least 85% Assyrtiko, with Athiri and Aidani permitted alongside it.[^1]
Many producers use Assyrtiko alone. The grape can retain marked acidity as
sugar accumulates, giving ripe wines a firm structure. Citrus flavours are
common, with a saline impression often used to describe the finish.

Cellar choices alter that structure. Maturation on fine
[lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md) can broaden the middle palate, while
[oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) can introduce further texture
and flavour. These treatments produce a range from taut young whites to
fuller wines suited to development in bottle.

*Nykteri* is a protected traditional term for dry Santorini with at least
13.5% natural alcoholic strength by volume and a minimum of three months
in wooden barrels.[^1] Hatzidakis gives a more extended example: its Nykteri
uses ripe Assyrtiko from Megalochori, short cold skin contact, fermentation
with native yeasts, and twelve months in French oak barrels of different
sizes. The
producer bottles it unfiltered. Harvest maturity and this longer cellar
programme help explain its full-bodied style.

## Vinsanto

Vinsanto begins with overripe grapes dried in the sun. Water loss concentrates
the must, providing the basis for a sweet wine whose acidity remains an
important part of its balance. The blend requires at least 51% Assyrtiko;
Aidani, Athiri, and small quantities of other traditional grapes may join it.
The rules cover both naturally sweet and fortified versions, with at least
24 months of oxidative ageing in oak.[^1]

Santo Wines describes its standard naturally sweet Vinsanto as 85% Assyrtiko
and 15% Aidani. Grapes dry for six to eight days, fermentation lasts forty
to sixty days, and the wine spends three years in oak. Its much longer-aged
1993 bottling spent 23 years in French oak. Such extended maturation deepens
the colour and develops aromas of dried fruit, nuts, coffee, and spice.

[^1]: European Commission, approved Santorini specification amendment and
    single document, published 31 March 2022, sections 5 and 9; checked
    14 September 2026. The dry-white Assyrtiko minimum rose from 75% to 85%.
