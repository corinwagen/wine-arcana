# Csopak

Csopak is a village and winegrowing area on the northern shore of Hungary's
Lake [Balaton](balaton.md). Its strongest contemporary identity is built around dry
[Olaszrizling](../grapes/olaszrizling.md), particularly wines that identify
individual vineyards. It lies within the broader Balatonfüred-Csopak district,
a larger area with a wider range of wines.

## Slopes above the lake

Vineyards rise from the lakeshore toward wooded hills. Exposures, distance
from the water, and air moving down from higher ground influence warmth and
humidity. The Hungarian Wine Marketing Agency describes substantial local
geological variation: marl, limestone, shale, and red sandstone occur in
different parts of the area. Red sandstone is an important feature of some
vineyards, but should not be treated as the soil beneath every Csopak vine.

These differences give practical meaning to vineyard names. A lower parcel
near the lake can have a different moisture regime from a more exposed
hillside, even when both carry the same grape.

## Olaszrizling and individual vineyards

Olaszrizling's relatively restrained perfume gives ripeness, [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), and
texture a prominent role in the wine. A grower can emphasize an immediately
approachable white or a more concentrated expression suited to maturation.

Jásdi's Siralomvágó illustrates the area's interest in named sites. The estate
identifies the wine as Olaszrizling from its Siralomvágó hillside, and the
national agency discusses the vineyard as part of Csopak's contemporary wine
identity. It is a useful reference for understanding why the vineyard name
matters alongside the grape.

For orientation, keep the scales separate: Csopak is a particular place,
Balatonfüred-Csopak is the broader district, and Lake Balaton has other
winegrowing areas with different grapes and landscapes.
