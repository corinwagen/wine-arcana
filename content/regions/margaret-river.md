# Margaret River

Margaret River is a maritime wine region in southwestern Western Australia.
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) are important references, while
[Sémillon](../grapes/semillon.md) and
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) supply a regional white-blend
tradition. Ocean exposure and the choice of planting material help explain
its modern wine identity.

## Maritime growing conditions

Wine Australia's account describes a strongly maritime climate with relatively
moderate day-to-night temperature differences. Gravelly loams over granite
and gneiss occur along the ridge between Cape Naturaliste and Cape Leeuwin.
Their water-holding capacity can be limited, making rooting and seasonal
water supply important alongside coastal cooling.

For Cabernet, a moderated growing season gives growers opportunities to
complete ripening while retaining acidity. Site exposure and vintage weather
determine the actual harvest window. [Coonawarra](coonawarra.md) offers
another Australian Cabernet comparison, with a different landscape and soil
history.

## Planting material and wine style

The regional association identifies Houghton as a heritage mass selection
of Cabernet Sauvignon. That distinction matters: a selection drawn from
several source vines can retain variation within the variety.
[Massal and clonal selection](../concepts/massal-and-clonal-selection.md)
explains how growers preserve and evaluate such material.

For Chardonnay, the association describes Gingin material and other clones.
Gingin can produce bunches with berries of different sizes, commonly called
*hen and chicken* berries. Plant material affects the crop available to
the cellar; barrel fermentation, malolactic conversion, and lees contact
then shape the wine's texture.

Sémillon–Sauvignon blends span fresh tank-made wines and more textured
expressions involving wood or other vessels. Changing proportions changes
the balance between aroma, acidity, and palate breadth. This white-wine
tradition sits alongside varietal Chardonnay and Cabernet-based reds.

## A producer's vineyard and blending choices

Vasse Felix describes its Home Vineyard at Wilyabrup as established in
1967, with gravelly loams and sea-breeze exposure.[^vineyard] Its Cabernet
account traces the use of Malbec as a blending component to the first
release in 1972. This supplies a durable example of a regional blend
developed from particular plantings.

The producer also describes small-batch spontaneous fermentation,
maceration, basket pressing, and French-oak maturation. Each operation
acts on a different part of the wine: skin contact extracts structure,
pressing separates fractions, and maturation changes aroma and texture.
Those choices explain differences among Cabernet wines beyond their
shared maritime origin.

[^vineyard]: Vasse Felix, “Cabernet Sauvignon,” Home Vineyard account and
    Cabernet history.
