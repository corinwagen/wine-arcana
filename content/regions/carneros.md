# Carneros

Carneros occupies the low hills north of San Pablo Bay, crossing the boundary
between [Napa Valley](napa-valley.md) and
[Sonoma County](sonoma-county.md). Its combination of marine cooling and a
largely dry growing season supports [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md),
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md), and sparkling wine. It supplies a useful
southern comparison with Napa's warmer Cabernet districts.

Recognized as an American Viticultural Area, or AVA, in 1983, Carneros follows
a growing landscape across two counties. Los Carneros is another established
form of the name. The appellation identifies vineyard origin; producers use
its fruit for several different harvest and cellar objectives.

## Bay air and the rooting zone

Afternoon breezes from San Pablo Bay moderate heat and can expose vineyards
to substantial wind. Fog also changes the timing of sunlight. The rolling
terrain creates differences in shelter and exposure within this maritime
setting.

The Carneros Wine Alliance describes predominantly clay soils, often with
restrictive subsoil layers. Clay can retain winter water, while limited
rooting depth constrains how much of that reserve a vine can use. Water
management therefore depends on the whole soil profile: a clay-rich surface
does not establish that roots have an extensive summer supply.

## One grape, different wines

Pinot Noir makes the consequences of skin contact particularly clear. Red
wine ferments with the skins to extract pigment and tannin; early separation
of juice from skins permits a pale wine from the same dark grape. Harvest
decisions also differ when the intended wine will undergo another
fermentation in bottle.

Domaine Carneros provides a continuing example of both still Pinot Noir and
[traditional-method sparkling wine](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md).
For sparkling production, it blends separately made base wines before adding
sugar and yeast for the bottle fermentation. The producer describes at least
three years of bottle ageing for its vintage sparkling wines. The vineyard
supplies the fruit; blending and time on yeast sediment add further texture
and maturation character.

Its still Pinot Noir follows another path. Blocks are picked, fermented, and
aged separately, with French oak maturation before final assembly. Comparing
these wines directs attention to picking, extraction, and maturation as well
as the common regional origin.

## A still white from Pinot Noir

Schug's 2024 White Pinot Noir offers a third, particularly instructive use.
The producer took pale juice from Carneros Pinot Noir, fermented it in neutral
French oak without skin contact, and kept it on its lees for five months.
It is a still white, with neither red-wine extraction nor a second
fermentation for bubbles.

The example connects [pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) with
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md): limiting skin extraction preserves
pale colour, while fermentation and contact with yeast sediment can build
texture. Neutral barrels supply a maturation vessel with relatively little
new-wood aroma. Carneros is thus useful for understanding how quite different
wines emerge from related vineyard material.
