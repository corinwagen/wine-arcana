# Livermore Valley

Livermore Valley is a wine region east of San Francisco Bay in California.
Its inland location brings warm summer days, but passages through the
surrounding hills admit cooling marine air. The combination supports a
broader range of grapes than either “coastal” or “inland” alone might imply.

The region is significant both for its wines and for plant material that
spread beyond it. Wente's work with [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md),
Concannon's association with [Petite Sirah](../grapes/durif.md), and a
continuing tradition of Bordeaux varieties connect Livermore with the
development of California wine.

## Warm days within reach of the bay

The Livermore Valley American Viticultural Area, or AVA, lies within the
larger San Francisco Bay and Central Coast appellations. Established in
1982, it includes valley ground and surrounding hills, rather than only the
vineyards close to the city of Livermore.

The broad east–west orientation helps marine breezes move inland. More
detailed studies distinguish several airflow routes, including the Dublin
Grade and passages farther south around Sunol and Vallecitos. Their effects
vary with position: a sheltered slope and a vineyard open to a cooling
corridor need not experience the same temperature pattern.

The soils vary too. Jim Gordon's 2019 account of commissioned soil and
climate studies describes alluvial ground, sandstone, and mudstone, with
textures ranging from gravelly sand to clay. That variation changes how
much water the root zone stores and how growers manage irrigation and vine
growth. A regional shorthand about gravel is useful for some vineyards but
incomplete for the whole AVA.

## A history carried by grapes

Charles Wetmore established Cresta Blanca in 1882; Wente and Concannon
followed in 1883. These nineteenth-century establishments predate
California's modern emphasis on [Napa Valley](napa-valley.md) and
[Sonoma County](sonoma-county.md), placing Livermore within the state's early
commercial wine history.

Wente selected Chardonnay material that subsequently influenced plantings
across California. Its legacy is a useful reminder that a regional
contribution can travel through vines as well as bottles. It does not mean
all wines made from Wente-derived material share a Livermore character.

Concannon's 1961 varietally labelled Petite Sirah is another documented
reference point. The grape's firm tannin and deep colour made it useful for
blending; presenting it under its own name helped establish a different
commercial role. The winery's history connects that development to a
specific estate rather than to a claim that the grape originated here.

## Beyond a single regional grape

[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md),
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md), and
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) extend the regional comparison
beyond Chardonnay and Petite Sirah. Site selection matters within that
range. A later-ripening red benefits from enough seasonal warmth, while
marine exposure, harvest timing, and the intended wine influence the balance
retained in a white.

Wente and Concannon are useful historical reference producers, but the
appellation is wider than their estates. Vineyard origin should also be
checked on individual bottles: a winery based in Livermore may make wines
from other California regions or use a broader appellation.
