# Priorat

Priorat is a wine appellation in the hills of inland Tarragona, Catalonia,
associated especially with [Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md) and
[Carignan](../grapes/carignan.md). Slate-derived ground, steep slopes, and
marked differences in exposure make the work of growing grapes conspicuous in
its landscape.

The appellation uses the Catalan designation DOQ, Denominació d'Origen
Qualificada. Its defined wine area should be distinguished from the wider
administrative comarca and from neighbouring appellations.

## Slopes, slate, and water

The Montsant massif rises above a landscape of ridges and valleys. Vineyards
occupy steep hillsides called *costers*, terraces, and less precipitous ground.
Mechanization becomes difficult where slopes or narrow terraces leave little
room for equipment; retaining soil and reaching the vines are practical parts of
cultivation.

The local rock commonly called *llicorella* is associated with the area's
slate-derived soils. Fractures, the depth of weathered material, slope, and fine
soil influence rooting and water supply. The regulator's account also describes
substantial differences in vineyard altitude and harvesting time between
villages.

A warmer, exposed position and a cooler higher site may develop fruit at
different rates. Low yields can arise from limited water or vine capacity, but
stress must still allow the plant to ripen healthy grapes.

## Grapes and extraction

Grenache and Carignan are the principal traditional red varieties. Other
permitted red varieties broaden the blends, and Grenache Blanc and Macabeo are
among the grapes used for white wines.

The final red's texture depends on the condition of the skins and seeds,
fermentation, [maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), pressing, and maturation.
Later picking or stronger extraction can increase some kinds of concentration,
while changing alcohol, acidity, and tannin balance. Producers need to choose
those operations for the fruit and the intended wine.

[Oak](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) is another variable. A new small barrel and
an older large vessel have different extraction and oxygen histories.

## Continuity and revival

The Carthusian monastery of Scala Dei is central to the region's historical wine
identity. Its later history includes the disruption of phylloxera and rural
depopulation, followed by renewed investment and recognition toward the end of
the twentieth century. The regulator's account emphasizes both the newer
projects and the growers who preserved cultivation through the less prosperous
period.

That continuity matters to an account of [old vines](../concepts/old-vines.md):
surviving plantings are also agricultural and cultural records.
