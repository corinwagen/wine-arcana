# Sonoma Coast

Sonoma Coast is a broad American Viticultural Area, or AVA, in
[Sonoma County](sonoma-county.md), California. Its vineyards share varying
degrees of Pacific influence, but the name covers more than the narrow strip
of exposed hills immediately beside the ocean. This breadth matters when
comparing its Pinot Noir and Chardonnay: coastal distance, shelter, elevation,
and fog exposure can differ substantially between wines carrying the same
appellation.

West Sonoma Coast is a smaller AVA established in 2022 within Sonoma Coast.
It includes the Fort Ross-Seaview AVA. The creation of the smaller area left
the broader Sonoma Coast designation intact.[^1] A bottle labelled Sonoma
Coast may therefore come from a vineyard also entitled to a more specific
coastal name.

## The coast above and within the fog

Marine influence generally lowers daytime temperatures and moderates
temperature swings relative to more inland sites. Fog and low cloud alter
the amount and timing of sunshine. Steep coastal terrain divides vineyards
into exposures with different access to that light and different protection
from wind.

Some elevated vineyards emerge above the fog while lower sites remain within
it. At Hirsch, in Fort Ross-Seaview, ocean-facing vines can receive more fog
and wind and ripen later than more sheltered exposures. The estate's account
also identifies greater difficulty with fruit set in those exposed blocks.
“Coastal” thus describes a set of growing conditions to investigate, not a
guarantee that every slope ripens slowly in the same way.

The federal West Sonoma Coast rule identifies the rugged terrain and widespread
Franciscan Complex rocks as distinguishing features of the smaller area.
Their weathering contributes relatively thin, sandy soils in many places.
For growers, the consequences concern drainage, root space, and water
availability, alongside the practical difficulty of farming steep land.

## Pinot Noir and Chardonnay in small parcels

[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) are central grapes of the cool coastal
vineyards. The season must supply enough warmth to mature fruit, while harvest
date sets the balance between ripeness and retained
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Winemaking can produce either a restrained
or a more substantial interpretation of the same site.

Hirsch separates its vineyard into small farming and fermentation blocks,
then makes both broader estate blends and wines from particular sections.
Its published cellar practices describe predominantly destemmed Pinot Noir,
open fermenters, and moderate extraction. Comparing its San Andreas Fault
blend with a ridge or block bottling therefore explores different selections
within one estate.

Chardonnay can develop further texture through
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) and
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md). Hirsch's
Chardonnay, for example, ferments in oak and later moves to stainless steel.
Vessel changes let a producer separate the roles of fermentation, wood
contact, and subsequent maturation.

## A place for cool-grown Syrah

[Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md) adds another useful coastal comparison. At Peay's
estate in the far northwest of the county, the producer describes cool autumn
weather extending the ripening period into October. Its La Bruma and Les
Titans bottlings offer different selections of estate Syrah, with aromatic
and structural differences within the same growing site.

Peay also describes the farming cost of maritime humidity: moisture can
increase mildew and rot pressure, while wind helps dry foliage and fruit.
This gives a practical counterpart to the appealing idea of a long, cool
season. Growers need fruit to remain healthy while they wait for it to ripen.
