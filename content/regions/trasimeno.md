# Trasimeno

Trasimeno is the wine-growing area around Lake Trasimeno in northwestern
[Umbria](umbria.md). Its most distinctive naming puzzle is *Gamay del
Trasimeno*: the local red grape belongs to
[Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md), rather than to the Gamay associated with
Beaujolais. It gives the district a connection to Mediterranean Grenache
traditions while supporting its own range of red and rosato wines.

The lake district also grows [Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) and white
grapes, including Grechetto.
The local Gamay is a useful starting point because comparing its bottlings
shows how harvest date, skin contact, and maturation reshape the same variety.

## The lake and its hills

Vineyards lie around towns including Castiglione del Lago, Magione, Panicale,
Paciano, Passignano, and Tuoro. The regional consortium describes a landscape
influenced by Trasimeno and nearby lakes, with soils combining clay, sand,
calcareous material, and stones. Conditions vary with position around the lake
and the height and exposure of a vineyard.

Those differences matter when a grape can accumulate considerable sugar.
Exposure and the timing of harvest help determine the balance of fruit,
alcohol, and acidity.

## Which Gamay?

The Italian Vitis Database lists *Gamay perugino* among the documented synonyms
of Cannonau, alongside the established Grenache and Garnacha names. The
Trasimeno consortium identifies its local Gamay with that same variety.[^identity]
A reader searching for Gamay del Trasimeno should therefore follow the
Grenache family of names, while keeping the district's local material and
wine traditions in view.

## Red wine: cement, bunches, and oak

Madrevite offers a useful comparison within one estate. Its Opra red ferments
spontaneously and matures in cement before bottling. Its C'osa Riserva includes
a proportion of whole bunches during fermentation and uses both cement and
large oak in maturation. The estate's published method specifies 20% whole
bunches for C'osa; that is a bottling-specific choice, not a regional rule.

[Whole-cluster fermentation](../concepts/whole-cluster-fermentation.md) changes
what enters the vat: stems join the berries and can affect extraction,
fermentation behaviour, and texture. [Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md)
introduces another set of choices, including vessel size and previous use.
A large cask and a new small barrel need not leave the same aromatic imprint.

## Rosato starts with a different objective

Madrevite's La Bisbetica, labelled Umbria IGP, uses the same local Gamay for
[rosato](../styles/rose.md). The producer describes six to eight hours of cold
skin contact before soft pressing, followed by fermentation and maturation
in steel and cement. Its red-wine harvest for C'osa comes later than the
rosato picking.

Short contact extracts less colour and tannin than fermenting a red wine with
its skins. Picking earlier also changes the raw material before that
extraction begins. Rosato is consequently a vineyard and cellar decision in
its own right, with a different balance to seek from the later-picked red.
