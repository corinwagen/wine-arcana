# Bordeaux

Bordeaux is a wine region around the Garonne, Dordogne, and Gironde in
southwestern France. Its red wines connect [Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and [Cabernet
Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md) through a long tradition of blending. Dry
whites, sweet whites, rosé, and sparkling wine make the regional picture
considerably wider than its best-known estate reds.

Riverbanks and tributaries organize the regional names. Soils, exposure, and
distance inland help explain differences within them. A bottle labeled simply
Bordeaux and one bearing a smaller appellation name draw on different
geographical definitions.

## The banks and the land between them

The Left Bank lies west of the Garonne and Gironde. The Médoc extends north of
the city; Graves and Pessac-Léognan lie to its south. Gravelly rises are
important vineyard sites, but sand, clay, limestone, and differences in the
subsoil complicate any description of the bank as one kind of ground.

The Right Bank includes Saint-Émilion, Pomerol, Fronsac, and neighbouring
appellations around the Dordogne. Clay-rich sites and limestone plateaus provide
contrasting rooting environments. Between the Garonne and Dordogne,
Entre-deux-Mers supplies another large landscape of vineyards and smaller
appellations. The regional wine trade body's geographical grouping also includes
the hills of Blaye and Bourg.

Atlantic influence brings relatively moderate temperatures and rain. Drainage,
fruit health, and the weather near harvest matter alongside summer warmth. A
site that drains readily in a wet year may have less stored water during a dry
one. [Terroir](../concepts/terroir.md) here involves the vine's response to
those conditions and the grower's choices.

## Grapes and red-wine blends

Cabernet Sauvignon's late ripening helps explain its association with
well-exposed gravel sites in the Médoc. Merlot is more prominent across the
region as a whole and is especially important on the Right Bank. Cabernet Franc
supplies another major component, with Petit Verdot, Malbec, and Carménère
appearing in smaller quantities.

A gravel site can also carry Merlot; a Right Bank estate can give Cabernet Franc
a substantial role. Producers ferment parcels or varieties separately, assess
their performance in the particular year, and choose the final
[blend](../concepts/blending.md). Extraction, press-wine inclusion, oak, and
bottle development then affect texture and aroma.

## White and sweet wines

[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) and
[Sémillon](../grapes/semillon.md) anchor white production, with Muscadelle and
other permitted grapes contributing in some wines. A rapidly pressed,
tank-fermented white and one fermented or matured in barrel offer different
expressions of the same regional varieties.

Graves and Pessac-Léognan are important settings for dry whites. Sauternes and
Barsac are associated with [botrytized sweet
wine](../styles/botrytized-sweet-wine.md), where suitable weather allows noble
rot to concentrate fruit. Other sweet-wine appellations broaden that tradition.
The necessary selection and weather risk help explain why sweet wine follows a
different harvest logic from dry red.

## Reading a Bordeaux name

An [appellation](../concepts/appellations-and-wine-labels.md) defines origin and
production conditions. An estate classification is a separate layer;
classification terminology should be checked in its local context. The words
*grand cru* do not have one identical meaning across all Bordeaux labels.
