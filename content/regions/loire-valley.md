# Loire Valley

The Loire Valley is a broad wine-growing landscape following the Loire and its
tributaries from central France toward the Atlantic. Its wines range from dry
coastal whites to Cabernet Franc reds, rosés, sparkling wines, and sweet Chenin
Blanc. The river links them geographically without making them one style.

Oceanic influence is strong around Nantes and Anjou; more continental conditions
emerge through Touraine and the Centre-Loire. Grapes, geology, and local harvest
traditions change along that route.

## Nantes and the Atlantic end

[Muscadet](muscadet.md) centres on [Melon de
Bourgogne](../grapes/melon-de-bourgogne.md), also called Melon Blanc. Its dry
whites show how a relatively restrained grape can be shaped by pressing,
fermentation, and time on the [lees](../concepts/lees-aging.md). The region's
crystalline bedrock and varied soils affect rooting and water supply, while
nearby Atlantic weather makes fruit health and the timing of vineyard work
important.

Folle Blanche provides a separate local white-wine tradition through Gros Plant
du Pays Nantais. Gros Plant and Muscadet use different grapes and appellation
rules.

## Anjou, Saumur, and Touraine

[Chenin Blanc](../grapes/chenin-blanc.md) gives the middle Loire a particularly
wide range of wines. Growers can pick for sparkling base wine, make dry or
off-dry still wine, or select concentrated berries for sweet wine where
conditions permit. Acidity helps support these different routes, but the harvest
and fermentation decisions determine the finished balance.

[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md) connects red-wine appellations
including Chinon, Bourgueil, and Saumur-Champigny. Sand and gravel terraces,
limestone slopes, exposure, and harvest date offer meaningful comparisons within
that shared grape. Extraction and oak then broaden the range from supple young
reds to firmer wines intended for development.

Rosé and sparkling production are also central here. Grolleau, Gamay, and other
local grapes make the picture more varied than a simple division into Chenin
whites and Cabernet reds. The individual appellation specifies what can be used
and how the wine is made.

## Centre-Loire and the upper reaches

[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) is especially important in
Centre-Loire appellations such as [Sancerre](sancerre.md) and Pouilly-Fumé.
Sancerre also makes Pinot Noir red and rosé. Limestone, marl, flint-bearing
clays, and river deposits give different root environments within a climate with
greater seasonal temperature variation than the coast.

Beyond these internationally familiar names, the wider Loire basin includes
other tributary valleys and upland vineyards.
[Romorantin](../grapes/romorantin.md) and [Pineau
d'Aunis](../grapes/pineau-daunis.md) illustrate local traditions that a tour of
only the dominant varieties would miss.

## A region and several legal names

“Loire Valley” is useful geographical language, but it does not identify one
uniform appellation specification. Local AOPs and the wider IGP Val de Loire
have different boundaries and conditions. InterLoire's account of the IGP also
distinguishes the western ancient rocks, the sedimentary formations of Saumur
and Touraine, and the volcanic landscapes farther upstream.

The [appellation](../concepts/appellations-and-wine-labels.md) supplies
production rules, but sweetness may require further information: Chenin Blanc
from the same area can be fermented dry or retain sugar.
