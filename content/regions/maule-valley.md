# Maule Valley

Maule is a broad wine valley in central Chile, extending from the Andes across
the central corridor toward the coastal hills. Its vineyards include
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md),
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), and [Carménère](../grapes/carmenere.md), alongside
old plantings of [País](../grapes/pais.md) and
[Carignan](../grapes/carignan.md). Those surviving vineyards have become
important to contemporary wines sold with a more specific grape and site
identity.

## A broad valley and its dryland vineyards

The regional name covers different landscapes and farming systems. Mountain
proximity, slope, exposure, soil depth, and access to water change as vineyards
move across the valley. Maule's interior dryland area, or *secano interior*,
is especially associated with old head-trained vines. Their low, freestanding
framework differs from vineyards whose shoots are arranged along trellis
wires.

[Dry farming](../concepts/dry-farming.md) carries vines through the growing
season using rainfall and water stored in the soil. Its success depends on
rooting depth, winter replenishment, and the demand from leaves and fruit.
An old vine may have an established root system, but its crop and water supply
still need to fit the season. The age of a vineyard identifies its history
more securely than it predicts the finished wine's concentration.

## Carignan and VIGNO

The VIGNO collective gives a particularly clear definition to one part of
Maule's wine production. Its current rules require at least 85% Carignan,
vines at least thirty years old, dry farming, head training, and origin in
southern Maule's interior dryland area. Wines must age for at least twenty-four
months before release.[^rules] These are the collective's requirements, not
conditions applying to every Maule wine.

The permitted maturation includes wood, concrete, amphorae, and bottle, allowing
different cellar approaches within the shared vineyard criteria. Carignan's
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and tannin can provide a firm starting
structure. Harvest ripeness, extraction, blending, and maturation decide
whether the result emphasizes freshness or a more substantial, developed red.
The name VIGNO therefore tells a reader about farming and origin while leaving
room for producer and vintage differences.

## País and the wider cellar landscape

Bouchon's País Salvaje Blanco 2023 offers another use of established plant
material. Its technical record describes vines growing into native woodland
at Mingre, with fruit harvested using ladders. The producer pressed the grapes
and fermented the juice with native yeasts. This unusual planting supplies a
specific example of a dark-skinned grape used for a paler wine, through
[pressing](../concepts/pressing.md) and limited skin contact.

Bouchon's broader cellar description includes small barrels, large wooden
vessels, clay amphorae, concrete, and steel tanks. Such choices let the producer
handle lots separately and vary the contribution of wood and oxygen during
maturation. Together with VIGNO, the example shows how Maule's inherited
vineyards can support different contemporary interpretations.

[Itata](itata-valley.md) offers a useful southern comparison for País,
Cinsault, and established farming traditions. Maule also contains substantial
production from international varieties, so its heritage vineyards form one
important part of a diverse regional wine economy.
