# Castelli di Jesi

The Castelli di Jesi are the hill towns and surrounding wine country of the
Esino valley in Marche, central Italy. The area's principal white grape is
[Verdicchio](../grapes/verdicchio.md), which supplies fresh still wines,
longer-matured whites, sparkling wines, and sweet passito. Most vineyards lie
in the province of Ancona, with part of the production area extending into
Macerata.

The hills spread inland from the Adriatic toward the Apennines. This gives
growers a broad range of elevations, exposures, and degrees of maritime
influence. Wines can combine a citrus and floral character with substantial
texture and a slightly bitter, almond-like finish.

## An open valley and its hills

The Esino provides a geographical thread through the region. Settlements such
as Cupramontana, Montecarotto, and Staffolo occupy the surrounding hills, with
vineyards on slopes facing different directions. Sea breezes moderate parts
of the area, while higher inland ground experiences cooler conditions.

Soils vary along this progression. Marly and clay-rich materials and limestone
occur toward the Apennines; sandstone and mixed clay soils appear farther
down the hills, with sand more prominent toward the coast. Soil depth and
texture affect drainage and the water available to vines during summer.
Aspect then changes the sunlight reaching the canopy and fruit.

A useful comparison is inland [Matelica](matelica.md), where mountain ridges
shelter the vineyard valley from much of the Adriatic's influence. The Jesi
area includes a
wider transition between maritime and inland conditions. The individual slope
and vintage determine how strongly either influence is expressed.

## Verdicchio through the harvest

Verdicchio retains enough [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) to support several harvest and winemaking
choices. Earlier-picked fruit can give lighter wines with a sharper outline.
Longer ripening can develop greater weight and riper fruit, provided that the
acidity remains in balance. Producers seeking sparkling base wine or a
concentrated still white consequently work toward different harvest targets.

Umani Ronchi's Casal di Serra provides a concrete example of the latter. The
producer sources Verdicchio from slopes on both sides of the Esino valley,
at roughly 200–350 metres, with deep, moderately calcareous clay-loam soils.
Its usual harvest runs from late September into early October. The estate
tracks sugar, acidity, and phenolic maturity together when choosing the date.

The cellar extends those decisions. Casal di Serra ferments in stainless steel
with temperature control, avoids
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md),
and spends about five months on its yeast sediment. Keeping malic acid helps
preserve acidity, while [lees contact](../concepts/lees-aging.md) contributes to
texture. The resulting combination of freshness and substance offers one
approach to a fuller Verdicchio.

## The denominations

Verdicchio dei Castelli di Jesi DOC requires at least 85% Verdicchio under the
specification amended on 23 September 2025. Other eligible white grapes may
supply the remainder. The DOC includes still wine, spumante, spumante riserva, and
passito, as well as Superiore, Classico, and Classico Superiore categories.[^1]

Classico identifies the delimited historic production zone. Superiore adds
stricter production requirements and can accompany either the broader DOC
origin or Classico. Castelli di Jesi Verdicchio Riserva is a separate DOCG,
allowing Classico for eligible origins. Its
specification requires at least eighteen months of ageing, including six in
bottle.[^2]

These longer-matured wines give Verdicchio time to develop beyond its early
fruit aromas. Some producers use wood; others build texture through tank
maturation and lees. Passito takes a different path, concentrating selected
grapes through drying and retaining sweetness in the finished wine. Acidity
provides continuity across this range, balancing both a ripe dry white's body
and a sweet wine's sugar.
