# Willamette Valley

The Willamette Valley is a wine-growing region in western Oregon, extending from
near Portland south toward Eugene. [Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) is its
best-known grape, accompanied by Chardonnay, Pinot Gris, other cool-climate
grapes, and sparkling wine.

The Coast Range to the west and Cascades to the east frame the valley. Within
that shelter, hillsides, openings toward the Pacific, and different soils create
substantial variation. Smaller American Viticultural Areas, or AVAs, identify
parts of this large growing region.

## A dry summer in a wet-winter landscape

Much of the rain falls outside the main summer ripening period. Warm summer days
and cooler nights provide a different seasonal pattern from the persistent
growing-season rainfall that the image of the Pacific Northwest can suggest.
Autumn weather still matters when deciding how long to leave fruit on the vine.

The Coast Range limits direct ocean influence but contains openings through
which marine air enters. The Van Duzer Corridor is especially important to the
southern part of the northern vineyard concentration. Wind, elevation, and
shelter consequently change the temperature and water demand experienced by
vines over relatively short distances.

Volcanic, marine sedimentary, and windblown soils occur on the hills. The valley
floor also contains extensive flood deposits. These soils differ in depth,
drainage, nutrient supply, and water storage.

## Hills and smaller AVAs

Dundee Hills, Eola-Amity Hills, Yamhill-Carlton, Ribbon Ridge, and the Chehalem
Mountains are among the valley's more local names. Some smaller AVAs sit inside
another subregion as well as the larger Willamette Valley AVA.

The Willamette Valley Wineries Association's overview contrasts the wind
exposure of the Van Duzer Corridor with other hillsides and soil settings.

## Grapes and wine styles

Pinot Noir's early ripening allows it to complete the season in this setting.
Picking date, crop, clone, skin contact, whole bunches, and oak then influence
whether a wine feels delicate, firm, or broad. As in [Burgundy](burgundy.md),
these choices produce substantial variation within a region associated with
Pinot Noir.

[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) supplies still and sparkling whites. Lees,
malolactic conversion, and barrel choices influence texture, buttery aromas, and
oak character. [Pinot Gris](../grapes/pinot-gris.md), Riesling, and other
varieties show that the valley's suitability extends beyond its best-known red
grape.

## Establishment and AVA recognition

Modern plantings by growers including David Lett, Charles Coury, and Dick Erath
helped establish the region during the 1960s. The broader AVA was recognized in
1983, with more focused names following as growers described local geographical
differences.[^history]

An AVA identifies grape origin under US rules. It does not prescribe a
Burgundian-style classification, a particular clone, or a required oak regime.
