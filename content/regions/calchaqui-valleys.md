# Calchaquí Valleys

The Calchaquí Valleys form a highland wine-growing landscape across parts of
Salta, Catamarca, and Tucumán in north-western Argentina. Cafayate is a major
wine centre within this wider geography; the regional name also includes
places such as San Carlos, Molinos, and Cachi.

[Torrontés Riojano](../grapes/torrontes-riojano.md) gives the area a distinctive
white-wine association, alongside [Malbec](../grapes/malbec.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and
[Tannat](../grapes/tannat.md). Their wines combine intense sunshine with
mountain temperatures and a landscape where water is essential to cultivation.

## Height, exposure, and water

Dry conditions make irrigation part of vineyard establishment and continuing
management. Rivers, springs, and mountain water sources supply cultivated
areas within the valleys. Gravel and sandy material influence drainage and
the volume of water that the root zone can store.

Colomé's vineyard descriptions give a particularly useful comparison across
heights: La Brava near Cafayate is placed at 1,700 metres, Colomé at 2,300,
El Arenal at 2,600, and Altura Máxima at 3,111.[^heights] These are estate
figures for named vineyards, showing the variation inside a region often
summarized with one elevation.

[Altitude](../concepts/altitude.md) changes temperature and radiation exposure,
while slope and shelter alter the conditions experienced by a particular
vine. Cool nights can help fruit retain
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), but a high vineyard still needs sufficient
warmth and time to ripen its crop. Water storage and distribution also remain
necessary: Colomé describes reservoirs, drip systems, and mountain-fed water
sources across its sites.

## Aromatic whites and varied reds

Cafayate offers a clear setting for aromatic dry Torrontés. At El Porvenir,
Finca El Retiro's overhead *parral* training is part of the producer's approach
to that grape. For Laborum Single Vineyard Torrontés, it describes harvesting
at three different points, pressing whole bunches, fermenting cool, and
retaining lees contact without malolactic fermentation. The choices help
separate aromatic intensity from sweetness and give freshness and texture
their own roles in the wine.

Reds allow a different set of decisions about harvest,
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md), and maturation. A Malbec, a Cabernet-led
blend, and a Tannat can draw on the same broad landscape while having different
structural starting points. Colomé's plantings also include white and red
varieties beyond those most associated with Salta.

Nicolás Orsini's April 2025 regional report describes producers exploring
fresher styles and a wider range of vessels, including concrete and clay.
These experiments sit alongside established wines. They make the Calchaquí
Valleys a contemporary region whose vineyard and cellar decisions continue
to develop.
