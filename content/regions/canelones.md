# Canelones

Canelones is a department immediately north of Montevideo and a central part
of Uruguay's established wine-growing area. Its association with
[Tannat](../grapes/tannat.md) sits alongside white wines and reds made from
other varieties. Gently rolling agricultural land, sedimentary soils, and
the influence of the Río de la Plata help define its setting.

The department belongs to a wider southern wine landscape that also includes
Montevideo and San José. Canelones identifies an administrative area within
that landscape, with different vineyard soils and farming choices inside it.

## Soil, growth, and vineyard work

Uruguay's wine authority INAVI describes generally fertile sedimentary soils
in Canelones. Pizzorno's technical account of a 2024 Cabernet Sauvignon
provides a more specific example: a San Francisco vineyard with silty-clay
ground, calcareous subsoil, and moderate depth. Familia Deicas describes
expansive calcareous clay and gentle slopes at Juanicó.

These profiles give roots different physical environments. Clay can retain
water, while drainage and rooting depth influence how much is accessible to
the vine. Fertility and moisture can support substantial growth, making
[crop load](../concepts/crop-load.md) and
[canopy management](../concepts/canopy-management.md) useful parts of the
regional explanation. Opening a crowded canopy changes light and ventilation
around the fruit; excessive exposure can create a different problem in hot
weather.

## Grapes and wine styles

Tannat gives Canelones a red grape with substantial potential for colour and
tannin. Fruit condition, harvest date, extraction, and blending determine how
that potential becomes wine. A gently extracted version and a more
concentrated, barrel-matured bottling can convey different impressions of the
same region.

[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md) offers another red-wine
route. Pizzorno's 2024 technical description documents concrete-tank
fermentation and six months in oak. The account makes the cellar sequence
visible instead of asking the regional or grape name to explain the whole
wine.

White varieties include [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md),
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md), and
[Viognier](../grapes/viognier.md). Familia Deicas's Juanicó Single Vineyard
white provides a useful process example: whole-bunch pressing, fermentation
in a mixture of new and used barrels, malolactic fermentation, and further
maturation in wood. Its texture can therefore reflect
[pressing](../concepts/pressing.md),
[malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md), and barrel
use as well as the source vineyard.

Comparing Canelones with [Maldonado](maldonado.md) opens another question:
how established southern vineyards and more directly Atlantic-facing sites
handle growth and ripening. Soil and parcel descriptions make that comparison
more useful than assigning one style to each department.
