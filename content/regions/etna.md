# Etna

Etna is a wine region on the slopes of the active volcano north of Catania
in eastern Sicily. Its vineyards combine Mediterranean sunlight with the
cooler conditions of a mountain, and its wines centre on
[Nerello Mascalese](../grapes/nerello-mascalese.md) for reds and
[Carricante](../grapes/carricante.md) for whites.

The mountain's slopes face different directions and stand at different
distances from the sea. Lava flows, ash deposits, erosion, and vineyard work
have produced a patchwork of growing conditions. These variations explain
the importance attached to individual districts, known as *contrade*.

## Growing around a volcano

The Etna DOC production zone forms a band around the mountain's northern,
eastern, and southern slopes, open toward the west. Its boundaries follow
a detailed map of places and elevations. A vineyard on Etna therefore needs
to fall within the specified zone to produce Etna DOC.[^1]

[Altitude](../concepts/altitude.md) generally lowers temperatures, while
exposure changes the amount and timing of sunlight. The sea moderates parts
of the eastern slope.
Rainfall varies around the mountain, and its growing conditions can be
considerably wetter than those of other Sicilian wine districts. For
late-ripening grapes, the timing of autumn rain can matter as much as summer
heat.

Volcanic soils include loose ash and sand as well as stony and gravelly
material. Their depth and texture affect drainage, rooting, and water
availability. Successive eruptions have left deposits of different ages,
while erosion and cultivation have modified them further. The word
*volcanic* consequently covers a range of vineyard environments.

On steep ground, growers maintain lava-stone terraces. Old bush-trained
vines survive alongside more recent vineyards trained on wires. Terraces
limit access for machinery and require continued maintenance, making the
shape of the landscape an immediate influence on vineyard labour.

## Grapes and styles

Nerello Mascalese produces reds that can combine relatively pale colour with
firm [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) and [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Its late ripening makes elevation, crop size, and
the autumn season particularly important. [Nerello Cappuccio](../grapes/nerello-cappuccio.md) is a traditional
partner, contributing a different balance of colour, fruit, and structure.
Extraction and [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) further alter
the firmness and aromatic development of the finished red.

Carricante is central to Etna's whites, especially on the eastern slope.
Its wines commonly emphasize acidity and restrained citrus or floral
character. They can develop greater breadth during maturation, with the
producer's handling of the lees and oxygen helping determine the result.

The official specification consolidated in 2022 requires at least 80%
Nerello Mascalese in Etna Rosso and [Rosato](../styles/rose.md). Etna Bianco requires at least
60% Carricante. Bianco Superiore raises that share to 80% and restricts the
grapes to the delimited area within Milo.[^1]

The denomination also includes white and rosé sparkling wines based on
Nerello Mascalese, made by a second fermentation in bottle. Etna Rosso
Riserva requires at least four years' ageing, including one year in wood.[^1]
These categories give the same regional grapes several different roles.

## Contrade and place

A *contrada* is a named local district. The specification lists and
delimits names eligible to accompany Etna on a wine label.[^1] Such a name
can connect a wine with a particular part of the mountain, but a district
may include several vineyards, elevations, or lava deposits.

Comparing wines from different contrade is most informative when their
producer, vintage, and cellar treatment are also known. A difference in
tannin could reflect extraction; a difference in ripeness could reflect
harvest date. Geographical detail becomes useful alongside those production
details.
