# Muscadet

Muscadet is a family of white-wine appellations in the southern Loire around
Nantes, where the river reaches the Atlantic. Its wines are made primarily, and
in the narrower appellations exclusively, from [Melon de Bourgogne](../grapes/melon-de-bourgogne.md).
The broad regional AOC and three smaller appellations divide the
Nantais vineyard into different geographical and production categories.

## A Loire vineyard near the Atlantic

The vineyard lies in Loire-Atlantique and extends into parts of Maine-et-Loire
and Vendée. The Atlantic, the Loire estuary, the Loire itself, Lake Grandlieu,
and the Goulaine marsh moderate winter cold and summer heat. Maritime air also
travels inland along the river valleys. This is a mild oceanic setting, but
distance from the coast, exposure, slope, elevation, and proximity to water
still change how quickly a parcel dries and ripens.

The geological base is varied: granite, gabbro, gneiss, mica schist, amphibolite,
eclogite, and other old rocks occur across the area, with local sands, clays,
gravels, and alluvial deposits. Many delimited vineyard parcels occupy slopes
with shallow, stony, well-drained soils. Those properties can limit vigour,
warm readily, and restrict available water; the effect depends on soil depth,
fracturing, aspect, and the season.

## One family, four appellations

The regional AOC **Muscadet**, recognised in 1937, covers the largest area. Its
current specification permits Melon B as the principal grape and [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) B
as an accessory up to 10 percent of the farm’s Muscadet vineyard. It is reserved
for dry, still white wine. The rule separating the wine from its fine
fermentation lees by 15 December of the year after harvest helps distinguish
this regional category from the longer lees élevage associated with the
subregional names.[^1]

**Muscadet Sèvre et Maine** and **Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire** were recognised
in 1936. **Muscadet Sèvre et Maine** occupies the core southeast of Nantes, on
slopes above the Loire, Sèvre, Maine, and their tributaries, as well as around
the Goulaine marsh. It is the largest of the three. **Muscadet Coteaux de la
Loire** follows the Loire east of Nantes toward Ancenis and reaches onto slopes
of tributaries such as the Divatte, Èvre, and Erdre. **Muscadet Côtes de
Grandlieu**, recognised on 29 December 1994, lies southwest of Nantes around
Lake Grandlieu and the rivers that feed it, extending toward the Acheneau and
into part of Vendée. The three subregional specifications reserve their wines
for still white wine from Melon B alone.[^2]

Within Sèvre et Maine, seven approved geographical names narrow the origin
further: Château-Thébaud, Clisson, Gorges, Goulaine, Monnières–Saint-Fiacre,
Mouzillon–Tillières, and Le Pallet. These names are not separate regional
appellations. All seven require lees élevage at least until 1 October of the
second year after harvest, followed by bottling in the vinification cellar.[^2]

## What “sur lie” means here

Lees are the fine yeast sediments left after alcoholic fermentation. Keeping a
wine on them without an early transfer can change texture and preserve some
fermentation-derived carbon dioxide; compounds released as yeast cells break
down can contribute to a rounder, more layered impression. “Sur lie” is both an
élevage practice and a regulated label term.

For the three subregional AOCs, the current specifications require the wine to
remain on its fine fermentation lees from the end of alcoholic fermentation at
least until 1 March of the year after harvest and to still be on those lees at
conditioning or first shipment from the vinification cellar. A wine eligible
for the mention “sur lie” may spend no more than one summer in tank or barrel;
it is bottled while still on the lees, which helps retain freshness and a
possible light natural sparkle.[^2]

For ordinary *sur lie* wines, the Sèvre et Maine and Côtes de Grandlieu
specifications require bottling in the geographical or immediate-proximity area
between 1 March after harvest and 30 June of the second year after harvest. The
2026 amendment makes the same change for **Muscadet Coteaux de la Loire**, also
setting 30 June of year N+2 as the deadline and replacing its former
estate-cellar restriction.[^3]
