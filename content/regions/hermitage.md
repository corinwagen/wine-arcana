# Hermitage

Hermitage is a small northern Rhône appellation on the left bank of the river,
centred on a south-facing hill above Tain-l’Hermitage. Its wines come in two
main colours: Syrah-based reds and Marsanne–Roussanne whites. Exposure, slope,
soil depth, and cellar choices change how the grapes
ripen and mature.

## The hill and its sites

The delimited appellation occupies parcels in Tain-l’Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage,
and Larnage. The hill is generally sheltered from northerly winds and receives
substantial southern exposure, but its face is not uniform. The western part
rests on granite overlain in places by mica-schist and gneiss, which weather
into fragile sandy soils. Farther east, older and younger alluvial terraces
bring rounded stones and loess. The official specification describes the
appellation as a small granitic relief with these contrasting geological
formations, and requires growers to maintain the terraces, walls, and
banquettes that help hold its soils.[^1]

These differences affect vine water supply, heat, drainage, and the practical
difficulty of cultivation. Steeper parcels can expose bunches to more sun and
wind while limiting soil depth; gentler eastern parcels can retain more soil
and support a different balance of growth and ripening. Names such as Les
Bessards, Le Méal, Les Greffieux, Les Murets, and Les Dionnières are useful
geographical references.[^2]

## Grapes and wines

Red Hermitage is built around [Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md). The rules require
Syrah to occupy at least 85 percent of an operation's red-wine vineyard
planting. [Marsanne](../grapes/marsanne.md) and
[Roussanne](../grapes/roussanne.md) are accessory grapes. If white grapes are
used, they must be vinified together with Syrah, which must comprise at least
85 percent of that grape assembly.[^1]

Syrah's response to this hill depends on ripening conditions. A warm, exposed
parcel or vintage can give riper fruit and more alcohol; a cooler or less
exposed situation can preserve more [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and make the wine feel firmer and
more savoury. Crop level, harvest date, extraction, and stem use can reinforce
or redirect those tendencies.

White Hermitage is made from Marsanne and Roussanne, either separately or as a
blend.[^1]

## Fermentation and maturation choices

The specification requires manual harvest and whole-grape transport, but it
does not require a single fermentation method or a minimum period of [barrel
ageing](../concepts/oak-maturation.md). It prohibits the use of pieces of wood,
leaving producers to choose
among stainless steel, concrete, older large vessels, and smaller or newer
barrels within the broader limits of the appellation rules.[^1]

## Hermitage and Crozes-Hermitage

Crozes-Hermitage is a separate appellation, not a junior tier within
Hermitage. Its delimited area covers 11 communes around and beyond the
Hermitage hill, including broader terraces, flatter ground, and northern
granite sectors. Its current specification also permits Syrah-based reds and
Marsanne–Roussanne whites, so the grape names alone cannot distinguish the two
appellations.[^3]

Hermitage comes from a tightly
delimited hill across three communes, with parcels whose slopes and exposures
are central to the appellation's identity. Crozes-Hermitage contains a much
larger and more varied area, so its wines can express a wider range of terrain
and production scale.
