# Sopron

Sopron is a western Hungarian wine district adjoining Austria, with vineyards
near Lake Fertő, known across the border as the Neusiedler See. Its central
red grape is [Kékfrankos](../grapes/blaufrankisch.md), the Hungarian name for
Blaufränkisch. The border divides national wine systems while the grape
provides a direct connection with neighbouring Austrian vineyards.

## A varied western landscape

The district extends beyond the immediate surroundings of Sopron, including
vineyards farther south around Kőszeg and Vaskeresztes. Alpine influence, lake
proximity, and exposure vary within this territory. Its soils and underlying
rocks also differ, with mica schist, gneiss, and limestone among the materials
encountered.

Kékfrankos gives the region an identity based on red wines with freshness and
structure. [Zweigelt](../grapes/zweigelt.md) also matters, while cooler sites
support white grapes including
[Grüner Veltliner](../grapes/gruner-veltliner.md). The regional picture becomes
more useful when a particular village or vineyard accompanies the name Sopron.

## A producer's comparison across sites

Weninger's Balf Kékfrankos and its Steiner vineyard bottling illustrate two
scales of origin. The estate describes Steiner as an east-facing site near
the southwestern corner of the lake, with gneiss and mica schist close to the
surface. It also bottles [Furmint](../grapes/furmint.md) from this vineyard.
These wines offer a way to compare different grapes within one place, as
well as Kékfrankos from a single site with a broader blend of parcels.

The same producer's Frettner
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md) comes from a site beside woodland,
which the estate describes as receiving cooling air. This concrete contrast
helps explain why Sopron's contemporary wines extend beyond a single grape
or a uniformly warm lakeside setting.
