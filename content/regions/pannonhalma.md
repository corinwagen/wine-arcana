# Pannonhalma

Pannonhalma is a wine district in northwestern Hungary, in the Sokoró Hills
south of Győr. Its vineyards produce both white and red wines, and the
Benedictine archabbey supplies an important historical and contemporary point
of reference. The region is especially useful for exploring how familiar
Central European grapes behave in a landscape of low ridges and valleys.

## Hills, soils, and grapes

Long ridges divide the growing area into different exposures. Loess and
other sedimentary materials cover much of the underlying geology, with
changes in soil depth and slope affecting individual parcels. The region's
white grapes include [Riesling](../grapes/riesling.md),
[Olaszrizling](../grapes/olaszrizling.md),
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md), and
[Müller-Thurgau](../grapes/muller-thurgau.md). Red varieties include
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md),
[Kékfrankos](../grapes/blaufrankisch.md), and the Bordeaux grapes.

Riesling and Olaszrizling are distinct varieties, despite the similar names.
Their coexistence makes Pannonhalma a useful place to compare aromatic
character and structure without assuming that every wine labeled *rizling*
has the same identity.

## The abbey and modern production

The abbey's connection to viticulture is documented in medieval records.
Its modern winery represents a restoration after disruption: the abbey
reports that postwar confiscation ended its earlier winegrowing operation,
followed much later by repurchasing land, planting vines, and building a new
winery. The modern revival should therefore be understood as a deliberate
reëstablishment of production.

The abbey provides a clear route into the region's history, while the wider
district includes other growers and many grape varieties. Pannonhalma is a
geographical origin, not the name of a single estate or a fixed wine blend.
