# Elgin

Elgin is an elevated wine-growing area east of Cape Town, where orchards,
vineyards, and surrounding mountains share the landscape.
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) and
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) are important contemporary references,
alongside Sauvignon Blanc and other varieties. Its interest comes from the
combination of inland elevation, cool maritime weather, and site selection.

## An elevated basin

The regional growers' association describes a mountain-enclosed basin with
soils derived from Table Mountain sandstone and Bokkeveld shale. Winter
rain supplies water, while cool southeasterly winds and cloud cover can
moderate growing-season conditions. Rain also sometimes arrives during the
season, making grape health and picking weather part of the picture.

Cooler conditions can slow ripening and help retain
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Elevation is one contributor, but the
explanation also involves cloud, exposure, and the movement of air.
A regional name cannot specify the temperature history of every vineyard.

Paul Cluver's De Rust property offers a site example on the edge of the
Groenlandberg. The producer describes vineyards at different elevations on
Bokkeveld shale with underlying clay. Such differences affect rooting and
water availability as well as exposure. Its planting programme began in
1987, illustrating the relatively recent development of this contemporary
wine identity alongside the area's established fruit farming.

## Grapes and planting decisions

Elgin's growers identify [Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md),
[Riesling](../grapes/riesling.md), [Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), and
[Syrah](../grapes/syrah.md) in addition to Chardonnay and Pinot Noir.
These varieties pose different
ripening questions: a site suitable for preserving white-wine acidity need
not offer the same harvest window for a later-ripening red grape.

Plant material adds another level of choice. Paul Cluver's 2022 Estate
Chardonnay technical sheet lists several clones and vineyards planted in
different years. [Clonal selection](../concepts/massal-and-clonal-selection.md)
provides options within a variety; the resulting crop still depends on the
site, season, rootstock, and farming. Comparing wines only by grape name
misses those vineyard decisions.

## From cool-grown fruit to texture

For that Chardonnay, whole-bunch pressing and overnight settling preceded
spontaneous fermentation in French barrels, followed by nine months on lees.
Only part of the wood was new. These choices separate several influences:
pressing controls juice recovery, fermentation vessels shape the environment,
and [lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md) contributes to texture.

An acid-retaining vineyard can therefore supply a wine with substantial
palate breadth. Cool growing conditions do not require a thin wine, and
barrel fermentation does not erase the importance of harvest balance.
Comparing Elgin with [Hemel-en-Aarde](hemel-en-aarde.md) offers another Cape
setting for Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, with different geography and vineyard
sources rather than a simple ranking of which region is cooler or better.
