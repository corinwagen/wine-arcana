# Marlborough

Marlborough is a wine region in New Zealand's South Island, especially
associated with [Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md). Its
contemporary range also includes Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, aromatic whites,
and traditional-method sparkling wine. Differences among valleys and
cellar approaches give the region several useful points of comparison.

## Wairau, Awatere, and the Southern Valleys

New Zealand Winegrowers describes a cool, sunny region with relatively
low rainfall and free-draining soils. Its account distinguishes the Wairau
Valley, Awatere Valley, and Southern Valleys. These names locate fruit
within a broad region whose vineyards receive different combinations of
warmth, wind, and water.

Wairau includes old gravelly river ground, warmer stony sites, and coastal
positions moderated by sea breezes. The Southern Valleys tend towards
heavier soils with more clay. Awatere lies farther south and includes
cooler, windier, and elevated sites. Soil storage and irrigation affect
water supply, while exposure changes the pace and conditions of ripening.

## Sauvignon Blanc and cellar contrasts

Marlborough's Sauvignon identity developed internationally during the
1980s. The grape's aromatic range reflects both fruit chemistry and
fermentation. Canopy light, harvest date, juice handling, and yeast help
determine the balance of herbal and fruity characters.

Dog Point provides a concrete cellar comparison. Its Sauvignon Blanc
account describes fruit from several Wairau vineyards, whole-bunch
pressing, fermentation, and ageing in stainless steel. Section 94 instead
uses one vineyard parcel and older French barrels for fermentation and
eighteen months of maturation.[^dogpoint]

These choices change more than oak aroma. Fermentation vessels, retained
solids, time, and oxygen exposure affect texture and development. The
same regional grape can consequently supply both a fresh tank-made wine
and a broader, more mature expression.

## Pinot Noir and sparkling wine

[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) extend the region beyond Sauvignon.
Site and harvest purpose determine whether those grapes enter still wine
or a sparkling base. Aromatic whites such as Riesling and Pinot Gris add
further choices of sweetness and texture.

Méthode Marlborough is a producer society with its own criteria. Its wines
must be grown, bottled, matured, and disgorged in Marlborough, use
Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, or Meunier, and spend at least eighteen months
on lees before first disgorgement.[^methode] These membership criteria
describe that group's wines, rather than every sparkling wine from
the region.

[Central Otago](central-otago.md) supplies another South Island Pinot
comparison, while [Hawke's Bay](hawkes-bay.md) adds a North Island setting
for Chardonnay, Syrah, and Cabernet-based blends.
