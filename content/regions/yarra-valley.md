# Yarra Valley

Yarra Valley is a Victorian wine region east of Melbourne, with important
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md), [Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and
[Shiraz](../grapes/syrah.md) wines. Still whites and reds coexist with
traditional-method sparkling wine. Differences in terrain and harvest
purpose help explain that breadth.

## Slopes and growing conditions

Wine Australia describes substantial variation in elevation and aspect,
with sandy loams and clay subsoils as well as younger red volcanic soils.
The regional association distinguishes northern grey-brown soils from
more fertile volcanic ground to the south. These profiles affect rooting,
water storage, and the growth a canopy must accommodate.

Cooler ripening can help retain acidity, particularly for early-ripening
Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. A later grape such as Cabernet also needs a
site and season that supply enough warmth. Growers match the variety to
its position within the valley rather than treating regional temperature
as a complete vineyard assessment.

## Revival and contemporary diversity

The regional association traces nineteenth-century winegrowing followed
by a decline and renewed planting from the 1960s. Wantirna, Yarra Yering,
Mount Mary, and other properties belong to that revival.[^history]
Its present account also identifies grapes such as Gamay, Nebbiolo,
Sangiovese, and Savagnin, extending the region's established range.

Chardonnay and Pinot Noir can supply both still wines and sparkling base
wines. Earlier picking for sparkling production generally prioritizes
retained acidity and lower potential alcohol. Subsequent
[bottle fermentation](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md)
adds another production sequence, while still wines develop through their
own fermentation and maturation choices.

## A Cabernet blend in the cellar

Yarra Yering's Dry Red Wine No. 1 gives a concrete red-wine example. Its
2023 blend combines Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, Malbec, and Petit Verdot.
The producer describes different handling for the components: Cabernet
was crushed, while the other varieties underwent whole-berry fermentation.
Some Cabernet also received extended skin contact in a large oak vessel.

After fermentation, the wine matured in French barriques, using both new
and previously used wood. Skin handling and maturation establish separate
influences on fragrance and structure. A blend also distributes ripening
and texture among several grapes.

[Barossa Valley](barossa-valley.md) offers a warmer Shiraz comparison,
while [Tasmania](tasmania.md) provides another setting for Chardonnay,
Pinot Noir, and sparkling wine. These comparisons connect wine style to
vineyard conditions and producer decisions across southern Australia.
