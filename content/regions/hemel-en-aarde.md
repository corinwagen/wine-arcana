# Hemel-en-Aarde

Hemel-en-Aarde is a group of three wine-growing wards behind Hermanus,
within [Walker Bay](walker-bay.md). Its contemporary identity centres on
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) and
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md). Maritime exposure and differences
among vineyard sites help explain why those grapes produce a range of wines
rather than a single regional expression.

## Three distinct wards

Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, Upper Hemel-en-Aarde Valley, and Hemel-en-Aarde Ridge
are separate wards. The broad name Hemel-en-Aarde can describe all three;
the Valley ward is only one part of that geography.

The growers' association describes Valley as the first ward inland from
the coast, with clay-rich Bokkeveld shale prominent alongside other ground.
Upper includes lighter soils derived from granite and sandstone, while
Ridge combines higher ground with varied aspects and shale- and
sandstone-derived soils. Its account generally places harvest later as
one moves towards Upper and Ridge, though site and season remain important.

Ocean exposure and southeasterly summer wind can moderate growing conditions.
Soil depth, clay, drainage, and the direction of a slope then alter the
resources and light available to a vine. A ward supplies useful origin
information without replacing a description of the particular vineyard.

## Comparing Pinot Noir sites

Storm Wines provides a practical comparison through Pinot Noir from all
three wards. It describes Vrede in Valley on stony, clay-rich Bokkeveld
shale; Ignis in Upper on decomposed granite; and Ridge on stony, clay-rich
Bokkeveld shale. The same rock formation can therefore occur in more than
one ward, and a ward should not be reduced to one soil type.

These vineyard wines offer a way to explore site while keeping the producer
and grape relatively consistent. Differences in harvest, vine material,
crop, and cellar treatment still need consideration. A tasting comparison
can suggest patterns, but cannot isolate the effect of geology by itself.

## Chardonnay and vintage conditions

Hamilton Russell Vineyards offers another documented approach. Its 2024
Chardonnay was barrel-fermented and matured for nine months in French oak,
using both new and previously used barrels. Such decisions build texture
alongside the balance supplied by the harvested grapes.

The producer's vintage account also records spring storm damage and rain
pressure before that harvest. Wind during flowering contributed to reduced
crop, while disease concerns required attention.[^2024] This illustrates
why [wind](../concepts/wind-and-wine-growing.md) and a coastal climate can
bring both useful moderation and practical hazards.

[Elgin](elgin.md) supplies a neighbouring Cape comparison for the same two
principal grapes. For either area, acidity, ripeness, fermentation, and
maturation help explain the wine more precisely than a claim that cool
conditions automatically produce one style.

[^2024]: Hamilton Russell Vineyards, *Chardonnay 2024*, producer account
    of the preceding growing season and its effect on the crop.
