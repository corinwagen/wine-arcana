# Alexander Valley

Alexander Valley is a wine-growing area in northeastern
[Sonoma County](sonoma-county.md), California, following the Russian River
between the Healdsburg and Cloverdale areas. It is especially associated with
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md). Its warmer inland
position gives that late-ripening grape a substantial growing season, while
marine air and nighttime cooling moderate the heat.

The appellation includes valley-floor vineyards, benches, and hillsides along
the Mayacamas. Those positions give Cabernet different combinations of soil
depth, drainage, exposure, and available water. A regional name narrows the
geography without prescribing one texture or level of ripeness.

## Inland warmth and different sites

Alexander Valley belongs to Sonoma's warmer inland wine country, adjoining
districts including [Dry Creek Valley](dry-creek-valley.md) and Knights Valley.
Morning marine layers and cooler nights help distinguish its growing conditions
from districts with less access to coastal air.

The terrain matters within that general climate. Weathered sedimentary and
volcanic materials, erosion, and river deposits have produced varied soils.
Rockier hillside vineyards can have shallower root zones than valley-floor
sites, changing both drainage and the water available during summer. Growers
therefore adjust irrigation, crop level, and canopy work to the block.

## Cabernet's modern development

The present Cabernet association developed through planting and winemaking
decisions during the twentieth century. Robert Young planted Cabernet on his
family property in 1963. Rodney Strong's 1974 Alexander's Crown Cabernet
provided an early Sonoma example of a vineyard-designated wine. These are
useful historical reference points for a district whose earlier agriculture
also included other crops.

Contemporary producers offer different ways to understand Cabernet's place
here. Alexander Valley Vineyards and Silver Oak have sustained regional
programs, with their published methods illustrating differences in blending
and maturation.

## Blending and oak

Alexander Valley Vineyards' 2022 Cabernet Sauvignon contained 95% Cabernet,
with smaller amounts of [Malbec](../grapes/malbec.md),
[Petit Verdot](../grapes/petit-verdot.md), [Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), and
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md). Its technical sheet describes
14 months in French and American oak, with a quarter of the barrels new.
The companion varieties and wood treatment are part of the finished wine's
structure, even though Cabernet supplies its name and most of the fruit.

Silver Oak's 2021 Alexander Valley Cabernet used a different sequence: the
components were blended before 24 months in American oak. This makes a useful
comparison with assembling a blend after its components have matured
separately. Blending earlier commits the cellar to a combined wine before
the wood has contributed its full effect.

[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) depends on barrel age, size,
wood, and duration as well as the fruit. French versus American oak is one
choice within that larger set. Neither a regional Cabernet label nor a
maturation period alone establishes how much wood flavour or astringency a
wine will show.
