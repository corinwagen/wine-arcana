# Neuquén

Neuquén is a northern Patagonian wine province in Argentina. San Patricio del
Chañar is an important modern production centre, alongside vineyards in other
parts of the province. Its wines give [Malbec](../grapes/malbec.md) and
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) a setting shaped by dry conditions, river
water, and long summer days, rather than the high Andean vineyards commonly
associated with Argentine wine.

## An irrigated Patagonian landscape

Development at San Patricio del Chañar accelerated around the end of the
twentieth century. A 2021 Wines of Argentina account connects that expansion
with irrigation infrastructure drawing on the Neuquén River. Establishing
water delivery made commercial vineyards possible in an arid landscape.
The relationship remains useful for understanding the region: rain over the
vines and the water available to their roots are different quantities.

The provincial tourism authority also identifies growing areas at Añelo,
Senillosa, and Chos Malal. San Patricio del Chañar is consequently a major
part of the wine story, rather than a synonym for the whole province.
Vineyards near the Limay River supply another geographical context.

At Chañar, low humidity, wind, and substantial differences between day and
night temperatures affect growing conditions. Wines of Argentina describes
sandy-loam ground alongside parcels with more stones and calcareous material.
Those profiles change water storage and rooting. Growers must match
[irrigation](../concepts/irrigation-and-vine-water-stress.md) to the soil and
season, while canopy and harvest decisions determine how ripe fruit retains
freshness.

## Grapes and contrasting wines

Malbec has a substantial role in Chañar, with Pinot Noir giving the region a
different structural starting point. The regional accounts also identify
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and white varieties
including [Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) and
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md). A Patagonian origin therefore
encompasses red blends, varietal reds, and whites with separate harvest and
cellar objectives.

Familia Schroeder supplies a documented Pinot Noir example. Its Saurus Barrel
Fermented 2018 began with cold maceration, followed by fermentation on the
skins in tank. During fermentation, the producer separated the juice from
the solids and transferred it to oak barrels to finish fermenting. Malolactic
fermentation and subsequent maturation also took place in wood.[^method]
The sequence separates initial colour and tannin extraction from the later
barrel stage; “barrel fermented” need not mean that intact red grapes remained
in a barrel for the whole process.

Malma and Bodega del Fin del Mundo provide further regional reference points
in Wines of Argentina's account, which describes their Pinot Noir, Malbec, and
blended reds. The provincial authority's current listings confirm their place
in Chañar's winery landscape. These examples show the range of wines that
developed alongside the newer vineyard infrastructure.

Neighbouring [Río Negro](rio-negro.md) offers a comparison through older
vineyards and its own river-valley sites. The two provinces share a northern
Patagonian setting while retaining different vineyard histories and local
growing conditions.

[^method]: Familia Schroeder, [*Saurus Barrel Fermented Pinot Noir 2018
    technical sheet*](https://www.familiaschroeder.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Ficha-ESP-SAURUS-BARREL-FERMENTED-PINOT-NOIR-2018-3.pdf),
    maceration, fermentation, and maturation sequence.
