# Brunello di Montalcino

Brunello di Montalcino is a red wine made entirely from
[Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) in the historic territory of Montalcino,
south of Siena in Tuscany. Brunello is the local name for the grape. The wine
combines that varietal identity with a long maturation period, including time
in oak and bottle before release.[^1]

Within those requirements, wines vary with the hillside, vintage, extraction,
and choice of ageing vessel. Sangiovese can retain marked [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md)
at full ripeness, giving producers substantial material to work with over
that extended period.

## The Montalcino hill

Montalcino stands between the Ombrone, Asso, and Orcia valleys. Its vineyards
occupy slopes with different elevations and directions of exposure. The
climate is broadly Mediterranean, with much of the rain falling outside
summer. The position between the coast and the inland mountains adds
continental influences.

The consortium describes a progression from relatively loose soils at lower
elevations to stonier soils derived from marl and limestone higher on the
hill. Local differences are considerable. Soil depth and the proportion of
fine material influence the water available to the vines through the dry
season, while slope and exposure affect light and heat.

These variations matter for a grape with a long ripening cycle. A cooler,
higher vineyard can develop differently from a sheltered, warmer slope.
Dry years increase the importance of water supply; cool or wet harvests place
more emphasis on achieving ripe skins and healthy fruit. Describing a Brunello
through its vineyard and season is consequently more informative than
assigning one character to the whole hill.

The appellation uses Montalcino's historical municipal boundaries, fixed in
the specification by reference to 30 November 2011. Later changes to the
administrative commune do not automatically extend the wine's production
zone.[^1]

## Time in the cellar

Brunello requires at least two years in oak. The rules allow oak containers
of any size, so the legal minimum accommodates both large casks and smaller
barrels. Ordinary Brunello also needs at least four months in bottle before
release; Riserva needs six.[^1]

Vessel size and age help explain stylistic differences. Small new barrels
expose more wine to fresh wood, making their aromatic and tannic contribution
more apparent. Large or well-used casks usually make a quieter contribution.
The wine's extraction, oxygen exposure, and time in each vessel interact
with those choices. [Bottle ageing](../concepts/bottle-aging.md) continues the
development after the oak stage.

Ordinary Brunello may reach the market from 1 January of the fifth year
after harvest, and Riserva from the sixth. Thus the earliest release dates
for the 2021 vintage are January 2026 and January 2027 respectively.[^1]

Rosso di Montalcino provides another outlet for the same grape and territory,
with a much shorter production timetable. A producer may choose fruit or
wine for Rosso according to the crop, vintage, and intended style. Its
existence gives estates flexibility while their Brunello remains in the
cellar.

## How the style developed

The modern Brunello tradition took shape during nineteenth-century work on
Sangiovese and wines intended for keeping. Clemente Santi was an early figure
in that development. Wider commercial expansion followed in the later
twentieth century, alongside DOC recognition in 1966 and DOCG recognition
in 1980. The long cellar cycle remains central to the denomination's
organization and to the producer's decisions about individual lots.
