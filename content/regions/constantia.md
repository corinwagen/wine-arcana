# Constantia

Constantia is a wine-growing area on the Cape Peninsula, combining
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md), dry white blends, red wines,
and the sweet Muscat wine Vin de Constance. Coastal exposure and mountain
slopes help explain its range, while individual vineyards receive different
amounts of warmth, shade, and wind.

## Mountains and maritime exposure

Wines of South Africa describes vineyards climbing towards the mountains
on the area's western side, with views towards False Bay. The familiar
description of a Constantia valley can obscure those differences in slope
and exposure. Sites suited to white grapes coexist with warmer positions
used for red varieties.

Klein Constantia's farming account makes the variation more concrete. Its
vineyards occupy south- and east-facing slopes, with higher ground receiving
afternoon mountain shade. Sandstone and decomposed granite occur in different
positions, with clay affecting water retention. Ocean breezes arrive from
different directions; shelter and damaging gusts vary by block. This is a
useful setting for understanding [wind](../concepts/wind-and-wine-growing.md)
as a farming influence rather than a guarantee of a particular wine flavour.

## Sauvignon Blanc and red wines

Sauvignon Blanc is a major regional reference, often joined by
[Sémillon](../grapes/semillon.md). The proportions, harvest decisions, and
use of wood can alter both aroma and texture. A brisk Sauvignon-led wine
and a broader white blend can share an origin without having the same
structure or maturation potential.

Red wines show another side of the area. Wines of South Africa identifies
warmer exposures and describes examples including Eagles' Nest Shiraz and
Constantia Glen's Bordeaux-variety blends. Cabernet, Merlot, and other
components can distribute fruit, tannin, and ripening demands across a blend.
The name Constantia therefore should not be read as a synonym for white wine.

## Vin de Constance

Klein Constantia's Vin de Constance uses Muscat de Frontignan and selective
harvesting to assemble a concentrated sweet wine. Its 2022 technical sheet
records eighteen picking passes, with batches handled separately. Most
received skin contact before pressing; fermentation stopped naturally,
leaving substantial [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md).[^2022]

That method connects sweetness to fruit selection and fermentation. Muscat's
grape-derived perfume remains part of the wine, while sugar, acidity, and
alcohol determine how the concentration feels. Subsequent maturation in
barrels and larger wooden vessels adds another stage of development.

The same sheet describes a warm, dry season and a compressed harvest affected
by wind and heat. It is a reminder that a coastal location still requires
vintage-specific decisions. Constantia's dry and sweet wines are most useful
to compare through their grape sources and production methods, rather than
through an assumed single regional taste.

[^2022]: Klein Constantia, *Vin de Constance 2022*, harvest and fermentation
    account; the number of passes describes this vintage.
