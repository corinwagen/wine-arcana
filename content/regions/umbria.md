# Umbria

Umbria is an inland region of central Italy, between Tuscany, Lazio, and the
Marche. Its wine country includes the hills around Perugia, Lake Trasimeno,
Montefalco, Spoleto, and Orvieto. Those places support quite different wines:
white blends, Sangiovese-based reds, tannic Sagrantino, sweet passito, and
increasingly varied interpretations of local white grapes.

The most useful introduction follows three districts: [Montefalco](montefalco.md)
for the relationship between grape structure and maturation,
[Orvieto](orvieto.md) for white blends and selective harvests, and
[Trasimeno](trasimeno.md) for the local expression of Grenache. Together they
show why an Umbrian label needs more explanation than a single regional style.

## A landscape of hills and distinct districts

The Apennines frame Umbria's eastern side, while much of its wine comes from
lower hills. Vineyard exposure, elevation, and access to water vary within
short distances. Around Trasimeno, the lake forms part of the growing
landscape; farther south, Orvieto's vineyards extend across the administrative
border into Lazio. Wine boundaries and regional boundaries therefore need
separate attention.

The region also resists a single soil description. Montefalco includes clay,
sand, and older sedimentary formations. Orvieto combines volcanic material with
marine sediments and other deposits. For growers, the practical questions are
how deeply roots can explore, how water moves and remains available, and how
quickly a particular slope ripens fruit.

## Red wines: blending, extraction, and time

[Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) connects Umbria to the wider wine culture
of central Italy. In Montefalco Rosso it forms the base of a blend that also
includes [Sagrantino](../grapes/sagrantino.md). The separate Montefalco
Sagrantino wine concentrates attention on the latter grape's substantial
[tannin](../concepts/tannin.md), with both dry and sweet versions.

That distinction makes Montefalco a useful place to learn about
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md): fermenting red grapes with their skins
extracts structure, but a grape already rich in tannin requires careful choices
about contact time and handling. Subsequent maturation changes how that
structure feels; a long ageing requirement cannot guarantee that every young
wine will be supple.

[Torgiano](torgiano.md) offers another Sangiovese reference.
Lungarotti's Rubesco62, a current
Rosso di Torgiano bottling, uses Sangiovese alone, ferments in steel, and spends
less than a year in barrel. It provides a concrete contrast with the longer
maturation associated with dry Sagrantino.

Near Trasimeno, the grape called *Gamay del Trasimeno* belongs to
[Grenache](../grapes/grenache.md), distinct from the Gamay of Beaujolais.
Local producers use it for both red wine and rosato. Earlier picking and short
skin contact can make a very different wine from a later-picked red fermented
with skins and matured for longer.

Farther south, [Narni](narni.md) and San Gemini give
[Ciliegiolo](../grapes/ciliegiolo.md) a distinct varietal role. Leonardo
Bussoletti's Brecciaro combines fruit from rocky and clay-limestone sites,
with most of the wine matured in steel and a smaller part in large oak.
This adds another grape and cellar approach to the region's red wines.

## White wines: several routes to texture

[Grechetto di Orvieto](../grapes/grechetto-di-orvieto.md) participates in the
white blends of Orvieto and also appears in varietal wines.

[Trebbiano Spoletino](../grapes/trebbiano-spoletino.md) gives the Montefalco–Spoleto
area a different white-grape focus. Antonelli's range illustrates its current
breadth: a pressed white fermented in large oak, a prolonged skin-contact wine,
and a traditional-method sparkling wine. Comparing these approaches helps
separate the contribution of the grape from those of extraction,
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md), and the second fermentation.

International varieties also contribute to Umbrian wine. At Castello della
Sala near Orvieto, Antinori's Cervaro della Sala combines
[Chardonnay](../grapes/chardonnay.md) with
Grechetto. In the 2024 wine, Chardonnay underwent barrel fermentation and
partial [malolactic fermentation](../concepts/malolactic-fermentation.md),
while the Grechetto component was fermented separately without oak. The blend
therefore combines components whose cellar treatment differs as well as their
varietal character.

## Sweet wine by two different paths

Montefalco Sagrantino passito uses grapes dried after harvest. Orvieto also has
sweet wines made with selectively picked grapes affected by noble rot, the
beneficial form of *Botrytis cinerea* under suitable conditions. Both can
concentrate grape sugars, but drying harvested bunches and managing a fungus
in the vineyard create different practical demands and flavours.
