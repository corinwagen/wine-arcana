# Jujuy

Jujuy is Argentina's northernmost province and a growing wine region with two
markedly different vineyard settings. The Quebrada de Humahuaca has dry,
high-elevation mountain vineyards; the Valles Templados farther south have
more rain, deeper soils, and lower elevations. Both belong in an account of
Jujuy's wines.

## The Quebrada de Humahuaca

The Quebrada is a narrow mountain corridor where small vineyards occupy
different slopes, exposures, and sheltered sites. Towns such as Tumbaya,
Maimará, Tilcara, and Huacalera provide useful geographical reference points.
The terrain makes much of the work manual, while dry conditions place water
supply among the basic constraints on planting.

[Altitude](../concepts/altitude.md) combines strong sunlight with cooler
conditions, including cold nights. This can help retain
[acidity](../concepts/acidity.md), but does not remove the risk of a damaging
frost before harvest. The relation between sun exposure, fruit protection,
and the time needed to ripen a crop is especially important in these sites.

Alejandro Iglesias's 2021 regional account places Fernando Dupont's early
Quebrada project at Maimará in 2003. It also records producers experimenting
with red blends and white varieties, including Sauvignon Blanc. That history
helps explain why the region's wine identity continues to develop around
small sites and several grapes, rather than one established formula.

## The Valles Templados

The southern valleys around Monterrico, El Carmen, and San Antonio offer a
different landscape. Nicolás Orsini's December 2025 report describes greater
rainfall, more fertile soils, and a longer agricultural association with
tobacco. These conditions change the questions facing growers. Managing
vigour and ventilation can become more consequential where soil moisture
supports a larger canopy.

The report identifies [Tannat](../grapes/tannat.md),
[Malbec](../grapes/malbec.md), and criolla grapes among the varieties grown
there. A bottle from this part of Jujuy therefore need not share the growing
conditions of a mountain vineyard in the Quebrada.

## Grapes and evolving styles

Malbec is also important in the Quebrada, with
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md) and
[Sauvignon Blanc](../grapes/sauvignon-blanc.md) providing contemporary
comparisons. Ripeness, healthy fruit, and extraction determine how much of the
mountain-grown grape's structure reaches the bottle. Elevation cannot supply
a fixed prediction of colour, alcohol, or tannin.

Orsini describes a current move among Jujuy producers toward less dominant
wood and fresher styles. This is an account of changing choices, not a rule
for every local wine. It makes Jujuy useful for understanding how an emerging
region can develop its cellar practices while learning more about individual
vineyards.

The [Calchaquí Valleys](calchaqui-valleys.md) offer a neighbouring comparison
for arid mountain viticulture. Keeping Jujuy's two landscapes distinct gives
the reader a better guide than treating all north-western Argentine wine as
one high-altitude style.
