# Narni

Narni gives its name to a wine area in southern [Umbria](umbria.md), near the
border with Lazio. Its contemporary identity centres on
[Ciliegiolo](../grapes/ciliegiolo.md), made as a varietal red and rosato. These
wines provide a useful counterpart to the Sangiovese blends and Sagrantino of
[Montefalco](montefalco.md), farther north.

The geographical indication is **Narni IGT**, or *Indicazione Geografica
Tipica*. It covers a wider area than the town or commune of Narni and permits
white, red, and rosato wines, including some dried-grape and lightly sparkling
styles. A bottle labelled Ciliegiolo Narni identifies both a grape and a place;
the geographical name alone does not specify Ciliegiolo.[^rules]

## Hills, soils, and growing conditions

The production area includes all of Attigliano, Giove, and Penna in Teverina,
and parts of several neighbouring communes, including Narni, San Gemini,
Amelia, and Alviano. Hillside vineyards encounter marine clays, limestone and
travertine, volcanic material, and river deposits. Summer rainfall is generally
limited, making soil depth and water storage important differences between
sites.

Leonardo Bussoletti's vineyards at Colle Ozio and Colle Pizzuto in San Gemini
illustrate one local setting: clay-limestone soils on hills between roughly
230 and 290 metres. The producer uses Guyot training and harvests the two
vineyards for its introductory Ciliegiolo.

## Ciliegiolo and the local revival

The recent development of varietal Ciliegiolo has given growers a common
regional focus. The Associazione Produttori Ciliegiolo di Narni formed in 2014;
Jacopo Manni's 2022 account documents producers presenting both younger wines
and selections with longer maturation. That range matters because
Ciliegiolo's reputation for supple, fruit-led wine can obscure the differences
created by harvest decisions, extraction, and time in the cellar.

Bussoletti is a useful reference for this revival: *Gambero Rosso* identifies
his work with Ciliegiolo, Grechetto, and Trebbiano as part of the renewal of
Narni viticulture. His introductory Ciliegiolo ferments spontaneously and then
spends about six months on its fine lees in stainless steel. Keeping it out
of wood lets the comparison focus on fruit, fermentation, and texture from
[lees ageing](../concepts/lees-aging.md).

## Red wine, rosato, and passito

Cantina Giovannini provides a documented contrast within one producer's
range. Its Ciliegiolo red ferments with the skins and undergoes further
[maceration](../concepts/maceration.md) before maturation in steel. Summa
rosato begins with whole-bunch pressing to limit colour extraction, followed
by fermentation and ageing on fine lees. The difference starts at the press:
separating juice from skins early removes much of the opportunity to extract
the colour and tannin of a red wine.

The estate also makes a Ciliegiolo passito, drying grapes indoors for three
months before fermentation and subsequent oak maturation. This is a useful
local example of [appassimento](../concepts/appassimento.md), which
concentrates the harvested fruit through water loss.

[^rules]: *Narni IGT production specification*, consolidated through the
    7 March 2014 amendment, articles 1–3. A named single variety requires at
    least 85% of that variety in the prescribed vineyard composition.
