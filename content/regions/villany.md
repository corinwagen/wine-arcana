# Villány

Villány is a wine district in southern Hungary, close to the Croatian border.
It is an important contemporary home for
[Cabernet Franc](../grapes/cabernet-franc.md), alongside
[Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md),
[Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md), and the longer-established
Portugieser. Its reds range from wines intended for early drinking to structured
bottlings shaped by prolonged [oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md).

## Warm slopes, varied exposure

The Villány hills shelter many vineyards from northerly air. Limestone and
dolomite underlie soils with variable coverings of loess and clay. Warm,
sun-facing slopes give late-ripening red varieties opportunities to mature,
but exposure changes substantially around the hills. Cooler sites also support
[Pinot Noir](../grapes/pinot-noir.md) and
[Kékfrankos](../grapes/blaufrankisch.md).

The district includes Siklós to the west, where white wines remain part of the
picture. A bottle's vineyard and producer therefore convey more than the broad
regional association with powerful reds.

## Cabernet Franc and a broader identity

The expansion of private estates after the end of communism helped establish
Villány's modern reputation. Cabernet Franc subsequently became the focus of
the collective Villányi Franc identity. Meanwhile, the Portugieser-based RedY
initiative gives a more youthful, fruit-centred expression a shared regional
name.

Vylyan's account of its 2016 Villányi Franc shows one realization of the
Cabernet Franc style: grapes from the south-facing Mandolás vineyard,
fermentation on skins, and maturation in Hungarian oak barrels of different
ages. Its 2013 Pinot Noir came from the differently exposed Gombás and Várerdő
vineyards and included some whole bunches in fermentation. These documented
wines show how growers combine site selection and cellar choices to work with
several red grapes within the district.
