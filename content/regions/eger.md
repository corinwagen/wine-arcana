# Eger

Eger is a wine district in northeastern Hungary at the foot of the Bükk
Mountains. Its two principal blending identities are Egri Bikavér, a red wine,
and Egri Csillag, a white. Together they express a regional approach in which
several grapes contribute to a wine identified primarily with its place.

## Climate and landscape

Eger has comparatively cool conditions within Hungary, while sheltered,
south-facing slopes provide warmth for red grapes. Rhyolite tuff, a rock
formed from volcanic ash, is widespread beneath the vineyards. Clay-rich soils
and sites with limestone and other geological materials add variation.

This combination supports both white wines with conspicuous freshness and
reds that can retain lively [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md). Exposure and
ripeness still matter: the district's northerly position alone cannot explain
every wine.

## Two blending traditions

[Kékfrankos](../grapes/blaufrankisch.md) is an important component of Egri
Bikavér, alongside other red varieties. The familiar translation “Bull's
Blood” is associated with a story about the defenders of Eger's castle.
The regional wine council treats the siege story as legend and distinguishes
it from nineteenth-century documentary evidence for the wine name.[^1]

Egri Csillag gives white blends a parallel regional identity. The council
dates its formal creation to 2010, drawing on an older practice of blending
white varieties. Styles range from fresh, youthful wines to fuller versions
whose maturation contributes additional texture and wood-derived spice.

The distinction between place and blend also matters beyond Eger:
[Szekszárd](szekszard.md) has its own Bikavér tradition. Comparing the two
regions is a useful way to explore how a shared idea changes with grapes,
climate, and producer decisions.

[^1]: Eger Wine Region Council, “Eger Wine Region,” history section. The council
    identifies an 1851 document while explicitly leaving the wine's precise
    origin unresolved.
