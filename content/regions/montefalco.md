# Montefalco

Montefalco is a wine district in central [Umbria](umbria.md), around the hill
town of the same name. Its defining red grape is
[Sagrantino](../grapes/sagrantino.md), used both alone and alongside
[Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md). The distinction between Montefalco
Sagrantino and Montefalco Rosso is fundamental: the first is a varietal wine;
the second is a Sangiovese-led blend.

White wine is another substantial part of the district's identity.
[Trebbiano Spoletino](../grapes/trebbiano-spoletino.md) connects Montefalco with
nearby Spoleto and provides material for white blends, varietal wines, and
experiments in skin contact and sparkling production.

## The growing landscape

The appellations cover Montefalco and parts of Bevagna, Gualdo Cattaneo,
Castel Ritaldi, and Giano dell'Umbria. Vineyards occupy hills with varied
aspects and soils, including clay, sand, and sedimentary deposits. These
variations affect water supply and ripening. Late-ripening Sagrantino also
makes the weather near harvest
particularly consequential.

## Reading the red-wine names

Under the specifications incorporating the 2023 amendments, Montefalco
Sagrantino DOCG uses Sagrantino alone, in dry *secco* and sweet *passito*
versions. Montefalco Rosso DOC requires 60–80% Sangiovese and 10–25%
Sagrantino, with the balance from permitted red varieties.[^rules]

Blending changes the starting material for the cellar. Sagrantino contributes
considerable tannic structure even as a minority component. Rosso allows a
producer to balance that contribution against Sangiovese and other grapes;
a varietal Sagrantino places more emphasis on managing the grape's own
structure through extraction and maturation. Neither name specifies a single
level of ripeness or a uniform amount of new oak.

Dry Sagrantino must undergo 33 months of maturation counted from 1 December
of the harvest year, including at least 12 months in oak, followed by at least
four months in bottle. Passito has the same 33-month period and subsequent
bottle minimum, without the dry wine's oak requirement.[^rules]

These rules leave substantial freedom. Antonelli, for example, describes
25–40 days of skin contact for its dry Sagrantino, followed by maturation in
large oak and then glass-lined cement. Long contact with skins and seeds
makes extraction management important; the subsequent vessels offer time for
the wine to develop without requiring a strong new-barrel flavour.

## Passito: sweetness with structure

Sagrantino passito starts with selected bunches dried after harvest, a process
called [appassimento](../concepts/appassimento.md). Losing water concentrates
the remaining juice. Fermentation with skins then brings tannin into a wine
that retains [residual sugar](../concepts/residual-sugar.md), giving sweetness
and astringency a place in the same mouthful.

Scacciadiavoli supplies a concrete example: its published method dries grapes
on racks from the harvest period into December, ferments in steel, and matures
the wine in barriques. That is a producer's approach, not a requirement that
all Montefalco passito spend time in small barrels. Drying conditions and the
health of the selected bunches matter before the wine ever enters the cellar.

## White wines and the neighbouring Spoleto tradition

Montefalco Bianco DOC requires at least half Trebbiano Spoletino under the
2023 specification. Montefalco Grechetto requires at least 85% Grechetto.[^rules]
Those names should be distinguished from varietal Trebbiano Spoletino wines
labelled under the neighbouring Spoleto denomination.

Antonelli's Trebium uses large-oak fermentation and time on the lees; its
Vigna Tonda explores prolonged contact with skins. The producer also makes a
[traditional-method sparkling
wine](../styles/traditional-method-sparkling-wine.md) from Trebbiano
Spoletino. These contrasting uses make the Montefalco–Spoleto area useful for
understanding how pressing, skin contact, and maturation reshape the same
grape.
