# Chianti Classico

Chianti Classico is a red-wine denomination in the hills between Florence and
Siena in Tuscany. [Sangiovese](../grapes/sangiovese.md) supplies its foundation,
bringing [acidity](../concepts/acidity.md) and [tannin](../concepts/tannin.md) to wines whose weight and ripeness vary across
the region's elevations, exposures, and soils.

Chianti Classico and Chianti are separate denominations with distinct
production zones and rules. The word *Classico* identifies the historic
central territory, whose wines carry the black-rooster emblem. It is useful
to keep that geographical distinction separate from the maturation categories
found on a Chianti Classico label.

## A landscape of differing slopes

Vineyards occupy a hilly landscape interspersed with woods, olive groves, and
villages. The production area includes Castellina, Gaiole, Greve, and Radda in
Chianti, together with parts of several neighbouring communes. Its many
aspects expose vines to different amounts and times of sunlight.

Elevation and exposure influence the pace of Sangiovese's long ripening
season. Cooler sites can preserve acidity while requiring more time to ripen
the skins and seeds. Warmer slopes can accumulate sugar more readily, with
harvest decisions determining the balance between alcohol, fruit, and
tannin. Vintage weather can change the advantages of a particular site.

The geological vocabulary includes *alberese*, a limestone-rich material,
and *galestro*, a flaky, clay-rich rock. Sandstone, sand, and other deposits
also occur. Their practical importance lies in differences in soil depth,
rooting conditions, drainage, and water supply. A commune can contain several
such environments, so its name gives a starting point for understanding a
wine's origin.

## Grapes and cellar choices

The specification consolidated on 22 June 2023 requires ordinary Chianti
Classico and Riserva to contain 80–100% Sangiovese. The remainder may come
from authorized red varieties. This permits both pure Sangiovese and blends
with grapes such as Canaiolo, Colorino, [Merlot](../grapes/merlot.md), or [Cabernet Sauvignon](../grapes/cabernet-sauvignon.md).[^1]

The proportions can change the wine's colour, tannin, and fruit profile.
Extraction during fermentation then determines how much material passes
from the grape skins and seeds into the wine.
[Oak maturation](../concepts/oak-maturation.md) offers another source of
variation. Large, established casks generally contribute less fresh wood
aroma per litre than small new barrels. Producers can combine vessels and
maturation periods according to the wine they want to make.

## Maturation categories and place names

Ordinary Chianti Classico may be released from 1 October following the
harvest. Riserva requires at least 24 months' ageing, including three months
in bottle. Gran Selezione requires 30 months, also including three in bottle,
and grapes from vineyards farmed by the bottling estate. The ageing clock
for Riserva and Gran Selezione begins on 1 January after harvest.[^1]

The 2023 revision also established additional geographical units, or UGAs,
for Gran Selezione. Names such as Radda, Panzano, and Lamole locate the fruit
more closely within the denomination. These are areas of origin, each capable
of containing more than one vineyard and wine style.

A further change raises Gran Selezione's minimum Sangiovese proportion to
90% and limits the remainder to specified local red varieties. It takes
effect with the 2027 harvest. Earlier vintages can therefore legitimately
follow the previous blending rules.[^1]

[^1]: Italian Ministry of Agriculture, Chianti Classico DOCG production
    specification, consolidated 22 June 2023, articles 2, 5, and 7,
    accessed 9 September 2026. Article 2 provides a five-harvest transition
    for the new Gran Selezione blend.
